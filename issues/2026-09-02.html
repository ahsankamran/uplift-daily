Nature · Altyn Dala, Kazakhstan

A wild horse foal was born on Kazakhstan's steppe for the first time in two centuries.

A male Przewalski's horse foal was born at the Alibi Reintroduction Centre in Kazakhstan's Altyn Dala State Nature Reserve to Tessa, a mare who arrived from Berlin Zoo in 2024 as part of an international restoration effort. Project coordinator Albert Salemgareyev called it "the first new generation of Przewalski's horses born in the steppes of central Kazakhstan," a milestone for a species that vanished entirely from the wild by the mid-20th century. The program, which began with seven horses from Prague and Berlin zoos, plans to grow the wild herd to roughly 40 animals by 2029.

Global Progress · Uganda

The World Health Organization declared Uganda's Ebola outbreak over.

WHO confirmed the outbreak's end on September 1, exactly 42 days after the country's last confirmed patient — a case imported from the Democratic Republic of the Congo — was discharged from care on July 16. The entire outbreak traced back to people who had crossed the border from the DRC, which is still battling the worst Ebola epidemic in its history, and no further transmission was documented from that single case. In a show of regional solidarity, Uganda's health ministry now plans to open two Ebola treatment clinics of its own along the DRC border to help contain the outbreak still raging next door.

Small Wonders · Anglesey, Wales & Houston, Texas

A critically endangered sea turtle found freezing on a Welsh beach got a 5,000-mile lift toward home.

Rhossi, a Kemp's ridley — one of the world's most endangered sea turtles — was found cold-stunned and weighing just 2 pounds on a Welsh beach in December 2023, then nursed up to 46 pounds over two years at Anglesey Sea Zoo. On August 10, wildlife teams flew him by helicopter to London and then transatlantic to Houston, where veterinarian Dr. Joe Flanagan came out of retirement to oversee his care at the Houston Zoo. Once scans clear the lingering pneumonia in his lungs, Rhossi will be released into the Gulf of Mexico, the warm water he was always meant to call home.

Quiet Kindness · St Albans, Melbourne, Australia

A Melbourne landlord left his longtime tenant the home she had rented from him for 23 years.

John Perrett, a retired pharmacist in the Melbourne suburb of St Albans, phoned his tenant Jane Sayner in 2020 to tell her the two-bedroom unit with the garden she loved was hers, free and clear. Perrett had rented Sayner the property for 23 years and chose to leave it to her in his will rather than sell it, alongside a AUD$19.6 million gift to the Royal Melbourne Hospital. Sayner, now 74 and retired, still lives in the home and tends the same garden that made her want to stay two decades ago.

Everyday Joy · Oulu, Finland

A Finnish carpenter defended his air guitar world title before competitors from 13 countries.

Aapo "The Angus" Rautio scored 34.8 points to win his second consecutive Air Guitar World Championship in Oulu, beating more than 50 competitors from 13 countries with a 60-second routine on an instrument that does not exist. Judges scored contestants on technique, stage presence and "airness," awarding second place to Japan's Nanami "Seven Seas" Nagura and third to the United States' Saladin "Six String Sal" Thomas. Now in its 30th year, the contest still holds to its half-serious founding creed: a person miming a guitar solo cannot also hold a weapon.

Science & Medicine · San Francisco, California

A protein found in frog blood may work as an antidote to a deadly shellfish toxin in humans.

Researchers at UC San Francisco's Cardiovascular Research Institute found that saxiphilin, a protein naturally present in American bullfrogs, binds tightly to saxitoxin — the toxin behind paralytic shellfish poisoning — and neutralizes more than 50 of its variants. In mice, the protein prevented poisoning when given before or during exposure and cured nearly all animals when administered afterward, reaching the brain, heart and muscles as it worked. The findings, published in Nature Communications, point toward a possible treatment for a toxin that sickens an estimated 2,000 people worldwide each year and currently has no approved antidote.

Global Progress · Costa Rica

Costa Rica's lawmakers made wildlife crossings mandatory on roads that cut through animal habitat.

Costa Rica's legislature voted unanimously to require new and existing roads that disrupt wildlife movement to include crossings whenever data shows a road is cutting off a species' path.

Small Wonders · Christmas and Cocos Islands, Australia

Scientists surveying remote Indian Ocean seamounts came home with 149 species new to science.

Researchers trawling unexplored seamounts near Christmas and Cocos Islands surfaced with 149 previously unknown species, including a sea cucumber and a parasitic barnacle.

Science & Medicine · São Paulo State, Brazil

A daily fish-oil and aspirin combination worked as well as antibiotics against severe gum disease.

A year-long trial across four Brazilian universities and Harvard found the combination matched antibiotics in treating severe periodontitis.

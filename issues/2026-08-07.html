Nature · Okefenokee Swamp, Georgia

The Okefenokee Swamp became the first new natural UNESCO World Heritage site in the US in three decades.

The World Heritage Committee inscribed the Okefenokee National Wildlife Refuge on August 4, making it the 27th US site on the list and the first in Georgia, alongside natural wonders like the Great Barrier Reef and Iguazu Falls. The listing followed a $60 million land purchase led by the Conservation Fund that took nearly 8,000 acres bordering the swamp out of reach of a proposed titanium mine, ending a fight that had stalled the project for years. Conservation groups say the designation raises the swamp's profile but not its legal protection, so they are still pressing Georgia lawmakers to ban mining along its edge permanently.

Global Progress · Oromia, Ethiopia

Ethiopia became the first country to have verified forest-carbon credits issued under the World Bank's newest standard.

The World Bank confirmed 14.9 million tonnes of verified emission reductions from Ethiopia's Oromia region, where forest covers more than half the land and over 30 million people live. The credits come from the Oromia Forested Landscape Program, which pays communities to protect and restore forest instead of clearing it, and 12.4 million of the verified tonnes are now eligible for sale on carbon markets. At current prices the credits could unlock $124 million to $372 million for the forest-dependent communities who did the work, the World Bank said.

Science & Medicine · New Zealand

A trial of nearly 4,000 babies found paracetamol and ibuprofen are equally safe in infancy.

Researchers at the University of Auckland and the Medical Research Institute of New Zealand randomly assigned newborns across the country to receive one of the two medicines for fever or pain through their first year, then tracked eczema and bronchiolitis rates in each group. Eczema occurred in about 16 percent of the paracetamol group and 15 percent of the ibuprofen group, a gap lead researcher Professor Stuart Dalziel said was not statistically significant, and bronchiolitis affected roughly 5 percent of children in each group. The results, published in The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health, are expected to ease years of parental uncertainty over which medicine is safer, and the same children will now be followed until age six.

Quiet Kindness · Eastern Cape, South Africa

Two brothers who couldn't afford fuel after a day's work were paid back many times over by strangers.

At a petrol station in South Africa, philanthropist Bi Phakathi and Reward the Kind founder Logan Mayberry noticed two brothers short on cash after a long shift and paid for a full tank of petrol. Two weeks later the pair returned with groceries and R6,000 raised after Mayberry shared a video of the encounter online. One brother said he hopes to use the support to learn to operate a weighbridge, a step toward steadier work.

Small Wonders · British Columbia, Canada

A pod of Pacific white-sided dolphins put on an acrobatic show off British Columbia's coast.

Whale-watching guide Garry Henkel filmed the pod leaping and porpoising through the water on August 5 near the province's central coast, in a display typical of a species known for aerial acrobatics. Pacific white-sided dolphins can leap as high as four metres and travel in groups ranging from a handful of animals to several hundred. Local operators say sightings like this one have become a regular highlight of the summer season.

Everyday Joy · Cirencester, England

A 97-year-old Englishwoman broke her own world record for wing walking, a year after a stroke.

Betty Bromage, of Cheltenham, strapped onto the wing of a biplane at RFC Rendcomb Airfield in Cirencester on August 4 to reclaim the Guinness World Record for oldest female wing walker, a title she first set at age 93. Bromage suffered a stroke in August 2025 that still affects her speech, and she flew this attempt to raise money for the stroke unit at Cheltenham General Hospital, where she was treated. It was her sixth wing walk since she took up the hobby at 87.

Small Wonders · New South Wales, Australia

A drone pilot filmed a humpback whale giving birth off Australia, only the fourth such birth ever captured on camera.

Wildlife photographer Alexander Forrest filmed the birth off the New South Wales coast on July 28, from the legally required distance of more than 100 metres, in what researchers called the first such birth ever captured by drone.

Nature · Spain & Portugal

The Iberian lynx population reached a record 2,663 animals across Spain and Portugal.

Official 2025 census figures showed the wild population up 10.9 percent on the previous year and more than double the total from just four years ago, continuing one of Europe's most cited conservation turnarounds.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Food banks in 47 countries kept half a million tonnes of food out of landfills last year.

Member organizations of the Global FoodBanking Network reached 40.5 million people in 2025, a 6.5 percent rise from the year before, with farm donations of surplus produce growing 55 percent, new network-wide data showed.

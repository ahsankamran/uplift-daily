Nature · Xinjiang & Gansu, China

China's wild Przewalski's horse population has passed 900 individuals — recovered from extinction in the wild through forty years of methodical restoration.

China's State Forestry and Grassland Administration confirmed 900 Przewalski's horses now living wild across the Kalamaili reserve in Xinjiang and the Dunhuang Nanhu reserve in Gansu — up from 11 animals imported from European zoos in 1985, when the species had vanished from the wild entirely. Breeding herds now range across more than 14,000 square kilometres of steppe, with rangers conducting seasonal health checks rather than supplementary feeding — a sign the population is self-sustaining. China plans to mark the milestone at a formal ceremony this summer and to expand the protected corridor that links both reserves.

Science & Medicine · Daejeon, South Korea

Scientists at South Korea's Institute for Basic Science have identified two distinct gut-to-brain signals that redirect eating behaviour when the body runs short of protein.

The IBS team mapped two parallel neural circuits in mice: one that suppresses appetite for carbohydrates and fat, and one that sharpens the drive toward protein-rich food, both activated when dietary protein falls below a threshold. Mice whose circuits were blocked continued to overeat non-protein foods even while protein-deficient, confirming the pathways as necessary — not merely associated — with corrective eating. The findings, published in Science on 23 May 2026, identify specific neurons as targets for treating eating disorders and metabolic conditions linked to chronic protein imbalance.

Quiet Kindness · Strabane, Northern Ireland

A Yorkshire garden designer built a Chelsea show garden around a reflective pool and a self-supporting frame — then donated the whole installation to a food bank in Northern Ireland.

Rob Hardy constructed his RHS Chelsea Flower Show garden this week using salvaged and donated materials, centred on a still water feature and a sculptural timber frame planted with native perennials — then arranged before the show opened to have the entire structure relocated to a Trussell food bank in Strabane, County Tyrone, as a permanent community outdoor space. Hardy said he wanted the garden's life after Chelsea to be more meaningful than its week in London, and that the people who needed it most should be the ones who kept it. The food bank plans to open the garden to visitors and clients from June.

Everyday Joy · Chennai, India

A Chennai scholar has spent thirty years writing forty-seven books and delivering lecture demonstrations that teach audiences how to actually understand Bharatanatyam — not just admire it.

V.P. Dhananjayan, a Bharatanatyam master based in Chennai, has authored 47 books in English and Tamil — including the first English-language guides to the dance's grammar, repertoire, and codified postures — and has toured lecture demonstrations in which he breaks down the movement vocabulary for general audiences who would not otherwise know how to read it. His most recent volume maps all 108 karanas against their original temple sculptures, with photographs taken across 27 temples in South India. Together with his wife and teaching partner Shantha, he has trained more than 3,000 students at their school, Bharatakalanjali.

Small Wonders · Florida, USA

Florida's loggerhead sea turtles are nesting at a pace 82 percent ahead of last year's already-record season — and leatherback nests have crossed 1,000 for the first time.

Florida's Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission recorded 9,232 loggerhead nests on monitored beaches by mid-May 2026, compared with 5,074 at the same point last year, itself a record season. Leatherback nests, from the species listed as critically endangered, passed 1,000 for the first time in monitoring history, spread across Palm Beach, Brevard, and Broward counties. Biologists attribute the surge partly to several years of reduced predation pressure and to favourable sea-surface temperatures along the nesting migration corridor.

Global Progress · European Union

Battery-electric cars claimed one-fifth of all new registrations across Europe in the first quarter of 2026 — a milestone analysts had placed firmly in the next decade.

Data from the European Alternative Fuels Observatory showed battery-electric vehicles reached 20.3 percent of all new passenger car registrations across the 27 EU member states in Q1 2026 — the first quarter to clear the 20 percent threshold, which ACEA and most industry forecasters had not expected until 2028 or later. The Netherlands led at 34 percent BEV share; combined with plug-in hybrids, the electrified share across the EU reached 38 percent. Six countries introduced new purchase incentive schemes in January 2026, and ACEA credited those programmes with accelerating the shift ahead of schedule.

Nature · Royal National Park, New South Wales, Australia

A reintroduced platypus colony in Australia's Royal National Park has grown to 20 known individuals — and for the first time has confirmed a wild-born offspring.

Scientists from UNSW Sydney counted 20 platypuses in the Hacking River this month, including a wild-born subadult captured for the first time, proving the animals are reproducing in consecutive seasons rather than merely surviving.

Science & Medicine · Erasmus MC, Rotterdam, Netherlands

Dutch surgeons used a glowing molecular tracer to find oral cancer cells invisible to the naked eye — and detected every tumour in a phase I/II trial.

A trial at Erasmus MC in Rotterdam used cRGD-ZW800, a near-infrared fluorescent compound, to let surgeons see tumour margins in real time — achieving 100 percent patient-level sensitivity and a higher rate of clean surgical margins than historical controls.

Global Progress · Geneva, Switzerland

The World Health Assembly has adopted its first-ever dedicated resolution on stroke — a condition that now strikes one in four people during their lifetime.

On 22 May 2026, all 193 WHO member states unanimously adopted a resolution on stroke prevention, acute care, and rehabilitation — the first time in the Assembly's 78-year history that stroke has received dedicated attention at this level.

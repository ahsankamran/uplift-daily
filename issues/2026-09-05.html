Nature · Omo Forest Reserve, Nigeria

Former poachers are now the rangers guarding one of Nigeria's last rainforests.

At the Omo Forest Reserve in Ogun State, roughly ten former poachers now patrol as rangers, guarding 130,500 hectares of rainforest in week-long rotations from a solar-powered outpost. Sunday Abiodun, who hunted the reserve for fifteen years before joining the Nigerian Conservation Foundation in 2017, says the tracking skills that once found prey now protect it, working alongside the nonprofit Wild Africa. Camera traps have since recorded forest elephants returning to areas empty for years, and a rise in sightings of the endangered white-throated guenon.

Science & Medicine · Birmingham, England

A urine test now catches nine in ten bladder cancers without a scope.

In a trial across seven NHS urology departments in England and Scotland, the GALEAS Bladder urine test correctly identified 71 of 77 cancers among 964 patients, a 92 percent detection rate. Developed at the University of Birmingham with Cancer Research UK funding, the test screens urine for genetic material shed by tumors and caught every one of the trial's 17 muscle-invasive cases. The results, published this month in European Urology Oncology, suggest the test could spare roughly 730 people per 1,000 an unnecessary, invasive cystoscopy.

Global Progress · China

Solar power capacity overtook coal in China for the first time.

By the end of July, China's installed solar capacity reached 1.286 billion kilowatts, edging past coal's 1.285 billion kilowatts to become the country's single largest power source. The National Energy Administration called it a signature achievement in the country's energy transition, noting solar generation had risen 15.5 percent over the past year to 802.4 billion kilowatt-hours. Coal still generates far more electricity than solar today, since panels sit idle after dark, but the shift breaks a pattern that has held since the birth of China's power industry more than a century ago.

Quiet Kindness · Mathura, India

A German-born woman has spent thirty years giving India's unwanted cows a permanent home.

Friederike Irina Bruning left Germany for India at twenty and settled near Mathura, where she began nursing an injured, abandoned calf back to health in 1996; neighbors soon started bringing her every sick or unwanted cow they found. Her sanctuary, Radha Surabhi Gaushala, now shelters more than 2,500 cows, keeps mothers and calves together for life, and buys in milk for orphaned calves rather than selling any of its own. Over the past three years alone, the trust says its services have reached 15,700 animals.

Everyday Joy · Kannur, Kerala, India

A Kerala home stays cool through monsoon season without a single air conditioner.

Architect Amrutha Kishor designed a house near Kannur for sisters Renu Krishnadas and Rekha Utham using perforated clay lattices, called jali, and a laterite stone frame that pulls air in through low walls and out through angled ceilings. The design layers old techniques: a north-south orientation to catch prevailing breezes, narrow clay openings that speed up airflow the way pursed lips speed an exhale, and double-layered roof tiles that slow the sun's heat. One resident stayed 21 days without once switching on an air conditioner and said she never felt the need to.

Small Wonders · Davidson Seamount, California

A bigfin squid was filmed alive in the northeastern Pacific for the first time.

Scientists aboard the research vessel David Packard filmed a Magnapinna, or bigfin squid, drifting nearly two miles down over the Davidson Seamount off central California on August 7 — the first confirmed live sighting of the species in the northeastern Pacific. The animal held its ten arms out in the elbow-bent pose that has made the genus famous, one of fewer than 70 bigfin squid ever recorded by submersible or trawl. The team, led by MBARI postdoctoral fellow Olívia Soares Pereira, had been studying the seamount's beaked whales when the squid drifted into view, and still cannot say what the pose is for.

Global Progress · Bhutan

Bhutan eliminates dog-transmitted rabies, a first for its region.

The World Health Organization validated Bhutan as the first country in its South-East Asia region to eliminate dog-transmitted human rabies, with zero deaths recorded since June 2023.

Small Wonders · Mindoro, Philippines

A pigeon missing for twenty years reappears on a Philippine mountainside.

A camera trap in the Puerto Galera rainforest photographed a Mindoro bleeding-heart pigeon in August, the first confirmed sighting of the critically endangered bird in two decades.

Everyday Joy · Louth, England

A self-taught carver turns tree trunks into a menagerie, one chainsaw cut at a time.

In Louth, Lincolnshire, Simon Bogg has spent thirty years turning felled trees into lifelike wooden animals using nothing but a chainsaw.

Nature · Sonora, Mexico

Bison are set to roam free in a Mexican desert they vanished from two centuries ago.

The binational nonprofit Cuenca Los Ojos moved a bison herd onto its 120,000-acre preserve in Sonora this summer and plans to release them from a holding pen within weeks, ending roughly 200 years without wild bison in the region. The animals descend from 23 bison the United States donated to a sister reserve in Chihuahua back in 2009, a herd that has since grown into the hundreds. Camera traps have already recorded pumas, lynxes and peccaries returning to the preserve, and organizers hope to add prairie dogs, elk and beavers next.

Small Wonders · Berry Head, Devon, England

An octopus was photographed hitching a ride on a dolphin's back off the English coast.

Volunteer Bill Coulson photographed a common octopus wrapped around the dorsal fin of a Risso's dolphin at Berry Head in Devon, during what researchers call the region's largest octopus bloom in at least 75 years. University of Exeter researcher Josh Symes said the images offer a rare look at how Risso's dolphins feed, as warming seas draw young octopuses north from breeding grounds near the Channel Islands. No one is quite sure if the octopus was hunting, hiding or just along for the ride, but the sighting is already reshaping what scientists know about the dolphins' diet.

Quiet Kindness · Baiona, Spain

A Spanish sailing club has spent fourteen years putting people with disabilities behind the wheel.

Monte Real Yacht Club's Adapted Sailing School in Baiona has run since 2012, spending about €150,000 a year to get dozens of sailors with disabilities onto the water annually, free of charge. César Maquieira, who has used a wheelchair since developing a degenerative muscle disease, joined the program in 2016 and says navigating the sea felt like "a leap, to arrive at something new." The club has since widened the program to seniors, foster children and abuse survivors, and director Olaia Cabo Martinez says the sense of freedom sailors feel leaving their wheelchairs behind is what keeps the volunteers coming back.

Global Progress · Timor-Leste

The World Health Organization's South-East Asia region has eliminated trachoma entirely.

WHO validated on September 3 that Timor-Leste, the region's last country with suspected endemic trachoma, has eliminated the disease as a public health problem, following a 2025 survey of nearly 5,000 children that found no active transmission. It's the 33rd country worldwide and fourth in the region to reach the milestone, built on more than a decade of eye clinic screenings and case-finding across all 13 of the country's municipalities. Trachoma remains the world's leading infectious cause of blindness elsewhere, and WHO is aiming to eliminate it globally by 2030.

Science & Medicine · Neijiang, China

The biggest review yet of knee arthritis treatments found a simple brace beats the rest.

Researchers at First People's Hospital of Neijiang analyzed 139 clinical trials covering nearly 10,000 patients and found knee braces outperformed eleven other non-drug therapies for easing arthritis pain, stiffness and mobility loss. Water-based therapy and regular exercise also ranked highly, while ultrasound treatment came in among the least effective, according to the analysis published in PLOS One. Lead author Yuan Luo says the findings point doctors toward cheap, low-risk options before reaching for drugs or surgery.

Everyday Joy · Roskilde, Denmark

A children's soccer field in Denmark turned out to be sitting on a lost medieval convent.

Ground-penetrating radar surveys in Roskilde revealed the largely intact ruins of St. Clare's Convent, founded in 1256, buried just three feet beneath a field that's hosted football since 1961. Researchers from ROMU, Denmark's National Museum and Norway's NIKU institute mapped roughly 230 feet of pillars, cloisters, a courtyard and a church, with NIKU archaeologist Arne Abel Stamnes calling the scan results "simply impressive." Excavations are planned next, and researchers expect to uncover pottery, pilgrims' badges and graves that could reveal who the convent's nuns were before it closed during the Reformation.

Nature · Altyn Dala Reserve, Kazakhstan

Wild Przewalski's horses have foaled on the Kazakh steppe for the first time in roughly two centuries.

Two colts were born at a reintroduction center inside Kazakhstan's Altyn Dala reserve, descendants of horses flown in from Prague Zoo and Tierpark Berlin.

Global Progress · Chile

Chile became the first country in South America validated for eliminating dog-transmitted rabies.

The World Health Organization credited decades of free pet vaccination; Chile's last fatal dog-bite rabies case was in 1972.

Small Wonders · Sepik River, Papua New Guinea

A Papua New Guinean fisherwoman found a freshwater fish not seen by science in forty years.

Helen Asu pulled a spinach pipefish from her hand net, a seahorse relative unrecorded by science since 1985.

Everyday Joy · Sinakara Valley, Cusco Region, Peru

Tens of thousands of Andean pilgrims gather in the Sinakara Valley this weekend for Qoyllur Rit'i — a UNESCO-listed mountain pilgrimage that marks the Pleiades' return to the night sky.

On the night the Pleiades constellation reappears after a weeks-long absence from the southern sky, pilgrims from scores of highland communities in Peru's Cusco region converge on the Sinakara glacier at 4,600 metres, dancing through snow and sub-zero darkness in elaborate embroidered costumes representing mythical figures. The festival blends Andean cosmological tradition — the Pleiades' return signals the opening of the agricultural calendar — with Catholic Corpus Christi practice in a form UNESCO recognised as intangible cultural heritage in 2011. This year's main day falls on 2 June; the Ukukus, the masked half-human half-bear guardians of the glacier, will lead the dawn procession as they have for centuries.

Science & Medicine · London, UK / Global

An antisense drug produced a functional cure for chronic hepatitis B in 19 percent of trial participants — the first phase 3 result to show any medicine clearing the infection entirely.

In a trial of 1,834 adults across 29 countries, bepirovirsen eliminated the hepatitis B surface antigen and reduced viral DNA to undetectable levels in 19 percent of participants, sustained 24 weeks after all treatment stopped — compared with zero in the placebo group. The results, published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 28 May 2026 and presented at the EASL congress in Milan, are the first evidence that any hepatitis B drug can fully clear the infection at phase 3 scale. Chronic hepatitis B affects an estimated 254 million people worldwide and is the leading cause of liver cancer; regulatory applications are pending in the United States, Europe, Japan, and China.

Quiet Kindness · Bardiya, Nepal

A volunteer network of 3,300 people in Nepal's Bardiya district patrols the forest edge each evening — using fire, laser light, and sound to redirect tigers and elephants away from the villages they share a border with.

The Community-Based Anti-Poaching Units (CBAPU), organised across 93 neighbourhood groups around Bardiya National Park, send volunteers out every night with torches, laser pointers, and noise-making tools to steer wild animals away from homesteads before an encounter can escalate. During daylight, the same volunteers walk door-to-door teaching neighbours how to read animal tracks and move safely along forest paths. The movement grew from a handful of local families and now co-ordinates thousands of people; organisers are seeking formal government recognition so that volunteers can receive basic equipment and training without covering the costs themselves.

Small Wonders · Ithaca, New York, USA

Five and a half million wild bees are living under a New York cemetery — discovered when a lab technician noticed the grass buzzing on her walk to work.

Cornell entomologists found that East Lawn Cemetery in Ithaca hosts an estimated 5.5 million Andrena regularis (regular mining bee) in 1.5 acres of undisturbed sandy soil — equivalent in scale to more than 200 beehives and one of the largest single-species aggregations ever documented. The colony has probably occupied the cemetery for over a century, emerging each spring between late March and mid-May to pollinate nearby apple orchards and garden plants. The study, published in Apidologie and covered by ScienceDaily on 27 May, adds the cemetery to a growing map of informal urban pollinator refuges that thrive in the sandy, undisturbed ground typical of historic burial sites.

Nature · North Sea / Aberdeen, Scotland

A 42-year study of offshore structures in 18 countries finds that decommissioned oil and gas platforms can evolve into reef habitats rivalling natural rocky seafloors in biodiversity.

Researchers at the University of Aberdeen and the Scottish Association for Marine Science reviewed evidence from offshore structures across 18 countries spanning 42 years, finding that as platforms age, sponges, corals, and commercially important fish colonise the steel and concrete surfaces in densities comparable to natural reef systems. Lead researcher Megan Squire found that the ecological value of a structure depends on its age and location, and that some North Sea platforms host species assemblages as rich as any natural rocky seafloor in the region. The study, published this week, calls on UK regulators to adopt a case-by-case approach to decommissioning, so that the most biodiverse artificial reefs can be designated for permanent retention rather than removed by default.

Global Progress · Sudan & South Sudan

Sudan and South Sudan have each received WHO validation for eliminating maternal and neonatal tetanus — completing decades of vaccination work in some of the world's most logistically difficult conditions.

Both countries met the global threshold for elimination — fewer than one tetanus case per 1,000 live births in every district — following successive rounds of mass vaccination campaigns that reached isolated communities by boat, motorcycle, and on foot. Sudan's assessment confirmed all 188 districts met the criterion, using 2022 data validated through a rigorous WHO-led process; South Sudan's completion ends a chapter on a disease that once killed a high proportion of the region's newborns. With these two confirmations, fewer than a dozen countries remain in the global campaign that began in 1989, and UNICEF credits frontline health workers who carried vaccines to communities with no fixed health facilities.

Nature · Xinjiang & Gansu, China

China's wild Przewalski's horse population has passed 900 individuals — recovered from extinction in the wild through forty years of methodical restoration.

China's State Forestry and Grassland Administration confirmed 900 Przewalski's horses now living wild across the Kalamaili reserve in Xinjiang and the Dunhuang Nanhu reserve in Gansu — up from 11 animals in 1985, when the species had vanished from the wild entirely.

Science & Medicine · Daejeon, South Korea

Scientists at South Korea's Institute for Basic Science have identified two distinct gut-to-brain signals that redirect eating behaviour when the body runs short of protein.

The IBS team mapped two parallel neural circuits — one suppressing appetite for carbohydrates and fat, one sharpening the drive toward protein — both activated when dietary protein falls below a threshold, with findings published in Science on 23 May 2026.

Quiet Kindness · Strabane, Northern Ireland

A Yorkshire garden designer built a Chelsea show garden around a reflective pool and a self-supporting frame — then donated the whole installation to a food bank in Northern Ireland.

Rob Hardy arranged for his entire RHS Chelsea Flower Show garden — built from salvaged materials and centred on a still water feature — to be relocated to a Trussell food bank in Strabane, County Tyrone, as a permanent community outdoor space opening in June.

Global Progress · Easter Island, Chile

Rapa Nui's Indigenous people now govern one of the largest marine reserves on Earth.

The waters around Easter Island have become a 270,000-square-mile protected reserve called Te Moana Tapu a Hotu Matu'a, with the Rapa Nui people holding six of eleven seats on the council that governs it alongside Chilean officials. The reserve combines ranger patrols, scientific monitoring and Polynesian traditional knowledge, and this year won the international Blue Park Award for its conservation standards. Researchers believe the protected waters may hold undiscovered breeding grounds for blue, fin and humpback whales.

Quiet Kindness · Uttar Pradesh, India

More than 150 farmers in Uttar Pradesh now patrol elephant corridors together each night.

Volunteers called Gajmitras, or friends of the elephant, fan out in teams of five around 11 p.m. across six villages bordering Katarniaghat Wildlife Sanctuary, using torches and whistles to guide 50 to 150 migrating elephants safely past homes and fields. The program, launched in 2021 by the Nature Environment and Wildlife Society with the Uttar Pradesh Forest Department, has grown from four women volunteers to more than 150 trained patrollers. Crop damage in the patrolled villages has fallen by nearly 70 percent over the past year, and neither elephants nor people have been harmed.

Nature · Siak, Sumatra, Indonesia

A palm oil farmer in Sumatra is replanting the mangroves his coastline lost to the sea.

Coastal erosion eats roughly 6.4 meters of shoreline a year in Siak district on Sumatra's northern coast, and Harisyah, a 53-year-old farmer and village head, has spent over a decade studying and replanting the mangroves that once slowed it. Working with the nonprofits Yayasan Gambut and the Global Environment Centre, he has helped restore stretches of the Beting Selayang coastline, including a site within 500 meters of a local elementary school. Indonesia fell short of its national goal to restore 600,000 hectares of mangroves by 2024, but Harisyah's plots are becoming a template other villages are starting to copy.

Small Wonders · Moorpark, California

A rescued horse who wouldn't be touched now plays guitar and harp for her own album.

At a horse rescue in Moorpark, California, a once-skittish seven-year-old named Yupia spent nine months warming to people before she began nuzzling and chomping the strings of a guitar and harp brought to her stall. Singer-songwriter Mikayla Khramov, who had been working to earn the horse's trust without treats or traditional training, recorded Yupia's sounds and wove them into an album blending Portuguese folk and electronic music. The record, Khramov's first, is due out in September.

Everyday Joy · Cardiff, Wales

A deaf interpreter brings the roar of stadium concerts to deaf fans through sign language.

Luke Holdsworth, who was born profoundly deaf, has become one of Britain's most sought-after performance interpreters, translating not just lyrics but the mood and energy of a show, down to air-guitar flourishes, at venues from Wembley to Cardiff's Principality Stadium. He interpreted all 14 dates of Oasis's 2024 reunion tour, a role he traces back to watching interpreter Tracey Tyer work Latitude Festival seven years earlier. "You get caught up in the atmosphere, it's electric," he says of bringing deaf fans into the middle of a crowd's excitement.

Science & Medicine · Sheffield, England

A radiation-free lung scan is helping doctors catch children's breathing problems earlier.

Researchers at the University of Sheffield have brought a hyperpolarized xenon-129 gas MRI technique into everyday use at Sheffield Children's Hospital, letting doctors watch a child's lungs fill, exchange gas and move blood in a scan that takes only a few minutes and uses no radiation. Children simply inhale the prepared gas, hold their breath briefly, and breathe it back out while the machine images ventilation and structure at once. In cystic fibrosis patients, the scans have already caught lung abnormalities that routine CT scans missed, and the Sheffield team is working with GE HealthCare to retrofit existing MRI machines elsewhere with the technology.

Nature · Cumbria, England

Ospreys hatched at an English nature reserve for the first time since Victorian times.

Three chicks hatched this June at RSPB Campfield Marsh in Cumbria, the first successful osprey breeding recorded at an RSPB reserve in England since the 1840s.

Global Progress · Brazilian Amazon

Deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon fell to its lowest level in thirteen years.

Brazil's national space agency recorded 2,874 square kilometers of deforestation alerts over the past year, a 36 percent drop and the smallest area cleared since 2013.

Science & Medicine · Southampton, England

A fridge-free vaccine passed its first human trial in Southampton.

In a trial of 60 adults, the reformulated tetanus-diphtheria vaccine SPVX02 produced protective antibody levels in every participant within 28 days.

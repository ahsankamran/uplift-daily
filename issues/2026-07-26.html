Nature · Kasungu National Park, Malawi

Four African wild dogs turned up in a Malawi park that hadn't seen the species in more than a decade.

A field team from the International Fund for Animal Welfare spotted the pack near Kasungu National Park's eastern gate on July 17, the first wild dogs recorded there in over ten years. Photo-matching against a database from Zambia's South Luangwa National Park showed the animals had crossed roughly 300 kilometres and an international border entirely on their own. Conservationists now hope the shared Malawi-Zambia Transfrontier Conservation Area will let more dogs move freely between the two parks in the years ahead.

Everyday Joy · Pune, India

A 62-year-old turned a spare room in her Pune home into a craft studio that's now taught more than 100 neighbours to sew, crochet and quilt.

Rhoda Gaikwad Staneley opened Creator's Hands Craft Room a year ago this month, running sessions of four to eight people at a time in embroidery, crochet and quilting. Teenagers, expecting mothers and retirees have all pulled up a chair, including one regular who says the classes showed her a creative side she never knew she had. The room now doubles as a weekly gathering point, with participants staying in touch over a shared group chat between sessions.

Quiet Kindness · Brunswick Heads, Australia

A volunteer-run lunch club is gathering isolated seniors around one table each week in a small Australian town.

Local resident Laura Upsall launched The Garden Table in Brunswick Heads, a free weekly lunch for people over 60 built around company, gentle movement, nutrition and mental wellbeing. A roster of volunteers, among them a physiotherapist, a dietitian, a music therapist and a local GP, are donating their time as guest speakers at no cost to attendees. The first gathering on July 29 will double as a 93rd birthday celebration for one of the group's earliest members.

Small Wonders · Arunachal Pradesh, India

Botanists confirmed a delicate alpine herb growing in India for the first time in 158 years.

A team from the Botanical Survey of India found Cyananthus hookeri, a purple-blue flowered herb, growing at 3,600 metres in Arunachal Pradesh's Chuna Valley, the first record in the country since 1867. Botanist Sudhanshu Sekhar Dash and colleagues counted fewer than 50 mature plants, all clustered in one remote pocket of the Eastern Himalaya. The team is recommending the species for endangered status and long-term monitoring, calling it proof that alpine ecosystems still hide plant life no one has catalogued.

Science & Medicine · Nottingham, England

A cheap daily fibre supplement eased knee arthritis pain in a six-week trial.

University of Nottingham researchers gave 117 adults with knee osteoarthritis 20 grams of the prebiotic fibre inulin each day, either alone or alongside a digital physiotherapy programme, against a placebo. Inulin significantly reduced pain and pain sensitivity and improved grip strength, an effect the team traced to gut bacteria producing more butyrate and GLP-1, compounds tied to inflammation and muscle health. The supplement also had far fewer dropouts than the physiotherapy arm, with the findings published in the journal Nutrients.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Global road deaths have fallen 21 percent since 2011, even as a billion more vehicles joined the world's roads.

The World Health Organization's new report counted 1.16 million road deaths in 2025, with the steepest declines in Europe, down 36 percent, and the Western Pacific, down 15 percent, while Africa's toll rose 17 percent. Ten countries have already cut road deaths in half since 2021, the baseline year for a UN target to halve deaths and injuries worldwide by 2030. WHO is urging the rest to adopt lower speed limits, stronger enforcement and safer road design to keep pace.

Small Wonders · Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, South Africa

A litter of bat-eared fox pups turned a den site in the Kalahari into their own private racetrack.

Wildlife photographer Peet van Schalkwyk caught a litter of bat-eared fox pups sprinting circles around their den in South Africa's Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, their mother watching from a few metres off as the pups broke away to check in before racing back into the chase.

Nature · Northumberland, England

England's largest private rewilding project has begun on 3,035 hectares of Northumberland moorland.

Nature-restoration company RESTORE completed its purchase of the Emblehope and Burngrange estate in Northumberland, planning to rewet roughly 800 hectares of drained peatland over three decades and let curlews, golden eagles and cranes recolonise naturally.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Global hunger fell for a third straight year, easing to 7.8 percent of the world's population in 2025.

The United Nations' State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2026 report found global hunger eased to 7.8 percent of the population in 2025, down from 8.6 percent in 2022, though Africa's rate held near 20 percent.

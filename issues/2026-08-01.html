Nature · Banat, Serbia

Serbia's national bird, the eastern imperial eagle, just had its best breeding season on record.

Twenty-nine breeding pairs were recorded across Serbia this year, and sixteen of them raised twenty-five fledged chicks — the most successful season since the Serbian Bird Protection and Research Society began targeted conservation work in 2017, when just one breeding pair remained in the country. Researchers are now fitting young eagles with radio transceivers to track where they settle and hunt as the population expands into new territory along the Tisa River and beyond. The eastern imperial eagle appears on Serbia's flag and remains classified as vulnerable by the IUCN, but conservationists call this year's rebound a sign of what eight years of steady protection work can achieve.

Science & Medicine · Michigan, United States

A one-minute, drill-free treatment stopped tooth decay in more than half of treated baby teeth, a trial found.

In a Phase 3 trial of 830 children under six recruited across Michigan, New York and Iowa, twice-yearly applications of 38 percent silver diamine fluoride arrested tooth decay in just over half of treated teeth, compared with about a fifth of teeth in the placebo group, according to results published in JAMA Pediatrics. The liquid is brushed directly onto a cavity in under a minute and requires no drilling, injections or sedation, though it permanently darkens the treated spot. Researchers at the University of Michigan, who led the trial, say the low-cost treatment could expand access to cavity care for young children and for patients with dental anxiety or limited access to conventional dentistry.

Global Progress · Europe

Europe's road and industrial pollution has dropped by more than a third in a decade, a new assessment finds.

Industrial sulphur oxide emissions across Europe fell 59 percent since 2015 and industrial nitrogen oxides fell 39 percent, according to the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service's latest assessment. Road transport made similar gains over the same period, with nitrogen oxide emissions down 40 percent and fine particulate matter, the pollutant most linked to lung and heart disease, down 34 percent. The agency credits long-standing air quality policies, cleaner industrial processes and stricter vehicle standards, and says the improvement is continuing rather than leveling off.

Quiet Kindness · Thiruvananthapuram, India

A Kerala mother turned her hardest years into a trust that now supports 200 other mothers.

After her husband left when their daughter Sandra was diagnosed with microcephaly in 2010, Sheeja spent years learning what mothers of children with disabilities needed most, then founded the Snehasandram Charitable Trust in Thiruvananthapuram in 2021. The trust is run exclusively by and for such mothers, and now supports more than 200 women across Kerala with livelihood training, emergency medical aid and a shared workspace. "We never wanted them to depend on us," Sheeja said. "We wanted them to stand on their own again."

Small Wonders · Derbyshire, England

A heatwave revealed a 17th-century garden design hidden under an English lawn for 350 years.

Record heat and drought at Chatsworth House in Derbyshire dried the grass unevenly enough to reveal the outline of the Great Parterre, an ornate garden of flowerbeds and paths measuring 473 by 227 feet, laid out around 1699 for the 1st Duke of Devonshire. The grass now covering the site has shallower roots than the surrounding lawn, so it browned faster in the heat and traced the old design like a photograph developing. Chatsworth's gardeners say they now have an exact record of the parterre's shape, more than three centuries after it was grassed over.

Everyday Joy · Conwy, Wales

A man returned two library books 116 years overdue, and no one asked for the fine.

A Conwy man returned two books to Llanrwst library that his grandfather, World War veteran Walter Griffith Owen, had borrowed from what was then the town's public library and reading room back in 1910. The books, originally donated to the library in the 1890s by English industrialist Benjamin Armitage, still carried their original 1894 date stamps when they were handed back this summer. At the original penny-a-week late fee, the fine would have run into the pounds many times over, but librarians in Conwy said they were simply glad to have the volumes home.

Science & Medicine · United States

A cell therapy slowed heart and muscle decline in boys with advanced Duchenne muscular dystrophy, a trial found.

Quarterly infusions of the cell therapy deramiocel slowed upper-limb muscle decline by 54 percent and eased heart-muscle scarring by 91 percent in a Phase 3 trial of 106 boys and young men, results published in The Lancet show.

Nature · Corrientes, Argentina

Giant anteaters have spread into Brazil from a population that started at zero in Argentina.

Fundación Rewilding Argentina's giant anteater reintroduction has grown from zero animals in 2007 to roughly 200, and camera traps just caught one more than 100 kilometers from where it started, in Brazil.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Global hunger fell below 8 percent for the first time since the pandemic, the UN reports.

The share of the world's population that is malnourished fell to 7.8 percent in 2026, the UN Food and Agriculture Organization reported, the lowest level since before the pandemic.

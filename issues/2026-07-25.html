Small Wonders · Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, South Africa

A litter of bat-eared fox pups turned a den site in the Kalahari into their own private racetrack.

Wildlife photographer Peet van Schalkwyk caught a litter of bat-eared fox pups sprinting circles around their den in South Africa's Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, chasing, pouncing and nipping at each other in bursts of speed. Their mother stood watch a few metres off as the pups broke from the chaos every so often to check in with her before racing back into the fray, play that helps young foxes build the coordination and social bonds they need as adults. Bat-eared foxes typically raise two to six pups per litter in small family groups, and sightings like this one out of the Kgalagadi circulate widely among the park's regular trackers and guides.

Nature · Northumberland, England

England's largest private rewilding project has begun on 3,035 hectares of Northumberland moorland.

Nature-restoration company RESTORE completed its purchase of the Emblehope and Burngrange estate between Otterburn and Kielder Forest, financed with debt from Triodos Bank UK, taking on land long managed for grouse shooting. Over three decades the group plans to rewet roughly 800 hectares of drained peatland and block old drainage ditches, work expected to store some 100,000 cubic metres of water and ease flooding downstream. Founder Benedict Macdonald expects curlews, golden eagles and common cranes to recolonise naturally as the habitat recovers, with reintroductions of species such as black grouse and wildcat under consideration.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Global hunger fell for a third straight year, easing to 7.8 percent of the world's population in 2025.

The United Nations' State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2026 report found 7.8 percent of people faced hunger in 2025, down from 8.1 percent in 2024 and 8.6 percent in 2022, a drop of roughly 43 million people from the 2022 figure. Five UN agencies compiled the report, crediting steady gains across Asia and Latin America, though Africa's hunger rate held near 20 percent and nearly a third of the world still cannot afford a healthy diet. The authors called the progress fragile and urged governments to sustain it through 2030, the UN's deadline for ending hunger altogether.

Everyday Joy · United Kingdom

Ten of Britain's toughest trees are competing for Tree of the Year, under the theme against the odds.

The Woodland Trust released its ten-strong shortlist for Tree of the Year 2026, each nominee chosen for having survived some extraordinary ordeal. Entrants include a Surrey yew possibly 4,000 years old that took a cannonball during the English Civil War and now holds a meeting room inside its hollow trunk, and a Devon yew that has grown atop a church tower for more than two centuries. Public voting runs through September 29 on the Woodland Trust's website, with the winner announced in early October.

Science & Medicine · Fukuoka, Japan

A newly identified compound helped aging muscle repair itself more effectively in lab tests.

Researchers at Kyushu University's Faculty of Agriculture, led by Professor Ryuichi Tatsumi, found that a sulfur compound called LASSS shields a key muscle-repair protein, HGF, from the chemical damage that blunts its function with age. Treated HGF bound more than twice as effectively to its receptor as the untreated protein, and mice with muscle atrophy showed markedly less of that damage, with the findings published July 24 in Scientific Reports. The team believes the mechanism applies broadly, from humans to companion animals, and is now testing LASSS in aging animals to check its safety before any human trials.

Quiet Kindness · Auburn, New York

A barber who has cut hair for free for a decade now runs a monthly event just for men in need.

Craig Diego, a barber and city councilor, opened his basement shop for "Servanthood Shape-Ups" on July 18, inviting men facing homelessness and financial hardship in Auburn, New York, in for haircuts at no cost. Diego has quietly given away free cuts for ten years, and the new recurring date means the men can plan around it rather than wait for an occasional favor. He says the goal is simple, a reminder that everyone deserves to feel presentable, and he plans to keep the basement shop open on a regular schedule going forward.

Nature · Alta Floresta, Brazil

Rope bridges strung through the Amazon canopy have logged 15,000 animal crossings without a single roadkill.

Biologist Fernanda Abra's Projeto Reconecta strung eight rope bridges through the rainforest canopy in Alta Floresta, Mato Grosso, recording 15,000 animal crossings over 15 months with zero roadkill below.

Small Wonders · Düsseldorf, Germany

Scientists found the exact brain switch that tells a worker bee which job to do.

Biologists at Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf traced honeybees' age-based division of labor to specific neurons, and silencing them reverted older workers to nursing the queen.

Global Progress · Zambia

Zambia launched the final round of a polio campaign aiming to reach 3.9 million children.

Zambia's Ministry of Health deployed 7,830 vaccination teams across six provinces to reach 3.9 million children under five, closing out a three-round campaign covering 71 districts.

Nature · Corrientes, Argentina

A giant anteater turned up in an Argentine reserve that hadn't seen one in forty years.

A male giant anteater was spotted last year at the Alma Guaraní Natural Reserve in Argentina's Corrientes province, the region's first recorded sighting of the species since 1986. Researcher Rosario Montiel of the National University of the Northeast called the appearance "the first register of the giant anteater in 40 years in Corrientes province, and it was spontaneous," arriving after four years of restoration work returned ecological function to more than 150 of the reserve's 6,400 hectares, with native trees replanted on eleven more. Montiel's team now hopes to bring in female anteaters to keep the newcomer company, though roaming dogs and fragmented farmland still make it hard for the animals to move between forest patches.

Small Wonders · Zambia

Three wild dogs set an African distance record hunting for a place to call home.

Three male African wild dogs, nicknamed the Mayukuyuku males, left Zambia's Kafue National Park in mid-2025 and wandered 4,126 kilometers over nine months, the longest movement ever recorded for an African mammal. Matt Becker of the Zambian Carnivore Programme tracked the trio by GPS collar as they passed through the cities of Kitwe and Ndola before settling in a game reserve between South Luangwa and Lower Zambezi National Parks. The trek, roughly half the distance from Cairo to Cape Town, is part of a wider study following 19 wild dog groups as the endangered species searches for new territory and mates across a fragmented landscape.

Quiet Kindness · Henrico, Virginia, USA

A barbecue chef drove a stranded regular home, then bought her lunch.

Executive chef Andrew Griffin found an elderly customer named Miss Duke waiting outside Deep Run Roadhouse's new Henrico, Virginia location one Sunday, after a senior transportation group dropped her at a restaurant that hadn't opened yet. Rather than let her walk home, Griffin drove her in the restaurant's van and took her to lunch nearby, thinking of his own grandmother along the way: "she probably didn't have food in her refrigerator." Owner Paul Hubbard later nominated Griffin for a local kindness award, telling him "what you did for Miss Duke is just a small piece of what you do for all these guys on a daily basis."

Everyday Joy · Sysmä, Finland

A metal detectorist dug up two Viking silver hoards in one afternoon.

Kalle Lappinen was searching a field in his hometown of Sysmä, Finland, when his detector signaled sharply, leading him to a hoard of compacted 11th-century coins about eighteen inches down, then a second nearby hoard with more coins, a silver bead, a bracelet and a charm. The roughly ten pounds of silver mark the largest Viking-era find in Finnish history, and archaeologist Eljas Oksanen praised Lappinen's decision to report it right away as "responsible and responsive detecting." The silver is now being studied and preserved at Lahti City Museums, adding fresh evidence of Viking-age trade reaching deep into Finland.

Global Progress · Ghana

Nine African countries have more than doubled average incomes since 1990.

A new analysis from Our World in Data finds Ghana, Mauritius, Egypt, Cabo Verde, Rwanda, Ethiopia, Uganda, Burkina Faso and Mozambique have all more than doubled average incomes since 1990, with Ghana's rising from about $1,850 to roughly $7,360 per person. Extreme poverty and child mortality fell in all nine countries over the same period. Mauritius leads the group at nearly $28,770 per person, though researchers note the figures are national averages and don't show how the gains are shared within each country.

Science & Medicine · Tabriz, Iran

The largest review yet of whole grains found a sweet spot for heart health.

Dr. Helda Tutunchi of Tabriz University of Medical Sciences led a meta-analysis of 87 clinical trials and more than 6,500 participants, published September 15 in the European Heart Journal, and found the strongest cardiovascular benefits at 60 to 100 grams of whole grains a day, roughly four to six servings. Across trials from Asia, Europe, North America, Australia and Brazil, that range brought improvements in body weight, blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, triglycerides and inflammation, whether from oatmeal, whole grain bread or brown rice. Most trials lasted only about eight weeks, so the team says longer studies are still needed to confirm whether the benefits hold up over time or translate into fewer heart attacks and strokes.

Nature · Sonora, Mexico

Bison are set to roam free in a Mexican desert they vanished from two centuries ago.

The binational nonprofit Cuenca Los Ojos moved a bison herd onto its 120,000-acre preserve in Sonora, ending roughly 200 years without wild bison in the region.

Small Wonders · Berry Head, Devon, England

An octopus was photographed hitching a ride on a dolphin's back off the English coast.

Volunteer Bill Coulson caught a common octopus wrapped around a Risso's dolphin's fin, during the region's largest octopus bloom in at least 75 years.

Quiet Kindness · Baiona, Spain

A Spanish sailing club has spent fourteen years putting people with disabilities behind the wheel.

Monte Real Yacht Club's Adapted Sailing School gets dozens of sailors with disabilities onto the water each year, free of charge.

Everyday Joy · Rebild, Denmark

A Danish homeowner dug up the country's largest Viking silver hoard by accident.

Thirty minutes into digging in his backyard in Rebild municipality, a Danish landowner struck a cache of roughly 700 silver objects, including bracelets, coins and a Thor's hammer amulet. The hoard weighs more than 40 pounds, nearly three times the previous record, and includes English coins minted between 899 and 924 alongside a silver bar marked with runes. The North Jutland Museums are now cataloguing the find, which is expected to go on permanent display at the Viking Museum Fyrkat.

Small Wonders · Cozumel Island, Mexico

A critically endangered raccoon on a Mexican island taught her family to make toys out of trash.

On Cozumel Island, graduate student Nessie O'Neil watched a young pygmy raccoon dunk a paper receipt in water, roll it into a ball, and bat it around like a plaything. The raccoon's mother and sister soon copied the technique, giving researchers a rare documented case of object-based social learning passed between animals in the raccoon family. Scientists call it the first evidence of this kind of tool-making culture in a species found nowhere else on Earth.

Global Progress · Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka marked a decade of staying free of malaria and newborn tetanus.

The World Health Organization recognized Sri Lanka this week for ten consecutive years without a locally transmitted malaria case since its 2016 certification. The country has also sustained the elimination of maternal and neonatal tetanus, which health officials credit to steady investment in immunization, vector control and antenatal care reaching cities and rural areas alike. WHO cited Sri Lanka's primary healthcare system as a model for how sustained, unglamorous public health work can hold a hard-won gain for good.

Quiet Kindness · Highbury, Adelaide, Australia

A community garden planted three decades ago is still growing neighbours together in Adelaide.

In Highbury, on Adelaide's outskirts, the Pioneer Court Community Garden marked thirty years of shared plots this month. Mario Minuzzo, now 88, started it from an overgrown lot in 1996 to recreate the feeling of an Italian piazza, and it now holds more than 40 plots, 75 members and some 400 fruit and nut trees. Members still donate surplus produce to local charities, keeping Minuzzo's original idea of a meeting place alive for a new generation.

ABC News · Photo by David Clode
Nature · Inn River, Bavaria

A fish missing from a Bavarian river for a century is expanding its range again.

Researchers at the Technical University of Munich documented the Danube gudgeon in a new stretch of the Inn River, a decade after it reappeared nearby following a hundred-year absence. Scientist Christoffer Nagel found the fish thirteen kilometers from its last known spot, during surveys tied to a hydropower project studying how nature-like fishways help river life recover. Researchers see the sighting as a sign the endangered species is spreading again on its own.

Technical University of Munich · Photo by Simon Spring
Science & Medicine · London, England

A diabetes drug was linked to fewer asthma attacks and COPD flare-ups in a large UK study.

Researchers at Imperial College London compared medical records from more than 80,000 UK patients and found semaglutide users had nearly 40 percent fewer asthma attacks than those on an older diabetes drug. Led by Professor Chloe Bloom, the real-world analysis, presented at the European Respiratory Society Congress in Barcelona, also linked the drug to roughly 20 percent fewer COPD flare-ups. Researchers caution that clinical trials are still needed before the drug is prescribed specifically for lung conditions.

Small Wonders · Aguarico River, Ecuador

Seven Indigenous women are hand-raising a threatened river turtle back from decline in the Ecuadorian Amazon.

Siekopai women known as the Tari Nomiowa'i patrol the Aguarico River logging charapa turtle nests before moving the eggs to a protected incubation site.

Nature · Mendocino Coast, California

Coho salmon returned to Northern California rivers in the highest numbers researchers have seen in decades.

An estimated 30,000 coho salmon returned to Mendocino coast rivers last season, ten times the region's 10-year average.

Science & Medicine · Denmark

Starting statins within a year of a diabetes diagnosis was linked to a lower dementia risk in a large Danish study.

Danish researchers tracked more than 132,000 adults with type 2 diabetes and found early statin use lowered relative dementia risk by 15 percent.

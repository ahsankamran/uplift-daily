Small Wonders · Central Pacific Ocean

Scientists have filmed a goblin shark alive in the wild for the first time in the species' known history.

University of Hawaiʻi researchers recovered footage of two healthy goblin sharks moving freely on the seafloor, one near a seamount by Jarvis Island and another along the slope of the Tonga Trench, nearly 700 meters deeper than the species had ever been recorded. Doctoral candidate Aaron Judah and co-author Alan Jamieson pieced the sightings together from submersible expeditions in 2019 and 2024, publishing the findings this month in the Journal of Fish Biology. Every prior record of the shark, a lineage some 125 million years old, came from animals hauled to the surface on fishing lines, so the new footage is the first look at how the long-jawed predator actually lives.

Nature · Western Australia

Australia's numbat has climbed off the endangered list after four decades of fox and cat control.

The IUCN has moved the numbat, a termite-eating marsupial that fell to roughly 300 animals by the late 1970s, from endangered to near threatened, with the population now estimated at 2,000 to 3,000. Wildlife scientists at Western Australia's Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions, working with Perth Zoo and volunteer groups, rebuilt the numbers by trapping foxes and feral cats and seeding new colonies with translocated and zoo-bred animals. Researchers caution the predator control has to continue without pause, or the numbat's still-small population could slide back toward extinction.

Global Progress · Germany

Renewable power supplied 61.8 percent of Germany's electricity in the first half of 2026, a new national record.

Solar generation climbed to 43.2 terawatt-hours and wind supplied nearly a third of the grid, according to the Fraunhofer Institute for Solar Energy Systems, which tracks the country's public electricity mix. Germany added roughly 7 gigawatts of new solar capacity in six months and cut net electricity imports to 1.3 terawatt-hours, down from 9.6 terawatt-hours over the same period last year. Fraunhofer researchers say the surge in clean generation helped keep electricity prices comparatively stable even as natural gas costs rose elsewhere in Europe.

Quiet Kindness · Uvongo, South Africa

A stranger built a shelter for a South African feral cat colony overnight and never said who they were.

Antoinette, who has fed the Uvongo Pier cat and bird colony alone for four years, arrived one morning in July to find a full wooden shelter waiting, fitted with lounging pads, sleeping coves and warm blankets. Whoever built it also left fresh food for the cats and treats for the birds and a resident dassie, then left without a name.

Everyday Joy · Bologna, Italy

University of Pisa students built and flew a paper airplane with a wingspan longer than a bowling lane.

Seven aerospace engineering students, joined by sixteen helpers, folded and glued a 65.75-foot-wingspan paper plane named Icarus and flew it across a hangar in Bologna to break a Guinness World Record held since 2013. The 62.8-pound aircraft, built from nothing but paper and glue, only needed to clear 49 feet to qualify — a distance the team says began as paper planes tossed between lectures.

Science & Medicine · Ann Arbor, Michigan, United States

A gut-repairing drug reversed severe fatty liver disease in animal trials by starving out a harmful bacterium.

University of Michigan researchers found that an experimental compound called DT-109 reversed metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis in mice and primates by cutting levels of the gut bacterium Clostridium perfringens and strengthening the intestinal lining. The findings, published July 11 in the Journal of Clinical Investigation, point to a new treatment approach for a liver disease that affects roughly 7 percent of people worldwide and has no approved cure.

Science & Medicine · Japan

A bacterium from the gut of a Japanese tree frog cleared 100 percent of tumors in mice with a single injection.

Researchers isolated the strain from wild frogs and found it multiplies inside low-oxygen tumors before the immune system clears it from the bloodstream within a day.

Global Progress · Nepal

Nepal's homegrown trail-bridge program has helped eleven countries replace muddy river crossings with steel and concrete.

Nepal has built more than 11,000 bridges and, through its TRAC4Change initiative, has helped Ethiopia alone construct 272 of its own.

Nature · Segovia, Colombia

Researchers have found the first confirmed nest of one of Colombia's most elusive birds of prey.

After nearly three years searching the forests around Segovia, the Grandes Rapaces Colombia Project located an active ornate hawk-eagle nest.

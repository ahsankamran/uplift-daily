Nature · KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa

Six rhinos hand-raised after poachers killed their mothers have each given birth in the wild.

Ezemvelo KZN Wildlife rescued the six white rhino calves around 2017 from reserves across KwaZulu-Natal, then spent four to five years rehabilitating them before releasing them back onto protected land. All six have since bred successfully in the wild, the conservation body confirmed this week. Game capture manager Dumisani Zwane said the team always raised the animals with the goal that they would "go back at some point and become a wild rhino that will contribute to the wild population."

Science & Medicine · Basque Country, Spain

A twice-yearly stool test has cut colon cancer deaths in half across the Basque Country.

A screening programme launched in the Basque Country in 2009 lowered colorectal cancer mortality among people 50 to 69 from 39.7 to 19.8 deaths per 100,000, a 21-year study of nearly 2.2 million people found. The programme offers a take-home stool test every two years, and it caught 71 percent of tumors at the earliest, most treatable stages. Researchers led by Dr. Luis Bujanda published the results in JAMA Network Open, giving other regions a two-decade case for adopting the same low-cost test.

Global Progress · Indonesia

Indonesia's poverty rate fell to its lowest level in years.

Indonesia's poverty rate dropped to 8.07 percent in March 2026, down from 8.25 percent six months earlier, Statistics Indonesia reported this week. The shift meant 430,000 fewer people living below the poverty line of roughly $37 a month, part of a decline that has held steady since March 2023, according to agency deputy Edy Mahmud. Rural poverty still runs nearly double the urban rate, at 10.67 percent versus 6.34 percent, showing where the next gains will need to come from.

Quiet Kindness · York, Maine

A Maine nonprofit is reviving York's only free ride service for seniors.

Heart to Heart, a volunteer service that drove York, Maine's older residents to medical appointments and grocery runs for five years, shut down July 15 when its founder stepped back to spend time with family. The York Community Service Association is now relaunching it as YCSA Ride, recruiting the program's 35 former volunteer drivers and using its model to fill the gap. The original service ran on about $8,000 a year in donations from the local Rotary Club, area churches and residents, and organizers expect the new version to run the same way.

Small Wonders · Alberta, Canada

Three Labradors are sniffing out invasive mussels before they reach Alberta's lakes.

Hilo, Jane and Winnie, three black Labradors, have spent more than a decade working boat inspection stations across Alberta as part of the province's Conservation Canine program. Their noses catch zebra and golden mussel larvae too small to see, which program leader Cindy Sawchuk says gives inspectors "a better idea about what could be on that boat." Similar mussel infestations cost neighboring provinces roughly $75 million a year to manage, so each detection keeps a lake clear.

Everyday Joy · Greater Manchester, England

Millie the cat turned 31 this year on a diet of spring water and salmon.

Millie, a tortoiseshell adopted as a kitten in Greater Manchester in 1995, marked her 31st birthday this year with a plate of chicken. Her owner, 71-year-old Leslie Greenhough, credits a decade of drinking only Buxton spring water alongside salmon, prawns and tuna, saying "she is a real trooper." In human years she's roughly 146, though the only proof of her age is a single 1995 photograph, since no formal birth record exists to make it official.

Nature · Torricelli Mountains, Papua New Guinea

266 clan leaders signed a deed protecting 125,000 hectares of tree kangaroo habitat in Papua New Guinea.

Leaders from 41 villages in the Torricelli Mountains signed a legally binding conservation deed banning commercial logging, mining and hunting of threatened species across habitat for three types of tree kangaroo found nowhere else.

Global Progress · Cambodia

Cambodia became the first country in Asia and the Pacific to hit the UN's 95-95-95 HIV targets.

Ninety-five percent of Cambodians living with HIV now know their status, 95 percent of those diagnosed are on treatment, and 95 percent of those treated have an undetectable viral load, UNAIDS confirmed.

Science & Medicine · Europe

A retinal implant that restores reading vision won approval for sale across Europe.

Science Corporation's PRIMA implant received CE marking to treat geographic atrophy, with trial patients gaining an average of more than five lines on a standard eye chart.

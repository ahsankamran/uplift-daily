Everyday Joy · Venice, Italy

Venice renews its thousand-year pact with the Adriatic today, as the mayor casts a gold ring into the sea from the ceremonial barge of the Serenissima.

On the Sunday of Ascension week since at least the year 1000, Venice has sailed its official barge out to the Lido and thrown a ring into the sea with the Latin words Desponsamus te, mare — "We wed thee, sea" — renewing the city's bond with the Adriatic. The ceremony began when Doge Pietro II Orseolo sailed east to protect Dalmatian coastal communities, and was solemnised in 1177 when Pope Alexander III gave a ring to the Doge as thanks for hosting the peace between papacy and empire. After the ring lands, traditional rowing boats in the colours of Venice's six neighbourhoods circle the Lido in a regatta before the day closes with a blessing at the church of San Nicolò al Lido.

Global Progress · Ghana, Kenya & Malawi

Four years of real-world data show the RTS,S malaria vaccine prevented one in eight deaths among vaccinated children in Ghana, Kenya, and Malawi — and the jab is now available in 25 countries.

A Lancet study published 8 May 2026 tracked children in the first three African countries to introduce RTS,S through national immunisation programmes and found all-cause child mortality fell 13 percent in vaccinated populations over four years. The protective effect held even when only 71 percent of children received three doses and 40 percent a fourth, resolving earlier concerns that near-total coverage was essential for meaningful protection. Twenty-five African nations now offer the vaccine in their childhood schedules, targeting over ten million children each year against a disease that still kills an estimated 438,000 African children annually.

Science & Medicine · Montréal, Canada

A molecular 'glycerol pocket' in brown fat that triggers calorie burning turns out to govern bone mineralisation too, opening paths to new treatments for metabolic disease and fragile-bone conditions.

A team led by Lawrence Kazak at McGill University's Goodman Cancer Institute, publishing in Nature on 12 May 2026, identified the trigger for brown fat's 'futile creatine cycle' — an alternative heat-generating pathway — as glycerol, which binds to an enzyme called TNAP in a region they named the glycerol pocket. The same TNAP enzyme governs bone calcification, and mutations that impair it cause hypophosphatasia, a disorder in which bones fail to harden normally. Researchers have already catalogued dozens of candidate compounds that bind the glycerol pocket, suggesting a single drug class might eventually address both metabolic and skeletal conditions at once.

Nature · North Sea, Netherlands

A Dutch court orders that bottom trawlers must obtain permits before dragging the Dogger Bank, the nursery of the North Sea — the first European ruling to force regulation of trawling in a marine protected area.

On 12 May, the District Court of The Hague found that the Dutch Ministry of Agriculture had unlawfully granted a blanket exemption allowing industrial trawlers to work the Dogger Bank Natura 2000 zone — a shallow North Sea sandbank that serves as spawning and nursery ground for cod, plaice, and sole — without environmental assessments. The case was brought by four NGOs including ClientEarth and Blue Marine Foundation, and the ruling requires each vessel to obtain an individual permit and habitat assessment before entering the protected area. Legal challenges to bottom trawling in marine protected areas are now active in France, Spain, Italy, Sweden, and Germany.

Small Wonders · Fiordland, New Zealand

New Zealand's rangers tunnelled through the back wall of a clifftop nest to rescue a kākāpō chick perched above a 60-metre drop, lifting the 2026 season's record total to 106 hatchings.

On Chalky Island in Fiordland, a kākāpō named Rimu was found nesting in an underground burrow whose only entrance sat on a ledge at the edge of a 60-metre cliff — too dangerous for rangers to approach safely. The Department of Conservation team dug a new access shaft through the back wall, confirmed the chick was healthy, blocked the hazardous entrance, and within a night both Rimu and her chick were using the safe route. The 2026 season, driven by a rare bumper crop of rimu berries, now stands at 106 hatchings — well past the previous record of 85 set in 2019.

Quiet Kindness · Vaal Region, South Africa

An SPCA inspector bound for an animal rescue in South Africa's Vaal region found her vehicle trapped in deep mud — and a stranger named Sean arrived with a tractor before she could ask.

Published in the Vaalweekblad on 13 May 2026, the account describes how a Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals inspector became mired during a rescue call, only for a local resident named Sean Chesworth to appear unbidden, organise his staff and drive a tractor to pull the vehicle free — asking nothing in return. The inspector wrote to the paper to thank Sean specifically by name, noting that his response was immediate and unconditional. Her letter closed with the observation that the moment reminded her that "humanity and compassion are still very much alive" in her community.

Everyday Joy · Yamanashi Prefecture, Japan

Eight hundred thousand stalks of ground-hugging moss-phlox bloom in stripes of pink, white, and mauve across a hillside facing Mount Fuji, through May 24.

At Fuji Motosuko Resort, 800,000 stalks of shibazakura are at peak bloom across a hillside facing Mount Fuji directly, creating one of Japan's most photographed spring spectacles. The 19th annual Shibazakura Festival features five cultivar varieties planted in distinct bands, so the slope appears draped in alternating ribbons of deep rose, pale pink, white, and mauve.

Nature · Western North Atlantic

North Atlantic right whales produced 23 calves in the 2025–26 season — the highest count in seventeen years — with two first-time mothers still in their tenth year.

NOAA Fisheries confirmed the most right whale calves since 2009, with 13 returning mothers on a 3-to-4-year interval close to the species' healthy norm. Two first-time mothers were each just ten years old, suggesting younger females are now entering the breeding population.

Global Progress · India

India added 55.3 gigawatts of clean electricity in a single year — nearly double its previous record — pushing its non-fossil capacity past 283 gigawatts and five years ahead of its Paris Agreement target.

India's Ministry of New and Renewable Energy confirmed 55.3 GW of non-fossil power installed in the financial year ending March 2026, with solar accounting for 44.6 GW — 31 percent above the annual target. The milestone puts India at 283 GW of renewable and nuclear capacity, just past 50 percent of the national grid for the first time.

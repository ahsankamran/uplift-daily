Nature · Lugela District, Mozambique

Mozambique granted permanent protection to a mountain rainforest first spotted on Google Earth.

On August 24, Mozambique's government published a decree creating the 9,341-hectare Mount Mabu Community Conservation Area, safeguarding one of southern Africa's largest surviving medium-altitude forest blocks. Ecologist Julian Bayliss, who located the forest on satellite imagery in 2005 and led survey expeditions there afterward, worked with four local communities — Nvava, Namadoe, Limbue and Nangaze — who now hold formal stewardship of land they have safeguarded for generations. More than 30 species new to science have turned up in the forest since its discovery, including a chameleon and a forest viper, and a community management plan is now being finalized to guide its future use.

Global Progress · The Gambia

A catch-up campaign delivered hundreds of thousands of missed vaccine doses to Gambian children.

Between January 2025 and March 2026, The Gambia's Ministry of Health ran six nationwide rounds of intensified immunization, delivering more than 103,000 pentavalent, 76,000 measles-rubella and 108,000 oral polio vaccine doses to children who had missed routine shots during and after the pandemic. With support from Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, the campaign also pushed HPV vaccine coverage to nearly 109 percent of its 2026 target by reaching girls from earlier missed cohorts, while WHO monitors visited more than 4,300 households to confirm children were reached. "Behind every child vaccinated is a family protected from the devastating consequences of vaccine-preventable diseases," said Dr. Oumie Jagne of WHO, as the country prepares for continued support through 2030.

Science & Medicine · Sydney, Australia

Scientists confirmed for the first time that human heart muscle can regrow after a heart attack.

Researchers at the University of Sydney, the Baird Institute and Royal Prince Alfred Hospital examined living heart tissue from patients undergoing bypass surgery and found heart muscle cells re-entering cell division in areas damaged by a prior heart attack. The finding, published in Circulation Research on September 10, gives the first direct human evidence of a regenerative response previously observed only in mice, according to lead author Dr. Robert Hume. The natural repair remains far too weak to replace all the muscle a heart attack destroys, but researchers say understanding it could guide future treatments that boost the heart's own capacity to heal.

Quiet Kindness · Newark to Grand Rapids, USA

A flight attendant gave an exhausted new mother a break by holding her baby for the rest of the flight.

On a September 2 United flight from Newark to Grand Rapids, attendant Monique Harris noticed a sleeping mother's infant starting to slip from her lap and quietly took over holding him so the mother could rest. Passenger John Frehse photographed the moment and later shared that Harris keeps a habit of leaving handwritten notes for passengers — this one told the mother she was doing a great job. Harris said she was moved to tears by the response online, adding: "I never do anything for recognition."

Small Wonders · Mornington Peninsula, Australia

A diver photographed a rare, exposed moment of a blue-ringed octopus guarding her eggs.

Diving under a pier on Victoria's Mornington Peninsula, photographer Jules Casey found a female southern blue-ringed octopus carrying a clutch of eggs fully out in the open, a posture the notoriously venomous, reclusive species almost never takes. Casey watched the octopus hold the exposed position for at least two hours before returning later to find she had moved on, and the image, titled Deadly Devotion, won silver in the Australian category of the Underwater Awards Australasia 2026. Females carrying eggs typically wall themselves into a den and stop eating until they hatch, making the sighting an unusually intimate glimpse of the species tending its young.

Everyday Joy · Taormina, Sicily

A Sicilian hotel gave a child's lost stuffed bear a five-star vacation before mailing it home.

When a young girl named Julia left her bear, Rosie, behind at the San Domenico Palace in Taormina, staff spent days photographing the toy lounging by the pool, at the bar and tucked into a hotel bed. They mailed Rosie home with a handwritten letter written in the bear's voice, describing sunny mornings, poolside naps and hotel breakfasts, with the hotel covering the shipping itself. Julia's older sister posted the photos on TikTok, where the bear's holiday drew millions of views from people charmed by the trouble staff took over one small guest.

Science & Medicine · United States

A cell therapy slowed heart and muscle decline in boys with advanced Duchenne muscular dystrophy, a trial found.

In a Phase 3 trial of 106 boys and young men with advanced Duchenne muscular dystrophy at 20 sites across the United States, quarterly infusions of the cell therapy deramiocel slowed the decline of upper-limb muscle function by 54 percent and eased heart-muscle scarring by 91 percent compared with placebo, results published in The Lancet show. The therapy uses cardiosphere-derived cells grown from donated heart tissue to calm the inflammation that damages both skeletal and heart muscle, rather than targeting a single genetic mutation. "Seeing evidence of benefit in both areas is a game changer," said lead investigator Craig McDonald of UC Davis Health, as the therapy's approval application undergoes review by the FDA.

Nature · Corrientes, Argentina

Giant anteaters have spread into Brazil from a population that started at zero in Argentina.

Fundación Rewilding Argentina's giant anteater reintroduction in the Iberá Wetlands has grown from zero animals in 2007 to roughly 200, after releasing 110 rescued and rehabilitated anteaters over nineteen years. Camera traps in Brazil's Espinilho State Park recently caught one of the wetlands' anteaters more than 100 kilometers from Iberá, the species' first confirmed return to that part of Rio Grande do Sul since it went locally extinct over a century ago. The foundation says the same rescue-and-release approach is now being adapted to bring back other missing predators and herbivores across the wetlands.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Global hunger fell below 8 percent for the first time since the pandemic, the UN reports.

The share of the world's population that is malnourished fell to 7.8 percent in 2026, the UN Food and Agriculture Organization reported, the lowest level recorded since before the pandemic disrupted food systems worldwide. The improvement was driven largely by a steep five-year decline in Central Asia, where malnourishment fell from 7.7 percent to 2.8 percent, and in Southeast Asia, where it dropped from 11.6 percent to 5.1 percent. Africa remains the most undernourished continent, with some regions above 20 percent, but the FAO projects the global rate will keep falling toward the lowest level ever recorded by the end of the decade.

Quiet Kindness · Wrexham, Wales

A Welsh care home funded a choir's trip to Pavarotti's birthplace, and his widow sent a thank-you gift.

Pendine Park care home in Wrexham funded the Froncysyllte Male Voice Choir's trip to Modena, Italy, where the singers performed in Welsh, Italian and French at the Teatro Comunale Pavarotti-Freni and presented traditional Welsh love spoons to the mayor and to Luciano Pavarotti's daughter, Alice. Weeks later, Pavarotti's widow, Nicoletta Mantovani, sent Pendine Park owners Mario and Gill Kreft a box of Italian pastries as thanks, which the home's kitchen shared with staff. "To have subsequently received the thank-you gift of confectionaries from Nicoletta was a wonderful surprise," Mario said, continuing a friendship with the Llangollen Eisteddfod that began when Pavarotti first sang there in 1955, aged nineteen.

Small Wonders · Tainan, Taiwan

Bamboo bridges built by volunteers have helped double a migratory crab population in Taiwan.

Volunteers and staff at Taijiang National Park built small bamboo bridges over new drainage channels and temporarily closed a coastal road, addressing man-made obstacles that were stranding migrating mangrove land crabs each July. The number of crabs observed completing their egg-laying migration to the beach rose from about 5,000 a year to more than 10,000 last year, said park director Chen Jun-shan. Protecting the crabs, he said, helps protect the coastal forest belt they depend on, since the two habitats are closely linked.

Everyday Joy · Liverpool, England

A Beatles tour bus pulled up beside Paul McCartney while he was FaceTiming Ringo Starr.

A Magical Mystery Tour bus stopped at a red light on Hope Street in Liverpool on July 20 and its passengers spotted Paul McCartney in the next car, phone in hand, mid-video call with his former bandmate Ringo Starr. "I had pulled up at the traffic lights on Hope Street when who should I see? Sir Paul with Ringo on FaceTime," said tour guide Neil Morton, as McCartney leaned out the window to wave and held up the phone so passengers could see Starr too. McCartney was in town to present a companionship of the Liverpool Institute for Performing Arts to actor Stephen Graham, a detour that turned into a two-Beatle cameo for one busload of tourists.

Nature · Nepal

Nepal's tiger population has more than tripled since 2009, a new census shows.

Nepal's latest national tiger census counted 429 tigers, up from 355 in 2022 and just 121 in 2009, with camera traps across five lowland reserves finding increases in every park but Bardiya.

Science & Medicine · Finland

A single sauna session sent every kind of white blood cell surging, Finnish researchers found.

A 30-minute sauna session, with a brief cold shower at the midpoint, increased every type of white blood cell circulating in the bloodstream of 51 adults studied, researchers found.

Global Progress · China

Coal generated less than half of China's electricity for the first time this year.

Coal-fired power made up 49.7 percent of China's electricity output in the first half of 2026, dropping below half for the first time, the National Energy Administration reported.

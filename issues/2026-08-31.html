Global Progress · Costa Rica

Costa Rica's lawmakers made wildlife crossings mandatory on roads that cut through animal habitat.

Costa Rica's legislature voted unanimously to require new and existing roads that disrupt wildlife movement to include crossings — bridges, culverts, tunnels or rope lines for tree-dwelling animals — whenever data shows a road is cutting off a species' path. At least 59 species, among them monkeys, sloths and kinkajous, have already been recorded using the country's existing wildlife bridges, and conservationists say any scientist or citizen can now submit data to request a new one. Once the president signs the bill, the National Environmental Technical Secretariat will vet new infrastructure at the design stage, reconnecting habitat before roads are built rather than patching the damage after animals start dying on them.

Small Wonders · Christmas and Cocos Islands, Australia

Scientists surveying remote Indian Ocean seamounts came home with 149 species new to science.

Researchers from Australia, New Zealand, Canada and the US spent weeks trawling unexplored seamounts near Christmas and Cocos Islands, lowering instruments to 5,431 meters and surfacing with 149 previously unknown species, among them a sea cucumber, a greeneye fish and a parasitic barnacle. At one site they nicknamed the "Shark Tooth Graveyard," the team found roughly 1,000 fossil shark teeth spanning more than 22 million years. The findings, published in Deep-Sea Research Part II, fill what the researchers called a massive hole in the data on how life persists in the ocean's most isolated corners.

Science & Medicine · São Paulo State, Brazil

A daily fish-oil and aspirin combination worked as well as antibiotics against severe gum disease.

In a year-long trial across four Brazilian universities and Harvard, patients with severe periodontitis who took three grams of omega-3 fish oil plus low-dose aspirin each day reached the same treatment goal as those given antibiotics, with close to 58 percent of each group down to four or fewer deep gum pockets. Patients who got only a placebo alongside standard cleaning reached that target just 23 percent of the time, underscoring how much the added therapy mattered. Researchers say the combination could give dentists an antibiotic-free option for a disease that, left untreated, contributes to tooth loss and heart and diabetes complications.

Nature · Sabaragamuwa Province, Sri Lanka

A plant unseen in Sri Lanka since 1855 was found growing wild again.

In January 2025, researchers located a population of 70 to 100 Indianthus virgatus plants in a lowland forest fragment along the Sitawaka Ganga river in Sabaragamuwa Province, the first record of the species in Sri Lanka in 170 years. The team combined historical botanical records from India and Sri Lanka with predictive habitat modeling to narrow down where the small white-flowered forest plant might have survived undetected. Most of its likely habitat has since been cleared for tea and rubber plantations, so the find gives conservationists a rare second chance to protect a species once presumed gone from the country.

Quiet Kindness · Broward County, Florida

Two siblings built an app that has turned unsold pizza into 25,000 free slices for hungry neighbors.

Aashrita Kaza, then an eighth grader in Broward County, and her brother Anish designed an app connecting local restaurants and donors with families facing food insecurity, after the pair noticed the need while volunteering at local charities. In the two years since, the free app has helped coordinate roughly 25,000 pizza slices delivered across South Florida. "Their faces just light up the second they get the pizza," Aashrita said. "It's very rewarding because it makes everything we do worth it."

Everyday Joy · Lightwater, Surrey, England

The world's oldest person marked her 117th birthday with a llama and a lifelong rule against arguing.

Ethel Caterham, born in 1909 and recognized by Guinness World Records as the oldest living person, celebrated her 117th birthday on August 21 at her care home in Lightwater, Surrey — the first British person on record to reach the age. She has outlived her husband, a British Army major she met while working as a nanny in India, by fifty years, and now spends her days with three grandchildren and five great-grandchildren. "Even at 117, I've had many firsts this year, from stroking a llama to holding hands with the King," she said, crediting her longevity to a habit of never arguing, though rarely compromising either.

Nature · Bodham, North Norfolk, England

A Norfolk field left alone for a decade grew itself a meadow full of orchids.

Eleven years after its last crop, a retired Norfolk field left to grow wild and cut once a year for hay nearly doubled its plant species and filled with thousands of orchids.

Global Progress · Niger

Niger became the first African country to eliminate river blindness as a public health problem.

Health ministers announced the milestone in Addis Ababa, capping decades of mass drug distribution against the parasitic worm that causes onchocerciasis.

Science & Medicine · Melbourne, Australia

A trial of nearly 10,000 healthy older adults found statins cut their risk of a first heart attack or stroke by 30 percent.

The STAREE trial followed adults 70 and older for six years and found daily statins cut major cardiovascular events by nearly a third.

Nature · Laikipia, Kenya

Four mountain bongos from a Czech zoo have touched Kenyan soil, joining efforts to restore a species down to fewer than fifty individuals in the wild.

Four male mountain bongos raised at Safari Park Dvůr Králové in the Czech Republic landed at the Mount Kenya Wildlife Conservancy near Nanyuki in late April, where over 100 managed animals are being readied for eventual forest release. Fewer than 50 mountain bongos survive in Kenya's wild today; the first ten were reintroduced to Mount Kenya forest in 2022 — the species' first presence there since 1994. Kenya's national strategy targets 750 wild individuals by 2075, requiring sustained breeding partnerships like this one with Chester Zoo and the European Association of Zoos and Aquaria.

Everyday Joy · Nasiriyah, Iraq

Conservators in southern Iraq are hand-making mud bricks to restore the 4,000-year-old Ziggurat of Ur, matching the original clay chemistry one brick at a time.

A $382,000 conservation project at the Ziggurat of Ur near Nasiriyah has begun producing handmade mudbrick on-site; lead archaeologist Khadim Hassoun Honaein first sampled original 2100 BCE bricks to identify their precise mineral composition. Workers source clay from soils chemically similar to ancient Ur's, then shape and sun-dry each brick by hand — the same sequence used when the ziggurat was first built under the Third Dynasty of Ur. The UNESCO World Heritage Site had not received significant conservation attention since 1999; the team expects to stabilise the outer walls within two years.

Science & Medicine · Global

An oral antiviral taken within three days of household exposure cuts the risk of developing COVID-19 by two-thirds, a Phase 3 trial found.

The SCORPIO-PEP trial, published 14 May in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled 2,377 household contacts of confirmed COVID-19 cases: 2.9 percent of those given a five-day course of ensitrelvir developed symptoms, against 9.0 percent given placebo. Among participants with at least one risk factor for severe disease, relative risk reduction rose to 76 percent, with adverse-event rates nearly identical in both groups. Ensitrelvir — developed by Osaka-based Shionogi and already approved in Japan as Xocova — has an FDA review decision scheduled for June 2026.

Global Progress · Global

New HIV infections have fallen 40 percent worldwide since 2010, with the African region recording a 70 percent drop in AIDS-related deaths since the 2005 peak.

The World Health Statistics 2026 report, published 13 May by the WHO, documents a 40 percent global reduction in new HIV infections over fifteen years; South Africa's annual AIDS-related death toll dropped from roughly 280,000 in 2005 to 53,000 in 2024. The same report found 567 million more people globally now covered by essential health services without catastrophic spending compared to the 2018 baseline.

Small Wonders · Valdivia, Chile

A 3.5 mm wasp that spent 40 years unsorted in a museum drawer turned out to be an entirely new genus — named after David Attenborough on his 100th birthday.

A parasitic wasp collected in Chile's Valdivia province in 1983 was identified by Natural History Museum volunteer Augustijn De Ketelaere and curator Gavin Broad as belonging outside any known genus, distinguished by a T-shaped abdominal marking and toothlike ovipositor structures. Named Attenboroughnculus tau in Zootaxa on 8 May — Sir David's 100th birthday — Attenborough replied with a handwritten note. The species joins only three other known members of the Darwin wasp subfamily Pedunculinae.

Quiet Kindness · Moncton, Canada

A retired gym teacher in Moncton has spent 14 years collecting and repairing donated bicycles so no child who cannot afford one goes without.

Krista Richard runs "Bikes and Trikes for Everyone" in Moncton, New Brunswick, distributing free bicycles on Sunday mornings from April to October; she gave away 88 in a single day last season — her personal record — from a waiting list of roughly 400 children. She and a small team collect donations year-round and repair each bike by hand before passing it on. This spring she expanded the programme to include adult bicycles so parents can ride alongside their children.

Nature · Eastern Mongolia

Hundreds of wild asses are grazing in eastern Mongolia again after a sixty-five-year absence.

Wildlife Conservation Society researchers documented 384 khulan east of the Trans-Mongolian Railway in October 2024, four years after the species first crossed back into its historical range. Removing 1.5 km of barrier fencing and formalising a safe-passage zone allowed the herd to reclaim more than 4,000 km² of steppe.

Quiet Kindness · Broken Hill, Australia

Five hundred freshwater turtles are swimming again after an Australian community raised A$100,000 to keep their lake alive.

Landcare Broken Hill launched a crowdfunding campaign that raised over A$100,000 in days, enough to truck in water until record rains arrived at the end of February. The turtles are now breeding normally and a reserve water fund has been set aside for the next dry season.

Global Progress · Tunisia

Tunisia has eliminated trachoma, ending a disease that once blinded at least half its population.

The WHO validated Tunisia on 14 May as the 31st country to eliminate trachoma as a public health problem — the first neglected tropical disease eradicated there. The global population requiring trachoma interventions has now fallen below 100 million for the first time.

Nature · Kyrgyzstan

Kyrgyzstan formalized an 800,000-hectare wildlife corridor designed around 2070 climate models, giving snow leopards space to shift as mountain habitats change.

The Ak Ilbirs ecological corridor links multiple protected areas across 14 rural municipalities in Kyrgyzstan's high-altitude ranges, covering nearly 2 million acres and projected to hold more than 60 percent of suitable habitat for snow leopards, argali sheep, and Asiatic ibex under future warming scenarios. Designed by the CAMCA initiative — led by UNEP with Humboldt University of Berlin and local partners CAMP Alatoo and Ilbirs Foundation — it does not exclude herders, but instead requires seasonal grazing bans, no-grazing buffer zones, and a minimum 40 percent vegetation cover for wildlife movement, monitored by rangers and satellite tracking. The design is unusual in being built around projected habitat as of 2070, not current habitat, giving the corridor a practical lifespan as temperatures continue to rise.

Global Progress · Atlantic Ocean

On World Sea Turtle Day, fifty organizations across 25 countries launched a coordinated alliance to protect Atlantic leatherback turtles, listed as critically endangered in part of their range.

The Wilkes Atlantic Leatherback Turtle Alliance (WALTA), led by University of Exeter professors Annette Broderick and Brendan Godley, formally launched on 16 June, uniting more than 50 partner organizations spanning 25 Atlantic-bordering nations from Suriname and Trinidad to Senegal and the Azores. The alliance will commission a fresh nesting census across all Atlantic beaches, an ocean-wide synthesis of bycatch data from industrial and artisanal fisheries — the primary cause of leatherback mortality — and a prioritized action framework targeting the most vulnerable populations first. The species is listed as "vulnerable" globally but "critically endangered" in the southwest Atlantic, where the last formal population assessment was conducted in 2013 and nesting numbers at key beaches in Brazil and French Guiana have since declined sharply.

Quiet Kindness · Orchid Island, Taiwan

Sixty Tao paddlers crossed 111 miles of open sea in a hand-built canoe to reunite with their Philippine kin after 300 years of silence.

On 15 June, a crew from Orchid Island departed in the Ovayan — a 12-metre traditional plank canoe built without nails — navigating the Bashi Channel by stars through four-metre swells before reaching Mahatao Port on Batan Island on 17 June, where roughly 400 Ivatan residents gathered at the dock. The Tao and Batanes Ivatan share Austronesian ancestry and once traded freely across the channel; a violent dispute roughly three centuries ago severed all contact, and the voyage was conceived as a gesture of reconciliation, answered with hours of shared drumming, dancing, and the Tao war-canoe ceremony. The chairman of the Orchid Island group has extended an invitation for Batan paddlers to make the return crossing to Taiwan next June, launching what both communities are calling the first exchange in living memory.

Small Wonders · Llanganates-Sangay, Ecuador

A spider found in Ecuador's Amazon disguises itself as a zombie-fungus victim to fool predators and prey alike.

Scientists at the Leibniz Institute for the Analysis of Biodiversity Change formally described Taczanowskia waska, an orb-weaver found in the Llanganates-Sangay corridor, which grows elongated pale structures from its abdomen to mimic the fruiting bodies of Gibellula — the fungus that infects and then sprouts from spider hosts. During the initial field survey the research team mistook the motionless specimen for an actual mushroom; it was first flagged by citizen-science observers on iNaturalist who were equally deceived. Named for the conservation organisation Waska Amazonia, whose naturalist guides first encountered it on a tour in 2025, the species was formally described in Zootaxa on 17 June 2026.

Everyday Joy · London, UK

A London podcast duo has been holding pub evenings where fathers learn to braid their daughters' hair and sit through a pub quiz on the menstrual cycle.

Matt Carter and Lawrence Price, hosts of The Secret Life of Dads, launched "Pints & Ponytails" in early 2026 — evenings at London pubs where roughly 35 fathers sit around tables stocked with mannequin heads and learn to plait and twist, over pints. On 7 June they partnered with menstrual-health company Fluxxbox for a "Periods & Ponytails" session at The Prince pub in West London, walking dads through a pub quiz on the menstrual cycle and circulating sanitary products for inspection. The events spread widely after public criticism from an anti-feminist influencer only multiplied requests to attend, and the hosts are now planning evenings in Manchester, Leeds, and Edinburgh.

Science & Medicine · 29 countries

A phase 3 trial of bepirovirsen achieved a functional cure in one in five chronic hepatitis B patients — the first time a finite course of treatment has cleared the surface antigen at scale.

In two randomized controlled trials — B-Well 1 and B-Well 2 — involving 1,838 adults across 29 countries, weekly injections of GSK's bepirovirsen for six months produced a functional cure in 20 and 19 percent of participants respectively, against zero in both placebo groups. A functional cure means undetectable virus and loss of surface antigen for at least six months after stopping all treatment — a threshold that standard lifelong antiviral drugs never reach, and one that allows the immune system to keep infection controlled without medication. The results, published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 28 May 2026, address a disease that chronically infects an estimated 240 million people worldwide and causes roughly 1.1 million deaths each year from liver failure and hepatocellular carcinoma.

Small Wonders · Lake Tuz, Türkiye

More than five thousand flamingo chicks have hatched at Lake Tuz in central Turkey — more than double last year's count — after a water-pumping project reversed the drought damage that wiped out entire cohorts of nestlings in 2021.

Drone footage shot on 10 June counted roughly 5,000 greater flamingo chicks at one of the Eastern Mediterranean's most important breeding sites; the directorate for natural asset protection began pumping freshwater into the nesting flats in 2022, after prolonged heat nearly dried the lake and killed thousands of young birds.

Nature · India, Nepal & Pakistan

Twenty years after India, Nepal, and Pakistan banned a cattle drug that had reduced vulture populations by 99.9%, captive-bred white-rumped vultures are now raising wild chicks of their own.

The RSPB-supported Saving Asia's Vultures from Extinction consortium has bred 740 birds across four specialised centres and released more than 110 into protected tiger reserves; the first cohort released in Haryana in 2020 is now breeding naturally without human support, and 31 birds from West Bengal have dispersed across India, Nepal, and Bhutan with zero recorded poisoning deaths.

Science & Medicine · London, UK

London's clean-air zones have cut emergency hospital admissions for heart disease at a rate of 8.1 percent per year since the scheme began — the first study to measure the effect directly against a matched control area.

Imperial College London researchers compared admissions data from inside and outside the Ultra Low Emission Zone and its predecessor the T-charge, from 2014 to 2020; cardiovascular admissions fell by 8.1% per year in the zone, respiratory disease admissions by 6.2%, and all-cause emergency admissions by 3.1%.

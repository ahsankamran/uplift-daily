Nature · Torricelli Mountains, Papua New Guinea

266 clan leaders signed a deed protecting 125,000 hectares of tree kangaroo habitat in Papua New Guinea.

Leaders from 41 villages in the Torricelli Mountains signed a legally binding conservation deed on June 30, banning commercial logging, mining and hunting of threatened species across rainforest that holds about 4 percent of the world's biodiversity, including three types of tree kangaroo found nowhere else. The Tenkile Conservation Alliance, led by Jim and Jean Thomas, helped broker the agreement, and every village's management committee must be at least 30 percent women. The alliance now plans to extend the same model to roughly two dozen villages in the nearby Bewani Mountains.

Global Progress · Cambodia

Cambodia became the first country in Asia and the Pacific to hit the UN's 95-95-95 HIV targets.

Ninety-five percent of Cambodians living with HIV now know their status, 95 percent of those diagnosed are on treatment, and 95 percent of those treated have an undetectable viral load, UNAIDS confirmed after 30 years of support to the country's national response. Much of the progress came from the nonprofit KHANA, which hired people living with HIV as peer counselors and built programs like SMARTGirl and MStyle that met nightclub workers, gay men and transgender women where they already felt safe. Cambodia now aims to cut new infections to under 250 a year by 2030, down from an estimated 958 in 2025.

Science & Medicine · Europe

A retinal implant that restores reading vision won approval for sale across Europe.

Science Corporation's PRIMA implant, a light-sensing chip paired with glasses that beam infrared light onto the retina, received CE marking to treat geographic atrophy caused by age-related macular degeneration. In a trial of 38 patients across five countries, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, recipients gained an average of more than five lines on a standard eye chart. The company expects to fit its first commercial patients in Germany within weeks, with Italy, the Netherlands and the UK to follow.

Quiet Kindness · Santa Ana, California

A stranger's viral video raised $174,000 for a Navy veteran still working the tarmac at 64.

LaCinda Thackery filmed James Blair, a 64-year-old Navy veteran and fuel mechanic, struggling to walk across the tarmac at Santa Ana's John Wayne Airport, and posted the video online asking for help. It went viral, commenters helped identify him, and Thackery worked with his employer to set up a GoFundMe that raised more than $174,000 from over 6,000 donors. Blair, who had kept working past retirement age to help cover his mother's hospice care, called Thackery "the Angel" and sent her flowers in thanks.

Small Wonders · Sabah, Malaysian Borneo

Camera traps confirmed the oldest wild Sunda clouded leopard ever recorded, still hunting at eight and a half.

Panthera and Sabah Forestry Department rangers photographed a female Sunda clouded leopard in Malaysian Borneo and confirmed she is at least 8.5 years old, the oldest wild individual of her species on record. Researchers found females are 68 percent harder to detect than males because they spend far more time in the forest canopy, and this cat has ranged up to 19 miles between reserves. The sighting gives conservationists a rare data point on how long these elusive cats live and move through Borneo's forest corridors.

Everyday Joy · Bristol, England

A 64-year-old father graduated as a nurse from the same university as his son.

John Adesoye, who grew up in a farming family in Nigeria and later worked as an electronics technician in Greece, completed a nursing apprenticeship at the University of the West of England alongside his son Daniel, who studied creative music technology there. The two spent one day a week on campus together, and John has since started a nursing job at Southmead Hospital in Bristol. He encourages other older students to follow him, saying, "if I can do it, others can too."

Nature · Invermere, British Columbia

First Nations released salmon into a stretch of Canada's Upper Columbia River blocked by dams since 1938.

The Secwépemc, Syilx-Okanagan and Ktunaxa Nations released 1,500 sockeye fry into water the Grand Coulee Dam has cut off from spawning salmon for nearly nine decades.

Global Progress · Pornainen, Finland

A small Finnish town cut its heating emissions by 70 percent with the world's largest sand battery.

Pornainen's 2,000-ton sand battery, built by Polar Night Energy, stores wind and solar power as heat for the town's district heating network.

Science & Medicine · Hong Kong, China

One 75-minute weekly workout cut belly fat about as well as three shorter sessions, a trial found.

University of Hong Kong researchers tracked 315 adults with central obesity and found once-weekly and thrice-weekly interval walking produced similar results.

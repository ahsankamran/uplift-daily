Nature · Saleh Bay, Sumbawa, Indonesia

More than half of a whale shark bay in Sumbawa is being set aside for the sharks.

West Nusa Tenggara's governor has designated 73,165 hectares of Saleh Bay for whale shark conservation, extending an existing marine protected area across more than half of the bay's 145,900 hectares and about 90% of the places the sharks are known to gather. Konservasi Indonesia has watched the bay since 2015 and has catalogued 174 individual sharks from 884 sightings, each one told apart by the pattern of spots on its back. Fishers whose lamp-lit bagan platforms draw the sharks in helped write the draft management plan, and the national fisheries ministry is expected to confirm the extension in 2027.

Quiet Kindness · Luma, Rukum East, Nepal

A 15-year-old paid for her village's new footbridge and helped build it.

Sadiksha Oli put Rs60,000 of her own money into timber, corrugated sheets and nails after the crossing over the Domai stream was carried off in June, leaving her neighbours wading. Relatives and residents worked beside the carpenters she paid, and the four-metre bridge was finished in three days. The ward office, the schools and the road to the district headquarters are reachable again on foot.

Everyday Joy · Córdoba, Spain

Three cheeses tied for best in the world, and the steadiest scores won.

At the World Cheese Awards in Córdoba on 12 November, 5,330 cheeses from 45 countries were laid out on tables and worked through round by round until fourteen super judges each carried one into a live final. A 24-month Comté from Fort des Rousses finished level on 81 points with a Swiss Gruyère and a French ewe's-milk Ossau-Iraty, and took the title on a tie-break because its marks were the closest together. It was matured by JuraFlore, a family affineur founded in 1907, whose Franck Arnaud accepted by video link and noted the firm turns 120 next year.

Small Wonders · Westport River, Massachusetts, USA

A survey meant to plan an oyster reef found one already there.

The Massachusetts Division of Marine Fisheries spent seven hours on the Westport River in October mapping where to build a 60-acre reef, and turned up stretches of riverbed already carrying hundreds of oysters per square metre. Restoration work in Chesapeake Bay treats 50 per square metre as the mark of a healthy reef, and the thickest beds here sat in water open to recreational shellfishing. In spring the team will spread old shell for larvae to settle on, the first move in a plan to rebuild 2,500 acres of reef by 2050.

Science & Medicine · An international trial

A leukaemia vaccine kept patients alive nine months longer than the alternatives.

In the phase 3 REGAL trial, adults with acute myeloid leukaemia in a second or later remission who could not go on to a stem cell transplant lived a median of 24.0 months on galinpepimut-S, against 14.7 months on whatever treatment their doctor would otherwise have chosen. The risk of death fell by 41% — a hazard ratio of 0.59, two-sided p=0.035 — in an open-label study that assigned patients to one arm or the other at random. These are topline numbers rather than a published paper, and the developer means to file for approval in the first half of 2027.

Global Progress · Guyana

Guyana has stopped passing HIV and syphilis from mother to child.

The World Health Organization has validated the country's elimination of mother-to-child transmission of both infections as public health problems, over the years 2022 to 2025. A team coordinated by the Pan American Health Organization reviewed the programme remotely in early June and then spent a week in the country; the global advisory committee cleared the dossier on 5 November and the director-general confirmed it by letter on 16 November. Guyana says hepatitis B is the next one it wants on the list, and the WHO will look again in 2030.

Global Progress · Bhutan

Bhutan has won an Earthshot Prize for keeping seven tenths of itself under forest.

The country took the Protect and Restore Nature category in Mumbai, and the £1 million goes to bringing 50,000 hectares back by 2030.

Quiet Kindness · Yueqing, Zhejiang, China

Five siblings cut 430 steps into a mountain so their mother could go home.

They hauled stone and mixed cement on the slope between August and October, building a stair that turns eleven times on its way up.

Nature · Tayside and Forth, Scotland

Scotland now has up to 405 beaver territories, against 39 in 2012.

Surveyors walked 2,397km of watercourses across the Tay and Forth and confirmed 349 territories, with 56 more classed as possible.

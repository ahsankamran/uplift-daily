Nature · Xingu Corridor, Pará, Brazil

A 42.8-kilometre illegal road that once threatened to split Brazil's Xingu Socioenvironmental Corridor has vanished, swallowed by regrowing rainforest between 2022 and 2025.

Brazilian agencies ICMBio and IBAMA built permanent control posts blocking the route in 2023, after the civil society groups Instituto Socioambiental and Rede Xingu+ pressed the federal government to act, and the illegal gold mine the road once served has shown no activity since. The corridor protects 26 million hectares of Indigenous territories and conservation units, and researchers say forest regrowth this complete over such a distance is rarely documented in the Amazon.

Science & Medicine · Portland, Oregon, United States

Immunologists at Oregon Health & Science University have identified 75 malaria protein fragments that stay nearly identical across parasite species and infection stages, a blueprint for one vaccine effective against malaria worldwide.

The fragments are presented to the immune system by HLA-E, a molecule that varies little from person to person, and two of them triggered protective T-cell immunity when tested in rodents, lead researcher Brandon Wilder and colleagues reported in Nature. Unlike current malaria vaccines, which need annual boosters, one built on these targets could protect for years once it clears human trials.

Global Progress · Eastern Cape, South Africa

South Africa's Department of Basic Education has replaced every pit toilet identified in a 2018 nationwide school safety audit, finishing repairs at all 3,372 schools on the list.

More than 3 million learners and 48,000 teachers now use the upgraded sanitation, which Minister Siviwe Gwarube called "a remarkable national achievement" during a visit to the Eastern Cape this week. She cautioned that broader school infrastructure needs, estimated at 120 billion rand nationwide, remain.

Quiet Kindness · Diepsloot, South Africa

A kindness shown to Karabo Legodi years ago has grown into Valley Football Club, where more than 200 children in Diepsloot, outside Johannesburg, now play every weekend.

Legodi fully sponsors more than 100 of those players, covering the kit, transport and meals that would otherwise keep a child off the pitch. Every weekend, kids from different backgrounds share one jersey and chase one ball on a patch of ground outside Johannesburg.

Small Wonders · Sesoko Island, Okinawa, Japan

A swimming crab survived about two months trapped inside a floating plastic bottle off Okinawa, growing too large to escape the very shelter that fed it.

Hiroshima University researchers Hajime Sato and Yoichi Sakai found the crab wedged inside a bottle with a 24-millimetre opening, its shell nearly four times that width, after it had eaten the small fish and algae that drifted in behind it. The case, published in the journal Ecosphere, shows how a single piece of ocean litter can become its own accidental world.

Everyday Joy · Basel, Switzerland

Record heat at Switzerland's national yodeling festival sent singers and alphorn players to rehearse in Basel's public fountains instead of its streets.

About 12,000 performers and 200,000 visitors came to the city for the Eidgenössisches Jodlerfest, its first time in Basel since 1924, as temperatures reached 39 degrees Celsius. Diners in nearby restaurants joined the impromptu bursts of yodeling, the first national festival since the tradition earned UNESCO cultural-heritage status in December.

Nature · Pendjari National Park, Benin

Camera traps in Benin's Pendjari National Park have recorded West African leopard density more than tripling, from 0.62 to 2.08 animals per 100 square kilometres between 2017 and 2023.

Panthera researcher Marine Drouilly credited the rebound to a decade of restoring prey species like hartebeest and kob across the 27,000-square-kilometre W-Arly-Pendjari Complex.

Quiet Kindness · Chicago, Illinois, United States

Sister Stephanie Baliga and her team have run the Chicago Marathon every fall since 2011, raising more than $2.6 million for the Mission of Our Lady of the Angels food pantry in West Humboldt Park.

A former Division I runner whose Olympic hopes ended with a fractured foot, Baliga now leads nearly 200 runners under the name Team OLA.

Science & Medicine · Bergen, Norway

A twelve-hospital trial across Norway and Sweden has found that rituximab controls newly diagnosed multiple sclerosis just as effectively as the far costlier standard drug, ocrelizumab.

The OVERLORD-MS trial, led by Øivind Torkildsen of the University of Bergen, followed 216 patients for 30 months and was funded entirely through Norway's public research program.

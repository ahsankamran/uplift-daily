Global Progress · Bhutan

Bhutan became the first South-East Asian country to eliminate dog-transmitted rabies.

The World Health Organization validated on September 4 that Bhutan has eliminated dog-transmitted human rabies as a public health problem, the first country in its South-East Asia region to do so. The result followed more than a decade of free post-exposure treatment across all 20 districts, mass dog vaccination and door-to-door surveillance run with health workers and De-suung volunteers, and the country has recorded zero human deaths from the disease since June 2023. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus congratulated Bhutan on September 7, calling it "another step towards our goal to end human deaths from dog-transmitted rabies by 2030."

Nature · New Zealand

New Zealand's kākāpō population passed 300 for the first time in roughly 75 years.

New Zealand's Department of Conservation confirmed on September 1 that the kākāpō population has reached 325 birds, after 90 chicks from this year's breeding season were added once each reached 150 days old and could be counted as independent. The species began its modern recovery in 1995 with just 51 birds, including only 20 females, and is now managed across three predator-free island sanctuaries in Fiordland and off Stewart Island. This year's breeding season alone produced as many chicks as the programme managed in its first sixteen years combined, and researchers now expect a shorter wait before the next big breeding year.

Everyday Joy · Bagnolo Cremasco, Italy

An Italian scrapyard worker built the world's narrowest drivable car out of a Fiat Panda.

Guinness World Records confirmed this week that Andrea Marazzi's hand-built car, made from a 1993 Fiat Panda in his family's scrapyard in Bagnolo Cremasco, is the narrowest drivable car in the world at 50.20 centimetres wide. Marazzi spent about a year cutting the Panda down to a single seat, using his own chest width as his measuring guide, and kept most of the original parts — wheels, doors, roof and seats — intact. He says he only set out to make a viral video and was startled when Guinness came calling, and he already has other quiet projects in mind.

Quiet Kindness · St. Stephen, New Brunswick, Canada

A small New Brunswick town turned neighbourly favours into a kind of currency.

After breaking both wrists and needing help around her house, Lorna Jones started the Charlotte County Time Bank in St. Stephen last year, where members earn one credit for every hour spent helping another member and redeem those credits for help of their own. Board member Sheila Brooks says people have used their credits for rides to medical appointments, skateboarding lessons, board games and simple company for those who live alone. The group is now recruiting beyond St. Stephen, and Jones hopes to see time banking spread across the province.

Small Wonders · Silverlake, Dorset, England

A rare Channel-crossing butterfly turned up nectaring at a Dorset nature reserve.

On September 6, naturalist Chris Rowland spotted a Queen of Spain fritillary — a butterfly that only occasionally strays across the English Channel — feeding on fleabane near the car park at Silverlake in Dorset. The sighting came during an unusually busy week for continental migrants there, with large numbers of clouded yellows and at least one painted lady also passing through the same patch of flowers. The species has never bred consistently in Britain, so each appearance depends on the right winds carrying a few stragglers over from mainland Europe.

Science & Medicine · Houston, Texas, USA

A compound found in magic mushrooms prevented chemotherapy nerve damage in a new lab study.

Researchers at MD Anderson Cancer Center found that giving psilocybin before chemotherapy protected mice from the cold sensitivity and nerve fiber damage that drugs like cisplatin typically cause, with as few as two doses providing protection that held up over six chemo cycles. The team, led by Moran Amit and Patrick Dougherty, traced the effect to a receptor that keeps a cell's internal transport system moving inside nerve fibers, confirming the mechanism in nerve tissue donated by 29 surgical patients. A non-hallucinogenic version of the compound worked just as well, and the researchers are now planning to test the approach directly in cancer patients.

Nature · Lugela District, Mozambique

Mozambique granted permanent protection to a mountain rainforest first spotted on Google Earth.

A decree created the 9,341-hectare Mount Mabu Community Conservation Area, giving four local communities formal stewardship of the forest.

Global Progress · The Gambia

A catch-up campaign delivered hundreds of thousands of missed vaccine doses to Gambian children.

Six nationwide immunization rounds reached children who had missed routine shots during and after the pandemic.

Science & Medicine · Sydney, Australia

Scientists confirmed for the first time that human heart muscle can regrow after a heart attack.

Living heart tissue from bypass patients showed muscle cells re-entering division in areas damaged by a prior heart attack.

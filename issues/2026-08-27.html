Everyday Joy · Buñol, Spain

Buñol turned red again for its 79th tomato fight.

On August 26, some 20,000 people packed the streets of the Spanish town of Buñol and hurled roughly 150,000 kilograms of overripe tomatoes at each other for exactly one hour. The crowd outnumbered Buñol's own population of 9,000 more than two to one, with about four in ten participants arriving from outside Spain for the 79th running of La Tomatina. Fire trucks hose down the plaza afterward, and by evening the only trace left is the smell of tomato lingering in the drains.

Nature · Punjab, Pakistan

Pakistan links two dolphin sanctuaries into 249 unbroken miles of protected river.

Punjab's newly created Punjnad Indus River Dolphin Sanctuary now connects with an existing reserve in Sindh province, placing roughly 249 miles of the Indus River — about the length of New York state — under continuous legal protection. An estimated 440 endangered Indus River dolphins live along the newly linked stretch, part of a total population of around 2,000 that has dwindled as dams and irrigation canals fragmented their habitat. Conservationists with WWF Pakistan, who have worked on the dolphin's recovery since the 1990s, say protecting an apex predator like this should help stabilize the whole river ecosystem it depends on.

Science & Medicine · Southampton, England

A drought-surviving plant's trick could take vaccines off the fridge for good.

In a Phase 1 trial at the University of Southampton, a tetanus-diphtheria vaccine stabilized with trehalose — a sugar produced by drought-tolerant "resurrection plants" — produced protective antibody levels in all 60 adult participants within 28 days. The powdered vaccine, developed by Stablepharma, stays potent for at least two years at temperatures up to 30°C, removing the need to keep it cold from factory to patient. The World Health Organization estimates roughly half of all vaccine doses worldwide go to waste because of breaks in the cold chain, and a larger Phase 2b trial has now begun.

Quiet Kindness · Ross Valley, California

A donor who has no children in the district gave its teachers $1 million anyway.

On August 13, Maja Kristin surprised about 100 teachers in California's Ross Valley School District with checks for $9,161.70 each, totaling $1 million. Kristin, who has no children in the district, said she stepped in after learning its teachers were among the lowest-paid in wealthy Marin County; "We as citizens, we can write direct checks," she told the gathered staff. Ross Valley sits in the bottom 20th percentile for state school funding despite its affluent surroundings, and teacher Amber Bunnell-Wild called it a gift from "someone I don't even know."

Small Wonders · Victoria, Australia

This orchid spends most of the year as a single leaf, waiting for one particular bee.

In July, volunteers and scientists at Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria planted lab-raised rosella spider orchids into the wild, the payoff of three years of propagation work. The orchid survives underground for most of the year, then sends up a single purple flower each August built to attract exactly one native bee species, Leioproctus platycephalus. Only four wild populations remain, together smaller than a Melbourne suburb, but the garden's nursery now holds roughly twice as many orchids as scientists estimate exist in the wild.

Global Progress · Uganda

A toilet designed for astronauts found its real job on Earth instead.

Engineer Diana Yousef's waterless "iThrone," first conceived for NASA spacecraft, has now reached 20,000 households across Uganda, Panama and the United States. The toilet evaporates 95 percent of the water in human waste without plumbing or sewage lines, addressing a problem astronauts share with the roughly 1 billion people worldwide who live beyond the reach of a sewer system. Yousef's company, change:WATER, won a $100,000 Global Citizen Waislitz Award this month to help scale the device to millions more.

Nature · Raja Ampat, Indonesia

Eighty-two zebra shark pups, raised in aquariums on four continents, have been released into an Indonesian marine sanctuary.

The ReShark consortium — 60 aquariums across 15 countries — bred zebra sharks in captivity, then released 82 pups into a marine protected area around Raja Ampat, Indonesia, each fitted with a satellite tag.

Global Progress · Oromia Region, Ethiopia

Ethiopian vegetable farmers who traded diesel pumps for solar power are saving nearly $6,000 a year in fuel.

A cooperative of 25 vegetable growers in Ethiopia's Oromia region swapped diesel pumps for solar-powered irrigation, cutting fuel bills and smoke over the fields.

Everyday Joy · Pflugdorf-Stadl, Bavaria, Germany

In a Bavarian village, competitors still settle their strength contests by hooking fingers through a leather loop and pulling.

At the German finger wrestling championships in Pflugdorf-Stadl, Bavaria, competitors hook a leather loop around a single middle finger and try to haul their opponent across a table before their grip gives out.

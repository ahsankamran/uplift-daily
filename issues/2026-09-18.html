Small Wonders · Yungas Forest, Bolivia

Biologists confirmed the first new wild cat species in a century, high in Bolivia's cloud forest.

Biologist Paola Nogales-Ascarrunz photographed an unfamiliar spotted cat in Bolivia's Yungas cloud forest, on the eastern slope of the Andes, and couldn't place it. Geneticist Eduardo Eizirik's team compared genomes from 38 tiger cats across South America and confirmed the animal, now named the tilcayo, split from its relatives some 1.4 million years ago — the first new wild cat species described since 1923. The three-pound tilcayo is known only from the Yungas so far, and researchers suspect the region holds still more cats waiting to be told apart.

Nature · Cheshire, England

Two reintroduced beavers raised a Cheshire peatland's groundwater nearly forty-fold.

Ecologist Melanie Baker's four-year study found that two Eurasian beavers, released into a fenced 4.5-hectare enclosure at Hatch Mere in Cheshire, raised local groundwater levels by up to 37 times and lifted surface water by a median of 50 centimeters. Working with the Cheshire Wildlife Trust, the beavers built dams that kept the peatland measurably wetter through the dry summer of 2022 than the surrounding land. Baker says the findings point to beavers as a low-cost way to make Britain's drained peat bogs more resilient to drought.

Everyday Joy · Chan Chan, Peru

Archaeologists opened a Chimú tomb that had stayed sealed for six hundred years.

Archaeologists at Chan Chan, Peru, unsealed an intact Chimú mausoleum holding the remains of at least 38 people, buried roughly six centuries ago with metal weapons, gold and copper ornaments, and fine textiles. Lead archaeologist Jorge Meneses said it was the first time researchers have found an elite Chimú burial platform with its contents undisturbed, in a complex where only 7 percent has been excavated. Anthropologist Gabriel Prieto called it "the discovery of the decade."

Quiet Kindness · Yonkers, New York, USA

A Yonkers charity turns a Wednesday haircut into an hour of dignity.

On the first Wednesday of each month, Catholic Charities' Westchester center in Yonkers, New York, opens its doors for free haircuts and a hot lunch, no appointment required. Volunteer barber Brayan Sanchez Sanchez, the child of a day laborer himself, and stylist Gloria Valencia, six years into her monthly shift, cut hair for neighbors enrolled in the center's job-training program. "I always feel good about myself after I leave," said Khaled, a regular.

Science & Medicine · England

England's health service approved a life-extending breast cancer drug.

NICE approved Enhertu for NHS use in England on September 17, reversing an earlier rejection made on cost grounds, and the drug is now available to roughly 1,000 women a year with HER2-low metastatic breast cancer. In trials, patients who had already had chemotherapy saw median overall survival reach 23.4 months and progression-free survival reach 9.9 months. The decision follows a two-year campaign by patients and clinicians to get the treatment funded.

Global Progress · South Africa

South Africa has gone 490 days without a rolling blackout.

Eskom reported this week that South Africa has run 490 consecutive days without loadshedding since May 2025, while its power plants hit 67.96 percent availability, the highest in six years. Lower unplanned outages and less reliance on diesel generators cut emergency fuel spending by 4.8 billion rand, and 1.4 million customers have had normal supply restored. About 247,617 customers, 3.4 percent of Eskom's base, remain on temporary load reduction as repairs continue.

Everyday Joy · Sapporo, Hokkaido, Japan

The 35th Yosakoi Soran Festival fills Sapporo with 30,000 dancers across seventeen outdoor stages — a tradition born in 1992 when Hokkaido University students fused two regional folk dance cultures.

The 35th Yosakoi Soran Festival opened on 10 June 2026 in Sapporo, Hokkaido, drawing around 30,000 performers to seventeen stages spanning Odori Park and the streets of the city centre, with an expected audience of two million visitors over five days. Every competing team writes its own original choreography and score — each fusing the percussion of naruko wooden clappers with arrangements of Soran Bushi, the traditional fishermen's work song of Hokkaido — so no two of the hundreds of groups perform the same piece. Admission is free at most venues; the festival runs through 14 June.

Nature · 21 Countries, Europe

A record 603 dams, weirs, and culverts were removed across 21 European countries in 2025 — the most in a single year since monitoring began — reopening 3,700 kilometres of river corridor for migratory fish.

A June 2026 report from the Dam Removal Europe coalition found that 603 river barriers were dismantled across 21 countries in 2025, surpassing the previous annual record and reconnecting roughly 3,700 kilometres of waterway for migratory salmon, lamprey, and other freshwater species. Sweden led all nations with 173 removals, followed by Finland with 143 and Spain with 109; Iceland and North Macedonia each completed their first-ever river barrier dismantlement. Europe's cumulative total has now exceeded 9,680 removals, bringing the EU's commitment to restore at least 25,000 kilometres of free-flowing river by 2030 measurably within reach.

Science & Medicine · Global

A daily pill called daraxonrasib nearly doubled median survival in previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer — 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemotherapy — in a Phase 3 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine.

The RASolute 302 Phase 3 trial enrolled 398 patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer who had progressed on prior treatment, and found that daraxonrasib extended median overall survival to 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemotherapy — a 60 percent reduction in the risk of death. The drug targets the mutated RAS protein that drives tumour growth in more than 90 percent of pancreatic cancer cases, a molecular target that had resisted effective treatment for four decades; results were presented to a standing ovation at the 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting and published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 31 May 2026. The FDA has granted expanded access while formal approval is sought; pancreatic cancer kills roughly 500,000 people each year worldwide.

Global Progress · Indonesia

Indonesia's national electrification rate reached 99.83 percent in the first quarter of 2026 — covering 280 million people across 17,000 islands, up from 91 percent a decade ago.

Indonesia's state statistics confirmed that 99.832 percent of households in the archipelago had access to electricity in the first quarter of 2026, a milestone reached across one of the world's most geographically complex nations through a decade of grid extensions, mini-grid systems, and off-grid solar installations for remote communities. Bali and Jakarta have achieved 100 percent coverage; most provinces now exceed 95 percent. The Village Electrification programme aims to close the remaining fraction within four years, completing one of the world's largest electrification drives by population.

Quiet Kindness · Nanaimo, British Columbia, Canada

In Nanaimo, British Columbia, a woman answered her door to find a 12-year-old boy whose father had collapsed — and performed chest compressions until paramedics arrived and revived him.

When a 12-year-old boy knocked on neighbours' doors in Nanaimo, British Columbia on 1 June 2026, Amy Pye was the one who answered — and found his father unconscious on the floor. She called 911 and performed chest compressions under dispatcher guidance until paramedics arrived with a defibrillator and restored the man's heartbeat; the boy had already tried three other doors without answer. The family later reached out to say the father was recovering; Pye told NanaimoNewsNOW: "I'm just happy that I answered my door."

Small Wonders · Jupiter Beach, Florida, USA

An olive ridley sea turtle nicknamed Tini came ashore at Jupiter Beach, Florida — 1,500 miles north of her species' usual nesting range — to lay the first olive ridley nest ever documented in the state.

On 18 May 2026, researchers at Loggerhead Marinelife Center in Juno Beach, Florida confirmed that an olive ridley sea turtle — nicknamed Tini — had nested at Jupiter Beach, 1,500 miles north of the species' typical nesting grounds in Brazil, Trinidad, and Gabon. Scientists collected genetic samples and submitted full documentation to the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission; researchers believe shifting ocean conditions may be drawing individual turtles poleward. Hatchlings are expected later this summer — the first olive ridley young ever to emerge on Florida sand — and researchers will monitor whether Tini returns in future seasons.

Everyday Joy · Huinchiri, Cusco Region, Peru

Every June for nearly 600 years, four Quechua communities in Peru's Andes dismantle and completely rebuild the world's last handwoven Inca rope bridge across the Apurímac gorge.

The Q'eswachaka suspension bridge — thirty metres long, suspended twenty-eight metres above the Apurímac River — is cut to the river below each June and rewoven from scratch over four days by around 1,000 people, a continuous practice unchanged since Inca times.

Global Progress · Global Oceans

On World Oceans Day, the UN confirmed that just over 10 percent of the world's ocean is now inside a protected or conserved area — crossing the threshold for the first time in history.

The protected area total reached 10.01 percent as of June 8, after roughly five million square kilometres of new protections were established globally over the preceding two years, including Ghana's first marine protected area after fifteen years of community advocacy.

Science & Medicine · Glasgow, Scotland

A phase 3 trial finds that finerenone slows kidney decline by 23 percent in patients with chronic kidney disease who do not have diabetes — the first treatment result for the most common form of the condition.

The FIND-CKD trial enrolled 1,584 patients with non-diabetic chronic kidney disease and found that finerenone reduced the composite endpoint of kidney failure and cardiovascular events by 23 percent over 32 months — the first large phase 3 study to fill this treatment gap.

Nature · Central Kazakhstan

A Przewalski's horse foal was born wild on Kazakhstan's steppe for the first time in two centuries.

On August 22, a colt was born to Tessa, a mare reintroduced from Berlin's Tierpark, at the Alibi Reintroduction Centre in the Altyn Dala State Nature Reserve — the first Przewalski's horse foal born wild in central Kazakhstan since the species vanished from the region roughly 200 years ago. Tessa arrived in 2024 as one of seven Przewalski's horses transported from European zoos to rebuild a self-sustaining herd on the Golden Steppe, and within minutes of birth the foal was on his feet, nursing at her side. "We hope that the number of kertagy will grow year by year," said Daniyar Turgambayev, chairman of Kazakhstan's Forestry and Wildlife Committee, of the effort to return the world's last truly wild horse to its former range.

Global Progress · European Union

New EU-wide right-to-repair rules took effect for household electronics.

On August 24, EU-wide right-to-repair rules came into force, requiring manufacturers to fix technically repairable washing machines, vacuum cleaners, phones and tablets for free or at a reasonable price, within a reasonable timeframe. The regulation also obliges manufacturers to publish clear repair information and guarantee access to spare parts, while a new European Repair Information Form and national platforms, including Ireland's RepairMyStuff.ie, help consumers find services. The European Commission estimates the rules will generate €4.8 billion in growth and investment while curbing the 35 million tonnes of waste created each year by goods thrown out too soon.

Science & Medicine · London, England

An AI system guided surgeons through a brain operation that saved a man's sight.

At the National Hospital for Neurology and Neurosurgery in London, an AI system built in-house at UCL analysed live video from inside Rhys Hibbert's skull, highlighting the nerves and blood vessels packed around his pituitary gland as surgeons removed an 11-millimetre tumour. Unlike existing surgical aids, which rely on scans taken before the operation, the system reads the live camera feed in real time, part of a clinical trial funded by the National Institute for Health and Care Research. Hibbert, 48, was walking unaided within a week of the operation and has since returned to work; researchers now plan to test the system on more patients.

Quiet Kindness · Phuket, Thailand

Two vacationing strangers stopped to fix a stranger's broken excavator for free.

On August 20, two Australian tourists walking along Phuket's Bangla Road stopped when they saw road crew workers struggling to force a steel track back onto a stalled excavator. One traveler set down his suitcase mid-errand to help lift the track into place, a second joined moments later, and together with the crew they tightened bolts and greased the machinery until it rolled again. The men left without pay, work caught on video by bystander Romklao Chooklin, who said: "There are still plenty of lovely, kind-hearted tourists out there."

Small Wonders · Liskeard, Cornwall, England

A wallaby was spotted hopping through a Cornish town before dawn.

At 5:30am on August 25, pasty-shop owner Scot Weller filmed a wallaby loping down a residential street in Liskeard, Cornwall, at first mistaking the dark, big-eared shape for a dog. He followed on foot past a corner shop before losing track of the animal near the town's leisure centre, and Devon and Cornwall Police searched the area but found nothing. It has since been sighted around the local secondary school's playing fields, and no one yet knows how a marsupial came to be loose in rural England.

Everyday Joy · Dozwil, Switzerland

A Swiss gardener broke his own chili pepper world record for the third time.

On August 11, Jürg Wiesli harvested a chili pepper measuring 52.5 centimetres — one foot, 8.6 inches — from his garden in Dozwil, breaking his own Guinness World Record for the longest chili pepper. Wiesli has held the title since 2017, growing his self-bred "Wiesli Big Long" strain from Cayenne seeds and topping his own mark twice more over nine years. "I'm happy to have a world-record chilli once again after more than 10 years of breeding," he said.

Nature · Tanzania, Kenya & Mozambique

A program that pays villages to protect lions instead of killing them is spreading across three countries.

Communities in Tanzania, Kenya and Mozambique now earn points for wildlife caught on trail cameras, redeemable for health insurance, veterinary care or school supplies.

Science & Medicine · Birmingham, England

A gentler cancer treatment is sparing four in five rectal cancer patients from losing their rectum.

A UK-led trial found four in five people with early-stage rectal cancer avoided radical surgery after five weeks of chemoradiotherapy, keeping their rectum intact a year later.

Small Wonders · Nepal

A pangolin collected in 1836 just solved a 190-year mystery about its own species.

DNA from a Nepal pangolin specimen collected in 1836 confirmed the Himalayan pangolin as a distinct species, settling a naming debate that had puzzled specialists for years.

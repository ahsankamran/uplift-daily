Small Wonders · Konstanz, Germany

The ripples that cross a starling flock may be aimed at the falcon's eyes.

Eduardo Sampaio and Iain Couzin of the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior have set out the case that the waves running through a threatened animal group are addressed to the hunter rather than to each other: under a peregrine, a dark band sweeps across a starling flock about every three-quarters of a second. They line up four displays that look nothing alike — starlings over a roost, sulphur mollies diving and resurfacing in Mexican springs, giant honeybees rippling their abdomens to turn back a hornet, a hunting cuttlefish running stripes down its own body — and note that in each one the wave travels faster than the animals making it, often arriving before the strike does. It is a proposal rather than a result, and the two have sketched how to test it: models of predator vision, virtual reality, and trials that vary the timing of a group's response while holding its size and density still.

Nature · Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia

Otters have started showing up along Kuala Lumpur's Klang River.

Smooth-coated otters are being seen along stretches of the Klang, a river graded Class 5 — severely polluted — within living memory and now holding at Class 3, with Class 2B, clean enough to swim in, the stated target. Hundreds of rubbish traps installed under the government's River of Life programme and solar-powered interceptors run by a Dutch non-profit have taken tens of thousands of tonnes of waste out of the water, and Woo Chee Yoong, a wildlife ecologist who co-founded the Malaysian Otter Network, reads the animals as a check on whether that work is reaching the river itself. Nobody yet knows how many family groups the city holds, so the network is setting up a scheme for residents to log sightings, and researchers want banks replanted rather than concreted so there are crevices and cover for the otters to den in.

Everyday Joy · Dorchester, Dorset, England

Dorchester has cast its wandering cat in bronze.

A life-size bronze of Susie, a cat who lets herself into the town's pubs, shops and supermarkets, was unveiled on Sunday outside the Julia's House charity shop on South Street. The Weymouth sculptor Ama Menec cast her as a Roman-era cat, a nod to Durnovaria, the Roman town underneath the modern one, and a poem by Rebecca Katya Danicic, a former Bard of Dorchester, is set into the plinth. Dozens of people stood in the rain for it alongside the town crier, the town's bards and the Rock Choir; Susie herself did not attend, having chosen to wait out the weather in a nearby supermarket.

Quiet Kindness · Nefyn, Gwynedd, Wales

A Nefyn pub has opened its back room to whoever in the village wants it.

Tafarn yr Heliwr, a mid-19th-century pub on the Llŷn peninsula that used to be called The Sportsman, has put in an interactive darts board and a set of screens, and made the room and the kit free for local groups to book. The work was done with guidance and a grant from Pub is The Hub, the same scheme that helped the pub open a community garden that volunteers gave around 1,000 hours to designing and planting. Lois Povey, the pub's community officer, says the aim is to get young families, older residents and local organisations into the same room on the same evening, over a game or an old film.

Science & Medicine · An international trial

A monthly injection held kidney function steady for two years in IgA nephropathy.

The VISIONARY trial gave 510 adults with IgA nephropathy either sibeprenlimab or a placebo every four weeks on top of their usual kidney treatment, and after two years the treated group's filtration rate had edged up by 1.3 mL/min/1.73m² while the placebo group's fell by 7.9. One of 259 people on the drug had a major kidney event against 28 of 251 on placebo; five people on placebo needed dialysis or a transplant, and none on the drug did. The trial was led by Dana Rizk of the University of Alabama at Birmingham, excluded the most severe forms of the disease, and has an extension study now following participants past the two-year mark.

Global Progress · Ethiopia

Ethiopia is knocking on doors to reach 11.3 million children with polio vaccine.

A house-to-house round that began on 21 November is aimed at 11,348,548 children under five across 629 woredas in Addis Ababa, Oromia, Central Ethiopia, South Ethiopia and Southwest Ethiopia. The teams of health workers and community volunteers are also writing down which children have had no routine vaccines at all, and walking them back into ordinary immunisation services while they are there. A second round follows from 18 to 21 December; Ethiopia has no wild poliovirus, but a variant type 1 turned up this year in Gambella, Oromia and South Ethiopia, and earlier response rounds in those areas reached more than a million children.

Small Wonders · Chetumal, Quintana Roo, Mexico

Mexican hairy dwarf porcupines turn out to glow under ultraviolet light.

Twenty adults checked under a 395-nanometre torch, and every one of them came up greenish-blue.

Chicks are hatching in the wild for the first time in those hills in more than 150 years.

Small Wonders · Aguarico River, Ecuador

Seven Indigenous women are hand-raising a threatened river turtle back from decline in the Ecuadorian Amazon.

Siekopai women who call themselves the Tari Nomiowa'i, or turtle women, patrol the banks of the Aguarico River logging charapa turtle nests with a mobile data app before moving the eggs to a protected incubation site. In the 2025–26 season they collected 235 eggs and released 208 hatchlings back into the river with local schoolchildren, shielding them from poachers, boat traffic and flooding. Community leader Janeth Piaguaje says the patrols are also training the next generation of caretakers.

Nature · Mendocino Coast, California

Coho salmon returned to Northern California rivers in the highest numbers researchers have seen in decades.

An estimated 30,000 coho salmon returned to Mendocino coast rivers including the Noyo, Garcia and Navarro last season, ten times the region's 10-year average. NOAA scientists credit more than 100 habitat restoration projects since 2009 — widened culverts, removed barriers, rivers reshaped back into natural meanders — for giving the fish passage and cooler water. Officials say the rebound shows recovery is possible when habitat, hydrology and ocean conditions align.

Science & Medicine · Denmark

Starting statins within a year of a diabetes diagnosis was linked to a lower dementia risk in a large Danish study.

Researchers tracked 132,585 Danish adults who developed type 2 diabetes between 2006 and 2019, comparing dementia rates over a decade based on when each person started statin treatment. Those who began statins within a year of diagnosis had a 15 percent lower relative risk of dementia than people who never took them, and even those who started one to five years later saw a 10 percent lower risk. The findings, presented at the European Society of Cardiology congress and published in The Lancet Regional Health – Europe, point to cholesterol control as another lever against cognitive decline in diabetes patients.

Quiet Kindness · Paris, France

A homeless shelter in Paris installed an outdoor gym, and residents say it is changing their habits.

Finnish fitness company Omnigym and French nonprofit Emmaüs Solidarité installed free outdoor exercise equipment inside a Paris shelter as a pilot they call the People's Gym. A month in, 80 percent of residents who used it reported better physical health and 90 percent said it helped them connect with other people, and one resident said simply that he now smokes less. Omnigym says inactivity affects lower-income communities disproportionately, and it hopes to install the equipment in other shelters next.

Everyday Joy · Buin, Chile

Injured penguins in Chile are healing faster in copper-threaded vests.

At Buin Zoo near Santiago, veterinarians fitted injured Humboldt penguins with snug red vests woven with copper and zinc fibers to protect wounds while they heal. Veterinarian Tomás Pino says the birds tolerate the vests well and their wounds visibly improve within a day, since the metals fight bacteria and zinc spurs new blood vessel growth. The idea, already used to treat cats and dogs, is now being tried on a species whose active nesting sites have fallen 63 percent since 2022.

Global Progress · Bangladesh

Bangladesh cut its maternal mortality rate below the global 2030 target, four years early.

The World Health Organization's South-East Asia Region confirmed Bangladesh has reached a maternal mortality ratio below 140 deaths per 100,000 live births, the UN's Sustainable Development Goal threshold, well ahead of the 2030 deadline. Officials credited sustained investment in reproductive and maternal health programs and consistent policy support for the decline. The milestone was announced alongside eight other countries' progress on tetanus and child mortality at a WHO regional session this week.

Everyday Joy · Quibdó, Colombia

A month-long festival honoring a saint and a culture opened in a Colombian river city.

Quibdó opened its month-long Fiesta de San Pacho, a UNESCO-recognized Afro-Colombian festival honoring Saint Francis of Assisi that dates to 1648.

Science & Medicine · Central Red Sea, Saudi Arabia

A probiotic treatment kept heat-stressed Red Sea coral alive through a marine heatwave.

KAUST scientists found that both a probiotic and a longer-lasting postbiotic formula significantly improved coral survival through a marine heatwave.

Nature · Hampshire, England

Thirty turtle doves were released into an English national park to help reverse a 98 percent decline.

The South Downs National Park Authority released 30 captive-bred turtle doves near Selborne, restoring ponds and forage along their migration route.

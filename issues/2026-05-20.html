Everyday Joy · Rukai, Lithuania

A 400-year-old oak in the Lithuanian village of Rukai has won the 2026 European Tree of the Year, drawing more than 200,000 votes across twelve countries and ending Poland's four-year run of victories.

The common oak (Quercus robur) standing in Rukai village took first place in the 2026 European Tree of the Year contest, with more than 200,000 votes cast across twelve countries — the highest participation in the contest's history since it launched in 2011. Generations of villagers built a stone wall around the tree and continue to celebrate beside it; a new points system introduced this year weighted cultural significance alongside raw vote tallies, making the result the most closely competed yet. The Rukai oak is Lithuania's first winner in the contest, beating nominees from 25 other countries.

Global Progress · Kenya

Kenya has been formally recognised by WHO for eliminating sleeping sickness, after recording no indigenous cases since 2009 — the second country to eliminate the more severe rhodesiense form.

At the 79th World Health Assembly in Geneva on 19 May 2026, WHO validated Kenya for eliminating Human African Trypanosomiasis, making it the tenth country globally and the second to eliminate the rhodesiense form, which can kill within weeks without treatment. Kenya sustained elimination through tsetse fly control, active case surveillance, and community health integration across six western counties including Busia, Homa Bay, and Migori, where the disease had persisted for generations. The validation adds Kenya to a list that now includes Benin, Côte d'Ivoire, and Rwanda, as WHO advances its target of global elimination of sleeping sickness by 2030.

Science & Medicine · Stockholm, Sweden

A 47-year study of 427 Swedish adults finds that fitness begins a measurable decline at age 35 — but taking up exercise at any point in adulthood still produces gains of 5 to 10 percent.

Researchers at Karolinska Institutet followed 427 randomly selected Swedish men and women from age 16 to 63 across 47 years, repeatedly measuring cardiorespiratory fitness, muscle strength, and endurance. Both fitness and strength began a steady decline around age 35 regardless of training history — but participants who became physically active later in adulthood still improved their capacity by 5 to 10 percent, confirming that the window for meaningful gains does not close. The findings, published in the Journal of Cachexia, Sarcopenia and Muscle and widely reported in May 2026, resolve a longstanding debate about whether beginning exercise late can meaningfully reverse the trend.

Small Wonders · Teruel, Spain

Europe's most complete stegosaur skull has been unearthed in a former crop field in Teruel, Spain — and the find reshapes the family tree of one of prehistory's most recognisable dinosaurs.

Researchers from the Fundación Conjunto Paleontológico de Teruel-Dinópolis discovered the best-preserved stegosaur skull yet found in Europe in a field at Riodeva, Teruel, belonging to Dacentrurus armatus — a species that lived 150 million years ago. After comparing 115 anatomical traits across 30 fossil groups, the team proposed a new evolutionary branch, Neostegosauria; the same site has yielded nearly 200 fossils including juveniles alongside adults. Intact stegosaur skulls are among the rarest finds in paleontology because the bone is fragile and poorly attached, making this the first specimen to reveal the full head shape of any European stegosaur.

Nature · Nagaland, India

Thirteen rescued Asian giant tortoises at a Nagaland zoo have produced 114 captive-bred offspring — roughly half the estimated wild population remaining across all of Asia.

The Nagaland Zoological Park began a captive breeding programme in 2017 with 13 Asian giant tortoises (Manouria emys phayrei) rescued from food markets and surrendered as pets, and has since bred 114 offspring. More than 100 juveniles have been released into 407 community forest reserves across Nagaland, where young local residents trained as Tortoise Guardians track each animal daily using VHF telemetry. The neighbouring state of Manipur has now replicated the model with its first successful artificial incubation, suggesting the approach can spread across the species' shrinking range in Northeast India and Myanmar.

Quiet Kindness · Bradford, England

A woman in Bradford crocheted and delivered more than 100 baby blankets to the maternity ward of the hospital that saved her sight — arriving in person with the hundredth.

Eliz Sandalls of Bradford began crocheting baby blankets for the maternity ward at Bradford Royal Infirmary after surgeons saved her remaining sight in 2023, finding the craft purposeful during her long recovery. She delivered her hundredth blanket in person in May 2026; hospital midwives say each one is given to a newborn family as a small welcome gift. She has no target number in mind and continues at the same pace, one blanket at a time.

Nature · Global / Colombia

On Global Big Day 2026, birders across 203 countries recorded 8,023 species in a single day — the first time the annual tally has broken the 8,000-species threshold.

On 9 May 2026, 80,000 volunteer birders submitted 4.1 million observations to Cornell Lab of Ornithology's eBird platform, documenting 8,023 bird species in a single day — a new global record, with results published 13 May. Colombia led all nations for the fifth consecutive year, with 4,352 observers recording 1,566 species — roughly 40 percent of every bird species known to inhabit the country, found in a single day.

Quiet Kindness · Edinburgh, Scotland

Edinburgh's library service gifted 150 prisoners hand-wrapped books in a Secret Santa scheme that cost £20 and helped earn the city's libraries the UK's Library of the Year award.

Staff at Oxgangs Library and those working inside HMP Edinburgh wrapped books chosen by prisoners' genre preferences and delivered them as a book Secret Santa, at a packaging cost of just £20. The City of Edinburgh Council Library Service was named Library of the Year at the 2026 British Book Awards on 11 May, beating 46 finalists from across the UK and Ireland.

Science & Medicine · St. Louis, USA

Thirty adults with high-risk blood cancer received CRISPR-edited stem cell transplants in the first human trial to safely combine gene editing with antibody therapy — and all thirty achieved full engraftment.

A phase 1/2 trial in Nature Medicine, led by Washington University in St. Louis across 15 North American sites, removed the CD33 protein from donor stem cells using CRISPR so an antibody drug could be used alongside — a combination previously impossible. All 30 patients achieved full engraftment by day 28.

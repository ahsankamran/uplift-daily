Nature · Otago, New Zealand

New Zealand just opened five new national parks of the sea.

Five marine reserves covering 308 square kilometres of the Otago coast came into force on July 1, the country's first new marine protection in more than a decade. Co-managed by the Department of Conservation and the Kāi Tahu iwi under the name Te Au Roa o Te Rakihouia, the no-take zones will be patrolled by nine newly hired rangers guarding waters used by yellow-eyed penguins, New Zealand sea lions, and Hector's dolphins. "For Otago's wildlife, this will be game-changing," said Jamie Fowler of WWF New Zealand, as kelp forests and reefs get room to recover.

Everyday Joy · Indiana, United States

A research administrator just walked the equivalent of Earth's circumference, on his own driveway.

Russell Mumper, a vice president for research at Indiana University, finished 24,901 miles of walking on June 25, fourteen years after he set the goal on a whim in 2012. He logged 5,900 separate walks and roughly 55 million steps while working full time and moving house three times, wearing out 49 pairs of running shoes along the way. His mother waved a checkered flag as he crossed the finish line in the driveway, the family's two dogs trotting beside him.

Science & Medicine · England

Regular trips to the museum or theatre may slow the body's biological clock.

Tracking almost 1,900 English adults over 50, researchers found that those who visited museums, galleries, the cinema or theatre at least every few months had a physiological age of 66.9 years, three years younger than infrequent attendees. The team, led by Yusuke Matsuyama, measured ten health markers including blood pressure, grip strength and walking speed to calculate each person's biological age. "The impact can be comparable to frequent physical activity," the researchers wrote in the Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health.

Quiet Kindness · Kingston, Canada

Kingston turned its little free libraries into little free pantries.

The city opened three no-questions-asked food pantries this month on Days Road, Clergy Street and West Campus, stocked with fresh produce and non-perishables anyone can take. Built on the same "take what you need, leave what you can" model as Kingston's book-sharing boxes, the pilot draws on donations and produce from the Kingston Community Garden Network.

Small Wonders · Madrid, Spain

A rescued chimpanzee carried a crystal back to bed and wouldn't give it up.

At a primate sanctuary near Madrid, chimpanzees given a choice between an ordinary stone and a clear crystal picked the crystal every time, holding it to the light to study its transparency. One chimp named Yvan carried his back to the dormitory and traded it only for bananas and yogurt, in a study led by crystallographer Juan Manuel García-Ruiz.

Global Progress · European Union

Solar became the European Union's single largest source of electricity in June.

Solar generated a record 52 terawatt-hours across the EU in June, 25 percent of the bloc's electricity and enough to outrank nuclear, gas, wind, hydro and coal. Spain and Germany each drew more than a third of their power from the sun that month, up from an EU-wide 10 percent just five years ago.

Small Wonders · Uttarakhand, India

A spider with Hawaii's famous smile turned up 7,500 miles away in the Himalayas.

Surveying ants above 2,000 meters in Uttarakhand, researchers found a new species carrying the same bright red smiley-face pattern long thought unique to Hawaii, evolved independently on the other side of the world.

Everyday Joy · Vindolanda, England

A Roman guardian spirit spent 1,600 years hiding under a barracks floor.

Excavators at Vindolanda fort turned up a rare sandstone relief of a Genius, a Roman household guardian, deliberately buried under a fourth-century barracks floor for 1,600 years.

Nature · Indonesia & Myanmar

Southeast Asia's mangroves have flipped from the world's biggest loss to one of its biggest gains.

A new satellite study finds Southeast Asia has gone from causing 60 percent of global mangrove loss to driving 43 percent of the world's gains since 2010.

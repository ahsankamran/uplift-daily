Quiet Kindness · London, England

A daughter's TikTok video saved her father's 30-year-old shoe shop.

A TikTok video about a rack of Y2K kitten heels drew 300,000 views overnight and sent two-hour queues to a small vintage shoe shop in Shoreditch, London. The video's creator, journalist Zineb Lazraq, was trying to help her father Rachid, who had watched custom dwindle at Rich Collection Italia after more than 30 years in business. Rachid took down the shop's closing-down sign as customers applauded, and now plans to open an online store to keep serving his new, younger following.

Nature · Tongjiang, China

China and Russia released nearly half a million sturgeon into their shared border river.

Biologists in Tongjiang, on China's border with Russia, released 200 Amur sturgeon and 485,000 kaluga — both threatened sturgeon species — into the Amur River on July 14, along with 5.2 million fry of other native fish. The release was the 18th joint restocking effort by Chinese and Russian conservation agencies since 2008, and many of the young sturgeon were tagged with fluorescent markers so scientists can track their migration and survival. The two countries have now released more than 20 million sturgeon and kaluga into the river system, part of a long effort to rebuild fish stocks depleted by decades of overfishing.

Global Progress · Burkina Faso

Burkina Faso just cut malaria deaths among young children by 60 percent in a year.

Malaria cases in Burkina Faso fell 32 percent between 2024 and 2025, to about 7.3 million, while deaths dropped 44 percent to just under 2,000, according to Malaria Consortium country director Pierre Kayende. Deaths among children under five fell even further, down nearly 60 percent, after the country distributed 15 million insecticide-treated bed nets in 2025 and scaled up two malaria vaccines, RTS,S and R21, to cover the whole country. Community volunteers also eliminated 2.1 million mosquito breeding sites, part of a push toward the country's goal of eliminating malaria entirely by 2030.

Science & Medicine · Granada, Spain

A 12-week fasting schedule kept weight off for a year, a Spanish trial found.

In a University of Granada trial of 99 adults with overweight or obesity, those who ate within an 8-hour daily window for 12 weeks kept off 3 to 4 more kilograms a year later than a group given only Mediterranean-diet advice. Led by researcher Alba Camacho Cardeñosa, the study tested four groups — an early window before 10 a.m., a late window after 1 p.m., a self-chosen window, and a standard longer schedule — and found early time-restricted eating best preserved fat loss over fat-free mass. A third of participants kept practicing intermittent fasting on their own long after the formal 12-week program ended.

Everyday Joy · Ipswich, Queensland, Australia

More than 600 trombonists gathered in Ipswich to break a world record.

Over 600 trombone players packed Tulmur Place in Ipswich, Queensland, on July 12 to perform "76 Trombones" from The Music Man, aiming to break the Guinness World Record for the largest trombone ensemble. Organized by Ipswich City Big Band director Matt Chambers as part of the SPARK Ipswich arts festival, the group easily topped the existing record of 368 players, set in Washington, D.C., in 2012. Mayor Teresa Harding has submitted the attempt to Guinness World Records for official verification.

Small Wonders · Saratoga, California

Four orphaned bobcat kittens who arrived within days of each other are now growing up together.

Four bobcat kittens, found separately in Kern County, Concord and Carmel, California, over the course of a single week, are now being raised side by side at the Peninsula Humane Society's Wildlife Care Center in Saratoga. Staff say the unrelated kittens have taken to one another readily, tumbling together and practicing hunting skills as caregivers oversee their enrichment. The four will stay at the center until October, when they are old enough to be released back into the wild.

Global Progress · Brazil

Amazon deforestation just fell to its lowest point in a decade.

Brazil's National Institute for Space Research recorded 1,295 square kilometers of Amazon forest cleared between January and June, a 38 percent drop from the same period in 2025 and the lowest first-half total in a decade.

Nature · Gir, Gujarat, India

Indian grey hornbills are breeding in Gir again, sixty years after they vanished.

Forest officials have confirmed the hornbills breeding successfully for a fourth consecutive year, tracing back to roughly 40 birds released in phases between 2021 and 2023.

Small Wonders · Lomami National Park, DR Congo

A monkey with orange-cream lips no one had catalogued turned up deep in the Congo Basin.

Researchers described Colobus congoensis from 114 sightings recorded between 2018 and 2022, only the fifth new African monkey species identified in 75 years.

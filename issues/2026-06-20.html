Small Wonders · Lake Tuz, Türkiye

More than five thousand flamingo chicks have hatched at Lake Tuz in central Turkey — more than double last year's count — after a water-pumping project reversed the drought damage that wiped out entire cohorts of nestlings in 2021.

Drone footage shot on 10 June over Turkey's great salt flat counted roughly 5,000 greater flamingo chicks sheltering under their parents at one of the Eastern Mediterranean's most important flamingo breeding sites; the directorate for natural asset protection had begun pumping freshwater into the shallow nesting flats in 2022, after prolonged heat nearly dried the lake and killed thousands of young birds. The president of the Bird's Eye View and Ecology Association told Good News Network that the current chick count is more than double last year's, which was itself more than double the year before — suggesting the recovery is still accelerating. The lake, once one of the world's largest flamingo breeding grounds, began losing water in 1988 and was entirely dry by 2016; the intervention has given it a second life.

Nature · India, Nepal & Pakistan

Twenty years after India, Nepal, and Pakistan banned a cattle drug that had reduced vulture populations by 99.9%, captive-bred white-rumped vultures are now raising wild chicks of their own.

The RSPB-supported Saving Asia's Vultures from Extinction consortium has bred 740 birds across four specialised centres and released more than 110 into protected tiger reserves since 2006, when the veterinary use of diclofenac was banned after surveys confirmed it had nearly wiped out three vulture species across the subcontinent. The first cohort of captive-bred birds released in Haryana in 2020 is now breeding naturally without human support, and 31 birds released from West Bengal have dispersed across India, Nepal, and Bhutan with zero recorded poisoning deaths. A new round of releases in Maharashtra's Pench, Tadoba-Andhari, and Melghat Tiger Reserves is under way in 2026, with population monitoring now showing measurable growth in wild-nesting pairs for the first time since the crash.

Science & Medicine · London, UK

London's clean-air zones have cut emergency hospital admissions for heart disease at a rate of 8.1 percent per year since the scheme began — the first study to measure the effect directly against a matched control area.

Imperial College London researchers compared emergency admission data from inside and outside central London's Ultra Low Emission Zone and its predecessor the T-charge, covering January 2014 to March 2020; cardiovascular admissions fell by 8.1% per year in the zone, respiratory disease admissions by 6.2%, and all-cause emergency admissions by 3.1%. Published on 12 June in Environment International and led by Dr Rosemary Chamberlain, the study drew on a control area in outer London to isolate the effect of the air-quality policy from broader health trends. The evidence that city-level vehicle restrictions produce measurable public-health returns comes as several other European cities weigh their own low-emission zones.

Quiet Kindness · Mumbai, India

Each Sunday morning at 4 a.m., a Mumbai couple wake, cook, and serve breakfast to a hundred people — a routine they have kept for nearly three years and fifteen thousand meals.

Pooja and Dipesh Dedhia rise before dawn every Sunday and prepare fresh food through the Matoshree Foundation for daily-wage workers and neighbours in their area; they fund the meals themselves, without grants or donors, and involve their children in the cooking. The couple told The Better India on 17 June that they have not missed a single Sunday in three years and have served more than 15,000 meals — the name Matoshree, a Marathi word for a beloved mother, reflecting the feeling they want anyone at their table to carry through the working week.

Global Progress · São Tomé and Príncipe

São Tomé and Príncipe's president has signed the nation's first marine protected areas into law — two coastal reserves protecting coral reefs, shark nursery grounds, and sea-turtle nesting beaches.

President Carlos Vila Nova approved the first two reserves around the islets of Santana and Ilhéu das Rolas this month, with six more sites in Príncipe's coastal waters to be gazetted before the end of 2026; the protected areas were designed with Fauna & Flora and local fishing communities, who helped shape sustainable-use zones that permit traditional artisanal gear while banning industrial trawling and seabed extraction. The eight Marine Protected Areas together represent the small Gulf of Guinea nation's first formal step toward protecting 30 percent of its coastal waters by 2030, and are expected to stabilise fish stocks for communities that have relied on these waters for generations.

Everyday Joy · Malmö to Oslo, Sweden & Norway

A new daytime train from Malmö to Oslo launched on 15 June — a six-and-a-half-hour journey through Gothenburg's waterfront, Sweden's ancient lake country, and granite gorges, with a dining car serving food sourced from communities along the route.

Snälltåget's Malmö–Oslo service follows Sweden's western coast through Helsingborg, Halmstad, and Varberg before threading north through the forests and lakes of Dalsland — past Bohus Fortress and into Norway via Sarpsborg — with a restaurant car called Krogen, where dishes are made from local ingredients gathered along the line. One-way fares start at 149 Swedish kronor, and the service runs without public subsidy, operated by a company that identified the absence of any direct daytime train across three of Scandinavia's largest cities as a gap worth filling.

Nature · Canterbury, Kent, UK

Four Sumatran tiger cubs — the first ever born at Howletts Wild Animal Park in Kent — have begun venturing outside their den, watched over by first-time mother Tipah.

Born on 9 April to Tipah and father Nakal, the four cubs are one of the largest litters on record for the subspecies; with fewer than 400 individuals surviving in Indonesia's rain forests, each birth coordinated through the European Endangered Species Programme carries genuine genetic weight.

Global Progress · Mombasa, Kenya

Nine African governments announced marine-protection commitments at the 11th Our Ocean Conference in Mombasa — the first time the global ocean summit has convened on African soil.

The three-day summit concluded with the Mombasa Declaration, signed by 16 countries, with pledges covering more than 2,600 ocean investments worth over US$160 billion; Tanzania, Madagascar, and Gabon announced the largest individual commitments.

Science & Medicine · India

A pilot trial in India found that older adults with depression who took a daily probiotic alongside standard antidepressants improved significantly more than those on medication alone.

In the PRODG trial, 58 adults aged 60 and older were assigned to add a probiotic or placebo to standard antidepressant care for 12 weeks; the probiotic group showed meaningfully greater reductions in both depressive and anxiety symptoms, alongside higher BDNF levels and improved gut bacteria composition.

Nature · Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan's ecology ministry says the saiga antelope population is on track to pass five million this calving season — a recovery from fewer than 40,000 animals two decades ago.

Kazakhstan's Ministry of Ecology confirmed in May 2026 that its saiga antelope census is on track to record more than five million animals after the spring calving season closes — up from a low of roughly 40,000 in 2005, a 125-fold recovery in twenty years. A nationwide hunting ban introduced after the 2005 crash, paired with intensive monitoring across the Betpak-Dala and Ural-Caspian steppe ranges, drove the rebound; more than 98 percent of the world's saiga live in Kazakhstan. The International Union for Conservation of Nature has updated the species' Red List status from "critically endangered" to "near threatened," marking one of the most rapid recoveries ever recorded for a large grassland mammal.

Science & Medicine · London, United Kingdom

Thirty-two patients with a genetically distinct form of bowel cancer given immunotherapy before surgery have had zero relapses after 33 months of follow-up.

The NEOPRISM-CRC trial, led by University College London and UCLH with hospitals in Southampton, Leeds, and Manchester, enrolled 32 patients with stage 2–3 bowel cancer carrying an MMR-deficient genetic profile and substituted up to nine weeks of pembrolizumab for post-operative chemotherapy. After 33 months of follow-up, none of the 32 had relapsed — against an expected relapse rate of roughly one in four under standard treatment — with results presented at the American Association for Cancer Research Annual Meeting in San Diego in April 2026. Researchers are now planning a larger confirmatory trial; the MMR-deficient profile affects around 10–15 percent of all bowel cancer cases globally.

Global Progress · Chile

Solar panels generated 29 percent of Chile's national electricity in March 2026 — and on one afternoon in mid-March, solar alone met three-quarters of the country's real-time power demand.

Chile's national grid operator confirmed that solar photovoltaic plants generated 2,141 gigawatt-hours in March 2026 — 28.7 percent of all electricity produced that month — a new monthly record reported by pv magazine on 9 May. At noon on 14 March, solar alone met 75.1 percent of Chile's instantaneous demand; total renewable capacity — solar, wind, and hydro combined — now accounts for 69.9 percent of the country's 38,005-megawatt grid. An additional 10,203 megawatts of renewable capacity is under construction, suggesting the March record will be exceeded before the end of 2026.

Small Wonders · Western Australia

Researchers sampling deep canyons off Western Australia's Ningaloo coast detected giant squid DNA in six locations — the first confirmed sign of the species in those seas in more than 25 years.

A Curtin University team published in the journal Environmental DNA on 13 May 2026 that they found traces of Architeuthis dux in 178 water samples collected from depths reaching 4,510 metres in the Cape Range and Cloates submarine canyons — the first eDNA record of giant squid in Western Australian waters and the northernmost presence confirmed in the eastern Indian Ocean. The same survey identified 226 species in total, including a new range extension for the Pacific sleeper shark and the first Western Australian record of the faceless cusk eel.

Everyday Joy · Tokyo, Japan

Japan's Hobby Show celebrates its 50th year at Tokyo Big Sight, drawing 120,000 visitors over three May days to a festival devoted to making things by hand.

The Japan Hobby Show, first held in 1976, marked its half-century at Tokyo Big Sight East Hall from 8 to 10 May 2026, filling eight halls with exhibitors and workshops covering needlework, embroidery, paper craft, miniature-making, and hand-lettering. The 50th-anniversary theme — "Create, Continue, Connect" — reflected the organisers' aim of linking generations through the love of making; 50 dedicated workshops ran alongside giveaway campaigns across the three-day event, co-organised with The Asahi Shimbun newspaper.

Quiet Kindness · Mumbai, India

A retired advertising executive in Mumbai carries a blank canvas into public squares and invites strangers to paint on it — free of charge, free of instruction, and free of the possibility of getting it wrong.

Zahir Mirza, 60, spent three decades on campaigns for national brands before leaving corporate life to start the Open Easel project: carrying a blank canvas to open squares across Mumbai, Delhi, Surat, and Nashik and setting it out for any passer-by to use, with no charge and a single rule — "There's no such thing as 'bad' art here. Just paint." He has observed that children approach the canvas without hesitation while adults first apologise for their lack of skill, and he says watching the moment that self-doubt dissolves is the reason he keeps going.

Everyday Joy · Venice, Italy

Venice renews its thousand-year pact with the Adriatic today, as the mayor casts a gold ring into the sea from the ceremonial barge of the Serenissima.

On the Sunday of Ascension week since at least the year 1000, Venice has sailed its official barge out to the Lido and thrown a ring into the sea — Desponsamus te, mare — renewing the city's bond with the Adriatic in a ceremony solemnised in 1177 by Pope Alexander III.

Global Progress · Ghana, Kenya & Malawi

Four years of real-world data show the RTS,S malaria vaccine prevented one in eight deaths among vaccinated children in Ghana, Kenya, and Malawi — and the jab is now available in 25 countries.

A Lancet study published 8 May 2026 tracked children in the first three African countries to introduce RTS,S and found all-cause child mortality fell 13 percent in vaccinated populations over four years, with 25 African nations now offering the vaccine in childhood schedules.

Science & Medicine · Montréal, Canada

A molecular 'glycerol pocket' in brown fat that triggers calorie burning turns out to govern bone mineralisation too, opening paths to new treatments for metabolic disease and fragile-bone conditions.

A team led by Lawrence Kazak at McGill University's Goodman Cancer Institute, publishing in Nature on 12 May 2026, identified the trigger for brown fat's futile creatine cycle as glycerol binding to an enzyme called TNAP — the same enzyme that governs bone calcification, suggesting one drug class might eventually address both conditions.

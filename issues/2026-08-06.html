Small Wonders · New South Wales, Australia

A drone pilot filmed a humpback whale giving birth off Australia, only the fourth such birth ever captured on camera.

Wildlife photographer Alexander Forrest was tracking a female humpback showing unusual behaviour off the New South Wales coast on July 28 when the water clouded with blood and a newborn calf surfaced for its first breath. The Organisation for the Rescue and Research of Cetaceans in Australia said it is the first time a birth has been filmed by drone, captured from the legally required distance of more than 100 metres. "Every observation like this has the potential to expand our understanding of humpback whale reproduction, maternal behaviour and the earliest stages of a calf's life," the group said.

Nature · Spain & Portugal

The Iberian lynx population reached a record 2,663 animals across Spain and Portugal.

Official 2025 census figures released this week show the wild population up 10.9 percent on the previous year and more than double the total from just four years ago. The species had fewer than 100 individuals left in 2002, when Spain and Portugal began a breeding and rewilding programme that has since become one of Europe's most cited conservation turnarounds. The 2025 count included 542 territorial females, 72 more than the year before, which together raised 952 cubs.

Global Progress · Worldwide

Food banks in 47 countries kept half a million tonnes of food out of landfills last year.

Member organizations of the Global FoodBanking Network reached 40.5 million people in 2025, a 6.5 percent rise from the year before, new network-wide data released this week shows. Farm donations of surplus produce grew 55 percent to 228,000 tonnes, with fruit and vegetables making up nearly half of everything distributed. The group estimated the waste prevented had the same climate benefit as taking 473,000 vehicles off the road for a year, said CEO Lisa Moon.

Quiet Kindness · Powhatan, Virginia

A Virginia man known for playing Santa Claus donated a kidney to a stranger.

Dave Bell, of Powhatan, underwent surgery at Henrico Doctors' Hospital on July 29 after losing 40 pounds to qualify as a living donor. "I have two of these things, and I only need one. There's somebody who has none and they need one," he said. Transplant surgeon Dr. Amanda Bailey said stories like his help close the gap left by a shortage of kidneys from deceased donors.

Science & Medicine · Hong Kong

Children who had less sugar in their first two years carried a lower dementia risk decades later.

Researchers tracked 64,737 people born before and after Britain's wartime sugar rationing ended in 1953, comparing those with restricted sugar in utero and early childhood to those without. People rationed before birth through age two had a 23 percent lower risk of developing dementia, with onset arriving 2.6 years later on average, according to the study in Neurology led by Jiazhen Zheng of the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. Brain scans of the low-sugar group also showed more gray matter and fewer markers of tissue damage.

Everyday Joy · Dimapur, Nagaland, India

An Ao community in Nagaland revived a tradition of communal labor before its harvest festival.

Volunteers in Signal Angami village, Dimapur, spent July 30 clearing drainage, cutting weeds and removing garbage ahead of Tsüngremmung, reviving a custom once central to Ao festivals like Moatsü and Tsüngremmung. The collective cleanup used to ensure clear paths to fields and wells before the feasting began, a practice organizers felt had faded as festivals became just about food. The initiative aimed to remind younger residents that celebration in Ao culture has always paired work with unity.

Nature · KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa

Six rhinos hand-raised after poachers killed their mothers have each given birth in the wild.

Ezemvelo KZN Wildlife rescued the six white rhino calves around 2017, rehabilitated them for four to five years, and confirmed this week that all six have since bred successfully back in the wild.

Science & Medicine · Basque Country, Spain

A twice-yearly stool test has cut colon cancer deaths in half across the Basque Country.

A screening programme launched in 2009 lowered colorectal cancer mortality among people 50 to 69 from 39.7 to 19.8 deaths per 100,000, a 21-year study of nearly 2.2 million people found.

Global Progress · Indonesia

Indonesia's poverty rate fell to its lowest level in years.

Indonesia's poverty rate dropped to 8.07 percent in March 2026, down from 8.25 percent six months earlier, meaning 430,000 fewer people below the poverty line, Statistics Indonesia reported.

A short, honest note about what this is.

Uplift is a daily publication of six genuinely good things happening in the world — reported by other people, summarized clearly, and linked back to the original source. That's the whole thing.

It started, plainly, because the news diet available to most of us is a poor reflection of what is actually happening. The world is unfinished, and a lot of it is going wrong; but a lot of it is also going quietly right, and almost nobody is paid to notice. Uplift is one person, then two, then a small team, trying to notice — carefully.

Every morning at dawn, six stories appear at uplift.daily. Each is summarized in three sentences, credited to the publication that did the reporting, and linked out clearly. If a story is worth your time, it is worth the original reporter's byline.

§ 01 — The shape of the thingWhat Uplift is, and isn't.

It is

  • Six stories a day, by email
  • Summaries that respect the original reporting
  • A link out to the primary source, always
  • A photograph on every story, thematic and credited
  • A browsable archive, no paywall
  • Reader-supported, one day

It isn't

  • A wellness brand
  • A doom-scroll antidote
  • A feel-good aggregator
  • A politically-coded publication
  • A place for crime, war, or celebrity news
  • A site with popups, pixels, or guilt

§ 02 — Editorial principlesHow we choose stories.

i.

The test is simple.

Before we include a story, we ask: does this make someone feel better about the world without manipulating them? If the uplift comes from fear, or from someone else's suffering, it's the wrong story.

ii.

We don't replace the source.

Uplift is a curator. Our three-sentence summaries exist to point you toward the original reporting, not to stand in for it. Every story links out; every photograph is credited; the work belongs to the people who did it.

iii.

No heroes of last resort.

We don't cast police, militaries, or corporations as moral heroes. We don't run stories whose uplift depends on a prior catastrophe ("brave survivor of terrible thing"). We prefer quiet competence to dramatic rescue.

iv.

Specific, not saccharine.

We write factually. If an effort worked, we say what it did and who did it. If the numbers are modest, we give the modest numbers. Warmth is earned by specifics, not adjectives.

v.

We don't compete for your attention.

One email a day. No push notifications, no retention emails, no "you haven't read us in 30 days." When you want to come back, we'll be here.

§ 03 — A way inWrite to us.

We read every note, and we especially want to hear about local stories we'd otherwise miss — schools, clinics, co-ops, small restorations, ordinary kindness done at scale. If you work in newsroom communications, we welcome pitches too.

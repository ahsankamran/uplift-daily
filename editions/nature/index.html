Nature · Indus River, Pakistan · September 30, 2026

Pakistan joined its Indus river dolphin sanctuaries into one 400-kilometre protected corridor.

The Punjnad Indus River Dolphin Sanctuary, created in June 2026, protects nearly 200 kilometres of river in Punjab and connects with existing reserves in Sindh. A 2025 study estimated 1,987 dolphins, up from 617 in 2001, with 99 percent living in a 690-kilometre stretch between the Chashma and Sukkur barrages. Gillnets caused 52 percent of documented deaths from 2008 to 2023, and they remain the main threat the new protections must address.

Ene River, Peru · September 29, 2026

Asháninka fire brigades brought wildfires in 45 Peruvian communities down to zero.

Across 45 Asháninka communities in Peru's Ene River region, wildfires fell from 25 in 2023 to nine in 2024, and none were recorded in 2025. Community members trained as certified firefighters watch for smoke by satellite, drone and radio under the Paamari programme, run by the Central Asháninka of the Ene River with the charity Cool Earth. Burned area dropped from 2,648 hectares to 492 in a year when fires across Peru destroyed 63,000 hectares.

Taï National Park, Côte d’Ivoire · September 26, 2026

A bird unseen for thirteen years reappeared in an Ivorian rainforest.

Field researchers photographed a Ghana cuckooshrike in Côte d'Ivoire's Taï National Park in June, the species' first confirmed sighting since 2013. Stephanie Bartlett and Paulo Ditzel of the International Bird Conservation Partnership spent six months surveying West Africa's forests before capturing what may be the best photographs ever taken of the bird. The team plans to return to Taï within the year to survey further and strengthen ties with the local researchers protecting the park's shrinking forest.

Punjab and Sindh, Pakistan · September 25, 2026

Pakistan linked two river reserves into a single 400-kilometer sanctuary for the world's rarest dolphin.

The government's new Punjnad Indus River Dolphin Sanctuary now connects with an existing reserve in Sindh, giving the critically endangered, functionally blind species a continuous protected corridor along the river. A 2025 survey counted roughly 1,987 of the dolphins, up from a low of 617 in 2001, though gillnets still cause more than half of recorded deaths. WWF-Pakistan biologist Uzma Khan says the connected corridor lets dolphins move between feeding grounds without crossing the busiest fishing waters.

Kerguelen Islands · September 24, 2026

Genetic testing revealed a fourth gentoo penguin species hiding on a remote sub-Antarctic island.

An international team analyzing genomes, body size, calls, and breeding schedules confirmed that gentoo penguins on France's remote Kerguelen Islands form their own species, Pygoscelis kerguelensis — the first new penguin species named in more than a century. The researchers, publishing in Communications Biology, found that three populations long treated as mere subspecies are genetically distinct enough to split the familiar gentoo penguin into four separate species. Conservationists say the finer-grained map gives each population, several already vulnerable to warming seas, a clearer claim to its own protection.

East Antarctica · September 23, 2026

A tropical heat pool sent 695 billion tons of extra snow onto East Antarctica.

Between 2021 and 2023, satellites recorded East Antarctica's largest ice-mass gain on record, equal to about four years of the continent's average annual loss. Researcher Qinghua Ding traced the cause to unusual warming in the tropical Pacific-Indian "warm pool," which sent an atmospheric river of moisture toward Queen Mary Land and Wilkes Land as heavy snow. Only 9 percent of the anomaly was linked to human-caused warming, and researchers say the gain is temporary, not a reversal of the ice sheet's long-term decline.

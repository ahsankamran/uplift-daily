Nature · Al Qurm Wa Lehhfaiiah Protected Area, Sharjah, United Arab Emirates · November 13, 2026

Young green turtles have settled into a lagoon dug for a waterfront that was never built.

Nine juvenile green turtles tracked on the Emirati coast of the Gulf of Oman spent more than 80% of their time inside the Al Qurm Wa Lehhfaiiah wetland, and were about five times more likely to be found in a basin excavated in 2003 for a waterfront development that was later scaled back. The Sharjah Environment and Protected Areas Authority and the University of Exeter, writing in Aquatic Conservation, put that down to an algae-covered floor to graze and almost no sharks able to reach in. Lead author Owen Exeter's team argues the basin only works because mangrove channels still connect it to the sea on the tide, and notes that one tracked turtle, once grown, swam more than 700 kilometres south to central Oman.

Phillip Island, Victoria, Australia · November 12, 2026

Bush stone-curlews have hatched chicks on Phillip Island for the first time in 50 years.

Wild bush stone-curlew chicks have hatched on Phillip Island, off the Victorian coast, for the first time in more than half a century. Phillip Island Nature Parks has released over 70 of the birds since 2024 alongside the Australian National University, the Odonata Foundation and the Penguin Foundation, and added 12 more on November 4 drawn from five wildlife sanctuaries around the country. Paula Wasiak of ANU calls the hatching a milestone for the programme, and Duncan Sutherland, who runs it for the Nature Parks, says the point now is a population genetically diverse and sturdy enough to carry on by itself.

Llanos de Challe National Park, Atacama, Chile · November 11, 2026

More than 200 kinds of flower have come up at once in the Atacama.

The driest desert on Earth is in colour again around Llanos de Challe National Park in northern Chile, where more than 200 plant species are flowering after winter rain of over 150 millimetres in places, the heaviest since 1997. Moisés Grimberg, who administers the park, describes the ground as a seed bank that only needs water, and botanists from Chile's national seed bank are out collecting specimens from patches no one has recorded in flower before. The park usually takes about 10,000 visitors a year and has had 15,000 in a few weeks; by the middle of this month the flowers will dry, drop their seed back into the sand and wait for the next rain.

Taro, Bali, Indonesia · November 10, 2026

A Balinese farmer is breeding fireflies back into the fields he grew up beside.

Wayan Wardika watched fireflies over his village's rice fields as a boy and then watched them go, thinned out by lighting, pesticides and building; since founding Bring Back the Light in 2020 he has released close to 20,000 larvae and adults, and the counts around Taro are climbing again. Lead scientist Cindy Chayani's team raises the larvae in purpose-built terrariums for up to two years and lets about 90 percent of the adults go, while Wardika composts manure from a facility that keeps Bali's sacred white cattle and brews a pesticide from fermented market waste. Breeding alone will not hold them, so he is trying to move 35 neighbouring farmers off agricultural chemicals — four so far, each given free compost and paid back for what the changeover costs them — because, as he puts it, if we don't see fireflies, there is something wrong with the ecosystem.

Ascea, Campania, Italy · November 8, 2026

Loggerhead turtles keep nesting further west, and Italy's beach clubs are learning to share the sand.

Loggerheads that for decades nested almost only in Greece, Türkiye and Cyprus now come ashore along Italy, France and Spain, where the first confirmed Spanish nest was found in 2001 and nesting has been annual since 2014. Sandra Hochscheid of the Stazione Zoologica Anton Dohrn follows nesting females by satellite, and Carlos Carreras at the University of Barcelona has traced the newcomers' genomes back to eastern Mediterranean and Caribbean populations; a study in Campania found 87 percent of nests in 2022 and 2023 lay within reach of beachfront lighting, and 82 percent needed someone to step in so the hatchlings went seaward rather than inland. Owners of the lidi who once brushed the tracks away now telephone them in, and Ascea, which has already dimmed its promenade, will require low-impact lighting on every beach concession from 2027.

Tawi-Tawi, Philippines · November 7, 2026

Horseshoe crabs have been confirmed in a second Philippine province.

Researchers interviewed 142 residents across four municipalities of Tawi-Tawi and logged sightings in Bongao, Simunul, Sitangkai and Panglima Sugala, the first record of the animals anywhere in the Philippines outside Palawan. Ivane Pedrosa-Gerasmio of Mindanao State University–Iligan Institute of Technology first heard of them at a Bongao market in December 2023; residents call them bangkas, after the shape of a boat, and the two kinds described resemble the endangered tri-spine and the mangrove horseshoe crab. More than half of those interviewed believe numbers are falling, and her student Quesie Tolero Omnos argues the new record should guide what local councils choose to protect.

Nature · Seal Beach and Huntington Beach, California, USA · November 17, 2026

The first sea turtles known to hatch on the US West Coast have reached the water.

Biologists moved more than 200 olive ridley eggs from two nests found in September at Seal Beach and Huntington Beach — the first natural sea turtle nests ever confirmed on the US West Coast — into incubators at the Aquarium of the Pacific as a tropical storm came in. The sand in the Seal Beach container cracked and sank on 12 November, the first hatchling broke through at 7:15 pm the next evening, 54 followed over the next hours, and staff carried them down to their own beach before sunrise on Saturday, with the Huntington Beach group released on Sunday; every one of them made it into the surf. Fifty eggs from the Seal Beach nest have still not hatched and are being watched, and no one yet knows whether these nests were a single accident of weather or the start of something, since the nearest known olive ridley beaches are in Mexico.

Bikar and Bokak atolls, Marshall Islands · November 15, 2026

Two atolls no one lives on hold some of the healthiest reefs left in the Pacific.

An expedition to Bikar and Bokak, the northernmost atolls of the Marshall Islands and 116 and 385 kilometres from the nearest inhabited land, confirmed 15 breeding seabird colonies and counted 132 green turtle nest pits on Bikar alone. Alan Friedlander of the University of Hawai'i, working with the Marshall Islands Marine Resources Authority and National Geographic Pristine Seas, surveyed from the shoreline to the deep sea by scuba, drop camera and submersible, recording 266 kinds of reef fish, 138 more at mesophotic depths and 79 in deep water — 14 of them never recorded in the country before and two possibly new to science. Two years after the fieldwork, about 48,000 square kilometres around the atolls became the country's first national marine sanctuary, closed to fishing under Reimaanlok, the Marshallese framework for community-led conservation.

Kozhikode, Kerala, India · November 14, 2026

More than 27,000 orchids were lifted off felled trees and tied onto new ones.

Researchers in Kerala gathered 27,324 orchids from trees that had been cut, or were marked for cutting, to make way for road building in the Western Ghats, then fastened them to trees at a beach, an urban wetland park and a botanical garden. After one to two years about 71% were still alive, among them more than half of the 6,126 plants belonging to 12 species found nowhere else, and many of those flowered and set seed capsules despite having come from cooler hill forests. Ten species at Sarovaram Bio Park now carry QR codes that visitors have scanned 126,000 times in two years, though co-author Viswambharan Sarasan of Kew says moving plants should stay a last resort for when the tree itself cannot be kept.

Al Qurm Wa Lehhfaiiah Protected Area, Sharjah, United Arab Emirates · November 13, 2026

Young green turtles have settled into a lagoon dug for a waterfront that was never built.

Nine juvenile green turtles tracked on the Emirati coast of the Gulf of Oman spent more than 80% of their time inside the Al Qurm Wa Lehhfaiiah wetland, and were about five times more likely to be found in a basin excavated in 2003 for a waterfront development that was later scaled back. The Sharjah Environment and Protected Areas Authority and the University of Exeter, writing in Aquatic Conservation, put that down to an algae-covered floor to graze and almost no sharks able to reach in. Lead author Owen Exeter's team argues the basin only works because mangrove channels still connect it to the sea on the tide, and notes that one tracked turtle, once grown, swam more than 700 kilometres south to central Oman.

Phillip Island, Victoria, Australia · November 12, 2026

Bush stone-curlews have hatched chicks on Phillip Island for the first time in 50 years.

Wild bush stone-curlew chicks have hatched on Phillip Island, off the Victorian coast, for the first time in more than half a century. Phillip Island Nature Parks has released over 70 of the birds since 2024 alongside the Australian National University, the Odonata Foundation and the Penguin Foundation, and added 12 more on November 4 drawn from five wildlife sanctuaries around the country. Paula Wasiak of ANU calls the hatching a milestone for the programme, and Duncan Sutherland, who runs it for the Nature Parks, says the point now is a population genetically diverse and sturdy enough to carry on by itself.

Llanos de Challe National Park, Atacama, Chile · November 11, 2026

More than 200 kinds of flower have come up at once in the Atacama.

The driest desert on Earth is in colour again around Llanos de Challe National Park in northern Chile, where more than 200 plant species are flowering after winter rain of over 150 millimetres in places, the heaviest since 1997. Moisés Grimberg, who administers the park, describes the ground as a seed bank that only needs water, and botanists from Chile's national seed bank are out collecting specimens from patches no one has recorded in flower before. The park usually takes about 10,000 visitors a year and has had 15,000 in a few weeks; by the middle of this month the flowers will dry, drop their seed back into the sand and wait for the next rain.

Human Spirit · Esperance to Bunbury, Australia · November 17, 2026
Photo: Antonio Vivace, via Unsplash

A Bunbury retail worker drove 1,400 kilometres through the night to get a stranded turtle into care.

A juvenile loggerhead turtle found floating in seaweed off Esperance, on the south coast of Western Australia, is now recovering in a heated tank at the Dolphin Discovery Centre in Bunbury, almost 700 kilometres away. Letesha Heslewood, who works in retail in Bunbury, answered an urgent late-night call-out and made the 16-hour round trip herself, pulling over every couple of hours to splash fresh water over the turtle's eyes and back as the wildlife hospital staff had shown her. The turtle, nicknamed Shaggy for the thick green algae on his shell, will stay in care until April 2027 before being taken north for release off the Ningaloo coast, and Heslewood is registering as an official volunteer wildlife transport officer.

Luma, Rukum East, Nepal · November 15, 2026

A 15-year-old paid for her village's new footbridge out of her Facebook earnings.

A four-metre wooden footbridge now crosses the Domai stream at Luma, in Bhume Rural Municipality-9 of Nepal's Rukum East district, replacing a crossing that washed away in June. Sadiksha Oli, 15, covered the Rs60,000 cost herself from her monetised Facebook videos, buying timber, corrugated iron sheets and nails and paying the wages of the skilled workers, while relatives and neighbours worked alongside her to finish the span in three days. The Domai is an easy step across in the dry months and swells past wading in the monsoon, and the children who walk that way to school now have a deck to cross before the next rains.

Bengaluru, India · November 14, 2026

A Bengaluru teenager and five classmates turned a school storage room into a 2,000-book library.

A disused storage room at Mullur Colony Government School in Bengaluru now holds more than 2,000 donated books, in a school that until then had only textbooks. Asavari Agrawal, 17, and five fellow students gathered the books from schools and neighbours over nearly two years, then redid the flooring, repainted the walls and put in shelves and furniture, choosing titles the children had asked for. She pays for the project, Shape the Future, out of her table tennis prize money and the sale of her piano, and has split the Saturday visits among about 50 student volunteers, with separate heads for fundraising and operations, so they carry on once she leaves for university.

Okobo, Akwa Ibom, Nigeria · November 13, 2026

A minibus driver in Akwa Ibom kept a passenger's 400,000 naira untouched for 11 months.

Nse Ekpenyong found a parcel holding 400,000 naira on the floor of his commercial minibus on 17 December 2025, after carrying passengers from Okobo to Uyo and making trips to Itam Market in Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria. He waited at the terminal for most of a day, asked the other drivers to pass on his number, and announced the find on a local radio station without saying that the parcel held money, so that no impostor could claim it; nobody came forward, and the bundles stayed hidden at his house for 11 months, including three weeks when a breakdown he could not afford to fix kept his bus off the road. A state official sent to check found the 400,000 naira still intact, and the Akwa Ibom government has since given Ekpenyong, a father of four, a furnished three-bedroom house with solar power and running water.

Melbourne, Australia · November 12, 2026

A Bristol woman tracked down the Melbourne family in her father's 1965 home movie.

Karen Walsh came close to throwing out an 8mm reel her father, Arthur Howes, shot on a suburban Melbourne street in 1965, and instead emailed the clip, which runs under a minute, to 774 ABC Melbourne to ask who the family in it were. Volkswagen Club of Victoria historian Chris Scane identified the green van as a standard split-window 1962 Kombi manufactured in Clayton, and a school archivist ruled out one college from the boys' uniforms, before Sydney medical student Zac de Boeck recognised five of his own relatives and rang the talkback line. Three generations of his family, the Vanners of Mitcham, have now been named, and Walsh, who says she is the curator of the footage rather than its owner, wants the film to go to the people who were filmed.

Mitta Mitta, Australia · November 11, 2026

A Mitta Mitta couple have kept the town's weather record by hand for 63 years.

Ernie and Merrillyn Cole have been recording the weather for Australia's Bureau of Meteorology from the same house in Mitta Mitta, a north-east Victorian valley town of about 170 people, since 1963. Merrillyn Cole, 82, took on the readings because her husband left early for his forestry job, kept them going while raising a baby and a toddler, and still writes the rain totals out by hand, and a neighbour, retired pilot Russell Kelly, reads the gauge when the couple are away. They have now asked the bureau for an automatic station so they can travel more, and the bureau says it will train a replacement volunteer if one comes forward, which matters in a valley where rainfall can vary by hundreds of millimetres within 10 kilometres.

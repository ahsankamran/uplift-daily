Human Spirit · Melbourne, Australia · November 12, 2026
Photo: Denise Jans, via Unsplash

A Bristol woman tracked down the Melbourne family in her father's 1965 home movie.

Karen Walsh came close to throwing out an 8mm reel her father, Arthur Howes, shot on a suburban Melbourne street in 1965, and instead emailed the clip, which runs under a minute, to 774 ABC Melbourne to ask who the family in it were. Volkswagen Club of Victoria historian Chris Scane identified the green van as a standard split-window 1962 Kombi manufactured in Clayton, and a school archivist ruled out one college from the boys' uniforms, before Sydney medical student Zac de Boeck recognised five of his own relatives and rang the talkback line. Three generations of his family, the Vanners of Mitcham, have now been named, and Walsh, who says she is the curator of the footage rather than its owner, wants the film to go to the people who were filmed.

Mitta Mitta, Australia · November 11, 2026

A Mitta Mitta couple have kept the town's weather record by hand for 63 years.

Ernie and Merrillyn Cole have been recording the weather for Australia's Bureau of Meteorology from the same house in Mitta Mitta, a north-east Victorian valley town of about 170 people, since 1963. Merrillyn Cole, 82, took on the readings because her husband left early for his forestry job, kept them going while raising a baby and a toddler, and still writes the rain totals out by hand, and a neighbour, retired pilot Russell Kelly, reads the gauge when the couple are away. They have now asked the bureau for an automatic station so they can travel more, and the bureau says it will train a replacement volunteer if one comes forward, which matters in a valley where rainfall can vary by hundreds of millimetres within 10 kilometres.

Boksburg, South Africa · November 10, 2026

A Boksburg grower cleared a dumping ground by hand and now gives the method away free every Tuesday.

Innocent Sukazi asked his local councillor for an unused, rubbish-strewn plot on Kukuku Street in Boksburg, east of Johannesburg, cleared it himself and planted 500 spinach seedlings on it in April 2022. The plot has grown into 2.4 hectares farmed as Sukazi Agriculture, carrying potatoes, tomatoes and spinach year-round with sweet potatoes, peppers and onions in rotation, and it now supports two full-time workers and six part-time helpers through the summer. Every Tuesday evening he teaches the method in a free live session for growers across South Africa and Eswatini, and the compost and seedlings he raises himself let him sell produce at lower farm-gate prices than bought-in stock would allow.

Rukum West, Nepal · November 8, 2026

Six men working in the United States paid for a kilometre of road to their village in Nepal.

Six men from Sani Bheri Rural Municipality-3, all working in the United States, put Rs 207,000 of their own money into a one-kilometre road track from Duabari to Gharillo Bhala Chaur in Nepal's Rukum West district. The track shortens a journey villagers previously made the long way round and makes it easier to move goods and livestock; in Musikot Municipality-1, in the same district, Lalbir Bohara separately spent more than Rs 150,000 finishing a second kilometre from Chyandanda to Gairabari. More than 50 households in the Chhelo Khelne and Gairabari settlements use Bohara's stretch, and a 74-year-old neighbour, Palbhan Nakal, said the men had taken responsibility for a village they no longer live in.

Cheviot, New Zealand · November 7, 2026

A Cheviot mechanic is rebuilding donated bikes into fleets for North Canterbury schools.

Nick Watson handed eight rebuilt push bikes to Cheviot Area School in July and says he has another pile ready for a second school in New Zealand's Hurunui district. He runs Sausage Dog Cycles from a workshop on Leader Road north of Cheviot, gives restored bikes away on what he calls Free Bike Fridays and strips the ones beyond saving for parts, while the Hurunui District Council road safety programme supplies helmets to go with them. The council paid for a two-hour maintenance class he taught at Waiau School last term, a second is planned for Cheviot Area School this term, and he will be at the Amberley Farmers Market on 14 November doing free safety checks.

Delhi, India · November 4, 2026

A Delhi theatre fellowship pays its trainees 15,000 rupees a month to learn the craft.

Bargad, a free six-month theatre fellowship in Delhi, will pay each of the 14 artists in its January 2027 cohort 15,000 rupees a month, up from the 10,000 rupees its first two cohorts received. Amba Suhasini K Jhala and Anirudh Nair started it in 2025 with two other Delhi theatre-makers, running classes in Hindi from 4pm to 8.30pm six days a week and funding the whole thing from roughly 20 to 25 individual donors; the stipend exists so that applicants from outside theatre families can tell their relatives the work pays. Applications grew from about 75 in the first year to around 200 in the second, and one graduate has since entered the National School of Drama.

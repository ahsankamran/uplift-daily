Science & Medicine · 74 centres in six countries · November 3, 2026

Replacing a moderately narrowed heart valve early keeps people out of hospital.

The PROGRESS trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, randomised 744 people aged 65 and over — average age 78 — with moderate aortic stenosis and at least one worrying sign, at 74 centres in six countries, either to a transcatheter valve replacement now or to careful monitoring. After two years, 16.7 percent of the valve group had died or had a heart failure event against 23.9 percent of those monitored, and admissions for heart failure ran at 7.4 percent against 14.9 percent; more than half the monitored group had a valve replaced within the two years anyway. The investigators are careful to say this does not mean every moderate narrowing should be treated, and they will follow both groups for ten years to see how the valves last.

35 stroke centres, China · November 2, 2026

A clot can be pulled from the brain three days after a stroke, a Chinese trial finds.

The LATE-MT trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled 336 adults at 35 Chinese stroke centres whose stroke came from a blocked large vessel, and gave half of them mechanical thrombectomy between 24 and 72 hours after symptoms began — a window usually treated as shut. At 90 days, 49 percent of the thrombectomy group were living independently against 35 percent of those on medication alone, and 26.9 percent had no significant disability against 15.6 percent. The procedure carries a real cost at that remove: bleeding in the brain affected 4.8 percent of treated patients and none of the others, and deaths ran at 6.0 percent against 1.8 percent, so the authors present the result as widening who might be considered rather than as routine practice.

Beijing, China · November 1, 2026

A vitamin B5 derivative sold as a supplement eased psoriasis in a small trial.

Researchers in Beijing report in the Journal of Experimental Medicine that pantethine, an inexpensive derivative of vitamin B5, relieved psoriasis in mice and improved symptoms in a 24-person trial over twelve weeks, with every patient also using the standard calcipotriene-and-betamethasone ointment. The work, co-led by Wei-Na Zhang of the State Key Laboratory of Biomedical Analysis and Cheng-Xin Li of the First Medical Center of the Chinese PLA General Hospital, traces the effect to a vanin-pantethine pathway that changes how dendritic cells behave, and found the clearest benefit in patients whose blood cholesterol was raised. The team calls the human half of the study proof of concept rather than proof of mechanism, and wants larger trials before anyone changes a prescription.

Montreal, Canada · October 4, 2026

A diet-and-exercise program reversed early-onset type 2 diabetes in most people who tried it.

In a trial spanning Canada and England, 54 percent of young adults with early-onset type 2 diabetes reached remission after 24 weeks on a supervised low-calorie diet and exercise program, compared with 4 percent given usual care. Researchers led by McGill University's Kaberi Dasgupta and the University of Leicester's Thomas Yates enrolled 96 adults aged 18 to 45, pairing an 800-to-900-calorie daily diet with twice-weekly aerobic and resistance training, and participants lost an average of 8.4 kilograms while preserving muscle mass. The results, published in The Lancet Regional Health – Americas and presented at the European Association for the Study of Diabetes meeting in Milan, suggest structured lifestyle programs could become a standard option for younger patients before the disease becomes harder to reverse.

Manchester, England · September 30, 2026

A simple cell test could spare many women with postmenopausal bleeding an invasive check for womb cancer.

The DETECT study tested urine and vaginal fluid from 1,864 women, 115 of whom had cancer, and the combined test found over 80 percent of cancers, including 95.8 percent of high-grade tumours. Its negative predictive value for womb cancer was 98.8 percent, so a clear result is very likely to be right. Professor Emma Davidson, who led the work, said further studies in routine practice are needed before it reaches clinics.

Seoul, South Korea · September 29, 2026

Blood thinners cut serious events by 69 percent in a middle-risk group of atrial fibrillation patients.

The SINGLE-AF trial followed 1,803 people with atrial fibrillation at intermediate stroke risk, comparing direct oral anticoagulants with no anticoagulation. Serious events, including stroke, major bleeding and cardiovascular death, occurred in 0.5 percent of those on the drugs against 1.5 percent of those without, and ischemic stroke in 0.1 percent against 1.1 percent. Professor Boyoung Joung of Yonsei University, who led the trial, said the New England Journal of Medicine results may inform guidelines and reimbursement.

A multicentre trial · November 18, 2026

Two arthritis drugs were tested against each other rather than against a placebo.

In a phase 3b trial of 553 adults with active psoriatic arthritis, 55% of those given bimekizumab reached at least a 50% improvement by six months against 44% of those given risankizumab, with the gap at four months running 50% to 38%. Direct comparisons are rare here, because new biologics are normally measured only against a dummy injection, which leaves rheumatologists ranking drugs by inference. Serious side effects stayed under 2% on bimekizumab and near 3% on risankizumab, and candida infections were slightly more common on the first — the sort of detail only a head-to-head can supply.

Sweden · November 17, 2026

A phone game helped antidepressants do more of their work.

In a Swedish trial of 148 adults who were still depressed while taking prescribed antidepressants, 51% of those given Fig — a game built around cognitive behavioural therapy, with check-ins twice a day — cut their depression scores by at least 30% over nine weeks, against 18.6% of a waitlist group. Josefin Brehmer's team, writing in the Journal of Affective Disorders, also recorded a larger fall in anxiety and steadier day-to-day adherence to medication, although fewer than a third of players ever messaged a therapist or finished one of the optional written exercises. Dropout was high — 40.8% of the Fig group left before the nine-week survey, against 18.1% of controls — and the authors call the findings preliminary, with nothing yet on whether the gains hold once people stop playing.

An international trial · November 15, 2026

Pacing the heart through its own wiring kept more people out of hospital.

Among nearly 700 adults with atrioventricular block who needed a pacemaker, 8.5% of those given left bundle-branch pacing died, were admitted for heart failure or developed pacing-induced cardiomyopathy, against 19.2% of those paced from the right ventricle. Jiangang Zou's international team followed them for a median of 35 months; none in the left bundle-branch group developed the weakened pumping that conventional pacing can bring on, compared with 11.7% of the others. An independent committee stopped the OptimPacing trial early for benefit, which the authors note can overstate an effect, and the full results are in the New England Journal of Medicine.

Source: The New England Journal of Medicine
São Paulo, Brazil · November 14, 2026

Pushing blood pressure lower after a stroke prevents more of them.

Among 4,368 adults who had already had an ischaemic stroke or a transient attack, treating to a systolic target below 120 mmHg left 10.9% with a further cardiovascular event over a median of 3.9 years, against 13.5% of those held to the usual target below 140. Gisele Sampaio Silva of Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein in São Paulo led the trial across 30 sites, and reports in the New England Journal of Medicine that average pressure settled at 122 mmHg in the intensive group against 135 in the standard one. Kidney function declined more often with the lower target, so the authors ask that anyone treated this way be watched for it.

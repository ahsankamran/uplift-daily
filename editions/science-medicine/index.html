Science & Medicine · An international trial · November 12, 2026

A personalised cancer vaccine cut melanoma's return by two-fifths.

In 1,137 people whose stage IIB to IV melanoma had been removed by surgery, adding a vaccine built from the mutations in each patient's own tumour to the immunotherapy pembrolizumab lowered the risk of recurrence or death by 42%. Two years on, 82.2% of the vaccine group had had no recurrence against 70.0% of those on pembrolizumab alone, and 88.5% were free of distant spread against 80.2%. The INTerpath-001 trial, run by Merck and Moderna and published in the New England Journal of Medicine on November 11, is the first phase 3 result of its kind for a personalised mRNA cancer vaccine; survival figures are not yet mature.

Budapest, Hungary · November 11, 2026

Talking back to a hallucinated voice through an avatar quieted it for 13 of 30 people.

Thirty people with schizophrenia who had been hearing voices for at least three months despite medication were followed for 12 weeks on medication alone, then given seven sessions in which a therapist spoke through an on-screen avatar built to match the voice they heard. Voices came less often and distressed them less: 13 of the 30 improved meaningfully, two stopped hearing them altogether and four more saw their symptoms at least halve. Luca Egervári's group at Semmelweis University, writing in Acta Psychologica, is careful about what this is — an addition to medication, not a replacement, tested without a control group and needing a larger trial.

Perth, Western Australia · November 10, 2026

Lifting weights three times a week changed what people lost on a weight-loss drug.

In a 40-week randomised trial, 120 people aged 50 to 70 taking tirzepatide for weight management lost almost identical amounts — 22.3 kg with resistance training, 22.6 kg without — but the half who trained kept about a quarter more of their lean tissue. Strength moved in opposite directions: up roughly 10 percent, upper and lower body, in the group that trained three times a week, and down roughly 10 percent in the group that did not. Daniel Green at the University of Western Australia led the T-REx trial, published in Nature Medicine; everyone taking part got the same advice on protein, fibre and water, so the training is the difference.

Boston, Massachusetts, USA · November 8, 2026

A single iron infusion let people with the commonest heart failure walk further.

Sixty-five people who had heart failure with preserved ejection fraction — the most common form, and the one with fewest treatments — together with iron deficiency were randomly given either one 1,000 mg infusion of ferric derisomaltose or a placebo. Twelve weeks later the iron group's peak oxygen uptake had risen about 10 percent while the placebo group's had not moved; they also covered more ground in a six-minute walk and moved up the scale doctors use to grade how much their symptoms limit them. Gregory Lewis and Rajeev Malhotra of Mass General Brigham published the trial in Circulation; it is small, the group was mostly white, self-reported health scores did not separate, and no serious harm was traced to the iron.

Flint, Michigan, USA · November 7, 2026

Flint halved its newborn deaths after it began paying every pregnant resident.

Rx Kids gives every pregnant woman in the city $1,500 and then $500 a month through a baby's first year; deaths in the first 28 days fell from 10.2 to 5.1 per 1,000 live births, and deaths in the first week from 7.9 to 2.0. Mona Hanna and colleagues at Michigan State University compared state birth and death records from 2021 to 2025 and published the figures in JAMA Pediatrics; the rest of Michigan moved from 4.0 to 3.9 over the same years. Flint's gap with the rest of the state is no longer statistically significant, though the study watched what happened rather than testing it, and cannot show the money is the reason.

Denmark · November 5, 2026

Two cholesterol tablets together shrank artery plaque by a fifth.

A Danish trial of 104 adults who had stable chest pain and narrowed coronary arteries, and had never taken a statin, found that 18 months of rosuvastatin plus ezetimibe cut total plaque volume by 21.1 percent, against 13.6 percent on atorvastatin alone. Martin Bødtker Mortensen and colleagues scanned everyone by coronary CT at the start, at nine months and at the end; LDL cholesterol fell 65 percent on the two drugs and 38 percent on the one, and the results are in JAMA Cardiology. The soft, fat-rich plaque most likely to rupture receded in 51 of the 52 people taking the pair, though the trial was far too small to say whether that prevents heart attacks.

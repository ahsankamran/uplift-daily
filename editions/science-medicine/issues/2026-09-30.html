Science & Medicine · Manchester, England

A simple cell test could spare many women with postmenopausal bleeding an invasive check for womb cancer.

The DETECT study tested urine and vaginal fluid from 1,864 women, 115 of whom had cancer, and the combined test found over 80 percent of cancers, including 95.8 percent of high-grade tumours. Its negative predictive value for womb cancer was 98.8 percent, so a clear result is very likely to be right. Professor Emma Davidson, who led the work, said further studies in routine practice are needed before it reaches clinics.

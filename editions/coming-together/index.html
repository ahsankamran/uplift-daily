Coming Together · Rathangan, County Wexford, Ireland · November 19, 2026
Photo: Samuel Solcan, via Unsplash

A Wexford vintage club raised €85,358 for a church roof by rebuilding one old tractor.

Rathangan Vintage Club has raised €85,358 towards re-roofing Rathangan Church in south County Wexford, through a draw for a restored Massey Ferguson 165 tractor and a Honda 50 motorcycle. Members spent months rebuilding the tractor and then spent the year selling tickets at events around Ireland, and Bishop Ger Nash drew the winners at Rathangan Bar, sending the tractor to Markie Redmond from Duncormick and the Honda 50 to Helen Futlong from Adamstown. The village carries the run into next month with a Christmas concert in the church on 11 December and, the following evening, a children's light-up circuit of decorated bikes, go-karts and buggies around it.

Caerphilly, Wales · November 18, 2026

Seventy-five volunteer knitters in Caerphilly finished a record 180 blankets this year.

The Craft and Chat group at Caerphilly Miners Centre for the Community handed over 180 hand-knitted blankets on 16 November, its biggest year in a decade and up from 140 last year and about 44 three years ago. Around 75 volunteers knit at the centre and at home, logging more than 6,000 hours over the year at roughly 35 hours a blanket, and Councillor Carol Andrews presented the pile for distribution to long-stay patients at Ysbyty Ystrad Fawr, a care home, a church and a youth advocacy service. James Grashoff, who chairs the Miners Centre, says the sessions do as much to bring people together as the blankets do to keep anyone warm, and the group is now asking which other local charities could use them.

Helston, England · November 17, 2026

Ten volunteers have spent five weekends putting up Helston's 2,000 Christmas bulbs themselves.

Ten volunteers have given up five weekends to rig the Christmas lights across Helston in Cornwall, testing roughly 2,000 bulbs and fixing 80 trees to shop fronts ahead of the switch-on on 27 November. Robert Williams, who first helped at 18 and has rigged the lights for 40 years, leads a crew that starts at 6am to stay clear of traffic and gets through about 15,000 cable ties and 20,000 attachment rings each year. They work to a £6,000 budget from the town council, against the £30,000 to £50,000 contractors would charge, and six restored displays spanning Coinagehall Street return for the first time since the pandemic.

Frankenthal, Germany · November 14, 2026

About 2,400 people cleared three tonnes of litter out of one German town.

About 2,400 people collected roughly three tonnes of litter from the streets and green spaces of Frankenthal in Rhineland-Palatinate, among them some 2,000 children from 11 schools and 22 kindergartens. The town's municipal works department ran the collection for the 22nd year, with eleven clubs and private groups joining the schools; about 1,200 kilos came back in bags, the rest as mattresses, tyres, building rubble, fridges and freezers. Collectors also noted a rising number of discarded disposable e-cigarettes, whose lithium batteries can catch fire in a bin lorry, and the groups that took part receive their prize money on 19 November.

Dijon, France · November 13, 2026

Nearly 650 people raised €30,000 in ten days so a Dijon bookshop could buy its own walls.

Nearly 650 people gave more than €30,000 in about ten days to La Fleur qui pousse à l'intérieur, an independent bookshop on the place des Cordeliers in Dijon. Julien Tardif and Clémence Roquefort have rented the shop for ten years and needed that sum as the deposit on the €280,000 purchase of the premises, and a café on the same square is putting two euros into the fund for every hot drink it sells until the end of November. The appeal stays open until 15 December, and the two booksellers say every euro past the target is now a bonus.

Source: ICI Bourgogne (Radio France)
Shap, England · November 12, 2026

A volunteer-run second-hand shop has given £250,000 back to its Cumbrian village.

A £10,000 grant towards new linings for Shap Swimming Pool pushed the Second Chance Charity Shop past £250,000 raised for causes in Shap and Shap Rural since it opened in 2015. Shap Community Enterprise runs the shop entirely on volunteers and pays no wages, only rent and electricity, which is why villagers have made it their preferred place to drop off clothing, bric-a-brac and the electricals the shop tests before resale. Trustees have now scrapped the £5,000 ceiling on single grants and could put up to £30,000 behind one project, after years of funding the football club, the play area, the tennis courts and the village loos.

Source: Cumberland and Westmorland Herald

Coming Together · Park City, United States · November 11, 2026
Photo: Olivia Hutcherson, via Unsplash

Park City gave $6.1 million to more than 150 local causes in a single day.

Park City, Utah, raised $6.1 million for more than 150 local nonprofits in the 24 hours of 6 November, the biggest total in the history of its annual Live PC Give PC giving day. More than 7,600 people donated, past the 6,800-donor goal the Park City Community Foundation had set, and the groups that signed up competed for a $2,500 top prize that went to the Literary Ladies Book Club, which is passing the money on to Clark's Christmas Foundation. Joel Zarrow, the foundation's chief executive, said participation had fallen short of target last year and comfortably cleared it this time, and the next giving day is already set for 5 November 2027.

Poienarii Burchii, Romania · November 10, 2026

2,512 people put ash saplings into the ground at the same moment.

On 7 November, 2,512 volunteers planted ash saplings simultaneously in the commune of Poienarii Burchii, in Romania's Prahova county, a Guinness World Records mark that passed the 2,055 set in India in July 2025. The adjudicator Richard Stenning validated the attempt on site, where every participant had to plant one tree correctly and start on a single signal; more than 4,000 people had signed up within two weeks of the call going out, and over 3,200 took part in the wider planting, among them students and staff from five universities and three forestry high schools. The Act for Tomorrow association and Kaufland Romania organised the Pădurea Prieteniei project with saplings from the state forest administration Romsilva, and its president Andrei Coșuleanu says the months of preparation now give way to the longer job of looking after the new forest.

London, United Kingdom · November 8, 2026

153,862 people in 92 countries read together for 12 hours.

Guinness World Records has confirmed that 153,862 people in 92 countries read at the same time during a 12-hour session on 8 October, a new mark for the most people reading online in 12 hours. The National Literacy Trust and the British Council ran the Take 10 to Read challenge, with more than 3,000 schools, libraries, families and hobby groups joining authors online for ten minutes of quiet reading; outside the United Kingdom the biggest groups logged on from Romania, India and Pakistan, and readers also took part from the Cayman Islands, Afghanistan and Venezuela. The adjudicator Will Munford presented the certificate at Malmesbury Primary School in east London on 4 November alongside the children's author Rashmi Sirdeshpande, and a second went to Holy Child Senior Secondary School in New Delhi.

Wamphray, Scotland · November 7, 2026

A Dumfriesshire village raised £45,000 and bought its own church.

Friends of Wamphray Church has completed the purchase of the 1834 kirk in the hills south of Moffat from the Church of Scotland, after raising more than £45,000 in under a year. Donations came from across the United Kingdom and from supporters in the United States and Canada, topped up by a £12,500 grant from Clyde Windfarm and SSE Renewables; the chair, Carol Beattie, said the group was grateful for the time it was given to raise the money and build support. Secretary Rob Harrison says the first job is plumbing in toilets, with a condition survey to follow, and the building opens again for a Remembrance service on Sunday and the village carol service on Christmas Eve.

Dietzenbach, Germany · November 3, 2026

People from 79 nations planted 2,500 trees together in one Hessen forest.

Six hundred and eighty-five people from 79 countries planted 1,750 sessile oaks and 750 hornbeams in the Eulerwald on the edge of Dietzenbach, south-east of Frankfurt, on 24 October. The non-profit Waldfreunde gGmbH ran the day with more than 20 volunteers and a HessenForst forester, Benedikt Reiss, who showed newcomers how to set a sapling; everyone brought a passport so the Rekord-Institut für Deutschland could count nationalities, and its adjudicator Meike Janz handed over the certificate the same day. The previous mark of 66 nationalities had stood since a planting in Qatar, and the organisers chose the date because it falls on United Nations Day.

Warsaw, Poland · November 2, 2026

Nearly 250 volunteers spent the All Saints' weekend collecting for Warsaw's oldest gravestones.

Nearly 250 volunteers — actors, musicians and journalists among them — carried collection tins along the paths of Warsaw's Stare Powązki cemetery from 9am until about 8pm on 31 October and 1 November, in the 52nd year of the appeal. The Jerzy Waldorff committee that runs it took about 270,000 złoty last year, money that went toward conserving 27 historic monuments alongside 1.9 million złoty in city grants. Forty more gravestones are booked for restoration next year, out of roughly 300 the committee still counts as urgent and about 1,700 saved over five decades.

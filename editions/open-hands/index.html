Open Hands · Holbrook, New South Wales, Australia · November 27, 2026
Photo: Annie Spratt, via Unsplash

A couple have given 2,500 pieces of Australian pottery to the national museum.

Geoff and Kerrie Ford have handed more than 2,500 pieces of nineteenth- and twentieth-century Australian pottery to the National Museum of Australia in Canberra, and closed the private museum they had run since 1995. The collection started in 1978 with a mint-condition 1849 ginger beer bottle bought at a clearing sale near Gundagai, and grew to take in salt-glazed water filters, jugs and bookends, some of them made by convicts. The national museum is planning a display of highlights for 2027, and the Fords will keep working on the collection there as associated researchers.

Magrath, Canada · November 26, 2026

A ranching family has given 160 acres of never-ploughed prairie on the Milk River Ridge to a land trust.

The Coppieters family has handed 65 hectares — 160 acres — of native fescue grassland along the Milk River Ridge, south of Lethbridge, to the Nature Conservancy of Canada. Peter Coppieters Sr. settled the area in 1925 and the quarter-section has never been cultivated; his son Peter Jr. and his wife Donna later bought the pasture and chose to give it away rather than sell it. Local ranchers can keep grazing the land while the conservancy writes a stewardship plan, and the soil beneath it holds an estimated 11,400 tonnes of carbon.

Ottawa, Canada · November 25, 2026

The National Gallery of Canada has been promised 56 glass works and the curator to look after them.

Charles Bronfman has promised the National Gallery of Canada in Ottawa 56 modern and contemporary glass works by 29 artists, a collection he and his late wife, Andrea, assembled together over decades. The works run from Tiffany Studios, Émile Gallé and the Daum studio through Harvey Littleton, Lino Tagliapietra and the Canadian artist Laura Donefer, and the gift carries an endowment that pays for the gallery's first curatorial position in decorative arts and design. A selection, among it Edward Hald's Fireworks Bowl, is on view in the European galleries until 30 April 2027, and the new curator will take on research, exhibitions and future acquisitions in a field the gallery has never staffed.

Rochester, New York, USA · November 24, 2026

A quilting group has sewn more than 500 quilts for the newborn unit at a Rochester hospital.

The Itty Bitty Baby Brigade has given more than 500 handmade quilts to the neonatal intensive care unit at Rochester General Hospital in New York, where they are draped over incubators to screen out light and noise. The quilters meet on the third Thursday of each month at a sewing machine shop in Wolcott and sew at home in between, buying their own fabric unless someone donates it. The unit goes through 20 to 25 quilts a month, and every baby takes their quilt home at discharge.

Shenyang, China · November 18, 2026

A Macau collector has given 41 pieces of Chinese export porcelain to the Shenyang Palace Museum.

Zhao Qiang, chairman of the Macau Association for Preservation of Cultural Relics, handed 41 pieces of export porcelain to the Shenyang Palace Museum in Liaoning province on 16 November, the day the museum turned 100. The pieces fall into three categories of ware made in China for buyers overseas, and Zhang Guobin, director of the museum's collection department, said they fill a gap in its export porcelain holdings and round out its collection. The museum, which occupies the Qing palace complex used by three emperors and has passed six million visitors this year, will research and display them; Zhao said the pieces once crossed mountains and seas to reach the world.

Dhali, Himachal Pradesh, India · November 17, 2026

Three villages in the Shimla hills have repaired three kilometres of their link road by hand.

Residents of Dhali Bagra, Shilli and Paniser, in the newly formed Dhali panchayat of Shimla district, have filled the potholes along more than three kilometres of the seven-kilometre Shilli-Neen road with soil and stone they gathered themselves. They worked four days running and used no government money; panchayat head Anokhi Ram said the new panchayat has yet to receive any funds for road repair, and cooperative society secretary Amar Singh Thakur was the one who first asked neighbours to give their labour. Four kilometres are still to do, and the villagers plan to finish that stretch and then take on the road from Shilli to Chalog Pajal, the routes an ambulance has to use to reach these slopes.

Open Hands · London, United Kingdom · November 5, 2026
Photo: Ben Hadfield, via Unsplash

Phoebe Waller-Bridge bought a London flat and gave its use to playwrights starting out.

Phoebe Waller-Bridge has bought a flat in London and donated its use to writers early in their careers, who will pay the bills and no rent. The flat is the centrepiece of Writers' Block, a pilot built by Sofie Mason and Diana Jervis-Read on the Adopt a Playwright Award they founded in 2008, which finds rooms for playwrights from low-income backgrounds who need to be in the capital for meetings, rehearsals and performances. Jack Thorne, James Graham, Sally Wainwright, Peter Morgan and Martin McDonagh are funding the pilot as well, and Graham, who slept on friends' floors and worked in call centres when he first came to London, said each spare room keeps a writer from giving up.

Ottawa, Canada · November 4, 2026

An Ottawa donor has given blood 500 times, 52 years after his first donation.

Bob McDougall made his 500th donation to Canadian Blood Services on 2 November at the centre on Carling Avenue in Ottawa, 52 years after he first gave blood in 1974. He now gives plasma and platelets every two weeks, usually on a Monday, and Sandy Renny, a volunteer at the centre, said McDougall is the first person he sees each time he arrives for a shift. Canadian Blood Services, which says about 500 Canadians have reached the same mark, expects national demand for plasma to rise by half over the next five years, and McDougall said he will keep donating as long as his health allows.

Rothbury, Northumberland, United Kingdom · November 3, 2026

A two-year public appeal has bought the 9,500-acre Rothbury Estate for nature.

The Wildlife Trusts and Northumberland Wildlife Trust completed the £30m purchase of the entire 9,500-acre Rothbury Estate in Northumberland on 30 October, two years after opening their largest fundraising appeal. More than 25,000 people gave over £17m of that total, from across the UK and more than 50 countries including Finland, Barbados, Kenya and New Zealand, with the remainder coming from the National Lottery Heritage Fund, Defra and charitable trusts. The moorland, woods, river and working farms now stay in single ownership, and the trusts plan nature-friendly farming, habitat restoration and a greenway along a disused railway line, with volunteer soil and wildlife surveys starting this month.

Lexington, Kentucky, USA · November 2, 2026

A $30 million gift will expand the visitor center and education space at Kentucky's state botanical garden.

The Bill Gatton Foundation has committed $30 million to the Arboretum, State Botanical Garden of Kentucky, the 100-acre public garden on the edge of the University of Kentucky's campus in Lexington. Foundation trustee Danny Dunn traced the gift to an earlier version of the will left by Bill Gatton, a university alumnus, and the money covers six projects, among them an expanded visitor center, an education building with two flex classrooms, a reconfigured entrance and improved walkways. The garden hosted at least 75 classes and more than 1,300 students last year, and its director, Michael Wall, expects construction to start in 2027 and run about a year.

London, United Kingdom · November 1, 2026

An art historian gave Tate the 50 Indian paintings he spent his life assembling.

The British art historian Charles Greig has donated 50 paintings and watercolours made in India during the 18th and 19th centuries to Tate, a collection he spent a working lifetime putting together. Alongside works by Ghulam Ali Khan, Johan Zoffany, Rudolf Swoboda and Thomas and William Daniell, the gift includes three watercolours by Sita Ram, who helped shape the European-style works known as Company paintings, and Greig said that at no stage did he see himself as other than a temporary steward. Tate Britain will open a free display drawn from the collection in 2028.

Harbor Springs, Michigan, USA · October 3, 2026

A Michigan family gave up 20 acres to make a 240-acre forest permanent.

In Emmet County near Harbor Springs, Michigan, the Little Traverse Conservancy closed on 180 acres of hardwood forest after a $3 million community fundraising campaign, and the Ford family donated an additional 20 acres of its own land outright, permanently protecting 240 contiguous acres from development. The Fords have owned the property since the early 1980s and had let neighbors use it for walking and cross-country skiing for decades; conservancy development officer Emily Hughes said the deal takes any future sale to a developer off the table. The conservancy, which named the tract The Hundred Acre Wood, plans to build a parking area and expand the trail network next year, linking the preserve to an adjoining 80 acres already owned by West Traverse Township.

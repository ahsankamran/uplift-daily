Open Hands · Dhali, Himachal Pradesh, India · November 17, 2026
Photo: Hardik Monga, via Unsplash

Three villages in the Shimla hills have repaired three kilometres of their link road by hand.

Residents of Dhali Bagra, Shilli and Paniser, in the newly formed Dhali panchayat of Shimla district, have filled the potholes along more than three kilometres of the seven-kilometre Shilli-Neen road with soil and stone they gathered themselves. They worked four days running and used no government money; panchayat head Anokhi Ram said the new panchayat has yet to receive any funds for road repair, and cooperative society secretary Amar Singh Thakur was the one who first asked neighbours to give their labour. Four kilometres are still to do, and the villagers plan to finish that stretch and then take on the road from Shilli to Chalog Pajal, the routes an ambulance has to use to reach these slopes.

Southwestern Ontario, Canada · November 15, 2026

A $10.5-million gift will let 60 more Ontario farms test regenerative practices without carrying the risk alone.

The Canadian Alliance for Net-Zero Agri-Food is extending its Million Acre Challenge to about 60 more farms and 36,000 more acres in southwestern Ontario, paid for by a new $10.5-million donation from the Weston family. The program covers part of the cost of changes such as no-till planting and better-timed fertilizer and pairs farmers with independent agronomists; in the first phase 32 farmers enrolled more than 15,000 acres across 175 fields, and every one of those farms took up at least one new practice. Recruitment for the second phase opens in December, and Lexi Soudant of Dave Gardiner Farms in Kirkton said the shared record-keeping is what made no-till worth trying on a family farm, where a failed experiment costs real time and money.

Bantwal, Karnataka, India · November 14, 2026

Auto-rickshaw drivers in coastal Karnataka pooled their daily savings to buy their villages a free ambulance.

The Navodaya Auto-rickshaw Drivers and Owners Association has put a fully equipped ambulance into free service for Kariyangala and Ammunje, two villages in Bantwal taluk in coastal Karnataka. The roughly 26 drivers who work the Badakabail stand set aside small amounts from their daily takings to cover a vehicle costing about Rs 10 lakh and a medical fitting done in Mysuru costing about Rs 2 lakh, with villagers and local donors making up the rest. The ambulance is parked at the auto stand and the drivers themselves will take turns at the wheel, in an area where people who needed a hospital were until now carried there in the back of a rickshaw.

Athens, Greece · November 13, 2026

A family has given its inherited collection of 22 antiquities and icons to the Greek state.

Panagiota Karpida-Razou has handed her family's collection of 20 antiquities and two post-Byzantine icons to the Greek state at a ceremony in the Altar Hall of the National Archaeological Museum in Athens. The objects run from Early Cycladic marble figurines and bowls through bronze Geometric and Archaic jewellery to a Roman helmet and a bronze figurine of Hermes, and reached her father, Konstantinos Karpidas, as a gift from the collector Alexandros Iolas in 1987. The 20 ancient pieces now join the permanent collections of the National Archaeological Museum and the two icons go to the Byzantine and Christian Museum, where Culture Minister Lina Mendoni said they will be accessible to all.

Rukum East, Nepal · November 12, 2026

A 15-year-old in western Nepal paid for her village's new footbridge out of her own earnings.

Sadiksha Oli spent Rs60,000 of her own money on a wooden footbridge over the Domai stream at Luma, in Bhume Rural Municipality of Rukum East, where the previous crossing was swept away in June. The money, earned through her monetised Facebook account, bought nails, corrugated iron sheets and the wages of skilled workers, while Oli, her relatives and neighbours cut local timber and finished the four-metre span in three days. The bridge restores the everyday walk between Luma and the ward office, the schools and the district headquarters, a route the stream cuts off whenever the monsoon fills it.

Gruver, Texas, USA · November 10, 2026

Farmers in a Texas panhandle town work a donated field for free and turn the harvest into tuition.

Farmers and agricultural suppliers around Gruver, in the Texas panhandle, give their labour, machinery and inputs to plant and harvest a field the town's school owns, and the crops have paid out nearly $3 million in scholarships since 2016. The land came from Carl Nielsen, a Danish immigrant who bought it in the late 1800s and left it to Gruver school after his death in the 1970s; it was cash-leased for about $25,000 a year until a group of local farmers proposed in 2012 that they farm it themselves. Forty-eight of the students the fund has backed became the first in their families to finish college, and awards are pegged to West Texas A&M tuition of roughly $4,000 a semester and applied after other scholarships, so they also cover trade school and junior college.

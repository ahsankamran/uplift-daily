Quiet Kindness · Moose Jaw, Saskatchewan, Canada · October 4, 2026

A Saskatchewan city names its quietly dependable neighbour of the month.

The City of Moose Jaw, Saskatchewan, named resident Dave Burns its 'Notoriously Good Neighbour' for September after his own street put his name forward. Neighbour Kim Reynolds nominated him for mowing an injured neighbour's lawn and spending his days off helping others nearby, saying simply, our street is lucky to have him. Burns received a gift certificate to the Tunnels of Moose Jaw, part of a city program built specifically to notice this kind of everyday help before it goes unremarked.

Phoenix, Arizona, USA · September 30, 2026

A twelve-year-old in Phoenix collected 30,000 diapers for her fifth annual drive.

Sloan began at seven, after volunteering at Nourish Phoenix and finding the diaper shelves empty; staff told her diapers were the most requested item and the first to run out. This year's drive beat its goal of 22,500, filling a U-Haul truck with Huggies, Pampers and Blueys. “If we have the ability to give someone something that they might not have or need, it’s really important to do that,” she told ABC15.

The Bronx, New York, USA · September 29, 2026

An eighteen-year-old from the Bronx now sends ten children to school in Accra.

Max Owobu started the Yenko Foundation at 15, paying a first child's school fees in Ghana out of his weekly allowance. It now sponsors ten children through kindergarten to fifth grade at Calvary Methodist Elementary School in Accra, where $1,000 covers a year of tuition, uniforms, transport and meals. He aims to sponsor ten to fifteen children all the way through primary school, then extend the work to hospitals and orphanages.

South Korea · September 26, 2026

A stranger tracked down a father's lost wallet just to return it.

A man in South Korea lost a wallet holding 700,000 won and his cards at a gas station while visiting his grandchildren, and spent the next day retracing his route with no luck. Nearly a month later a local community center called: someone had turned the wallet in through a police substation, every bill and card intact, along with a handwritten note mailed to his registered address. "This wallet, which must be precious to someone, ended up in the wrong hands," the finder wrote, adding that returning it brought more relief than keeping it ever could have.

Evanston, Illinois, USA · September 25, 2026

A mutual-aid group opened its fifth free food pantry in an alley behind a small-town library.

Evanston Community Fridges and the Woman's Club of Evanston unveiled a new refrigerator and pantry between the club's building and the public library, the group's first stocked spot downtown. The shelves, filled entirely by neighbors leaving what they can spare, typically empty within hours and serve an estimated 200 to 300 people a month, including students, seniors, and people without homes. Organizer Adrienne Edwards says the fridges work on one rule: anyone can be the one giving or the one taking, and no one has to say which.

Spring City, Tennessee, USA · September 24, 2026

A small-town diner runs on meal tickets nobody has to ask for.

At BB's Café in Spring City, Tennessee, customers can buy a ticket off a board by the register that anyone short on cash can quietly redeem for a hot meal. Owner Brandi Sanders started the pay-it-forward board after her own years of going hungry as a teenager: "I try to be more giving 'cause I know how it felt." The café was recently nominated for a local community award, but Sanders says the point was never recognition, just making sure nobody in town has to ask twice.

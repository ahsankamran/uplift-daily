Everyday Joy · City of Wanneroo, Perth, Western Australia · November 2, 2026

Perth residents spent their evenings in the bush listening for bats.

Researchers at Murdoch University handed ultrasonic detectors that clip onto a smartphone to a handful of curious volunteers in the City of Wanneroo, on Perth's northern edge, and sent them along set routes through local bushland after dark. Across 23 surveys the group recorded five species of microbat, Gould's wattled bat the most common among them, and picked up Holt's long-eared bat in Wanneroo for the first time. Questionnaires afterwards found the volunteers knew more about bats, felt more connected to the place they live, and simply liked the animals better than when they began.

Sardinia, Italy · November 1, 2026

Fifteen Sardinian restaurants are offering families a table with no screens on it.

From November 1, fifteen restaurants around Sardinia are inviting families to put their phones away for the length of the meal, a month-long trial called A tavola senza schermi — at the table without screens. Anna Cau, the region's guarantor for children and adolescents, presented the scheme in Cagliari on October 26 with Fipe Confcommercio Sardegna; in place of a handed-over phone, children get a kit del ritrovo of games sorted by age and a leaflet written with the Italian federation of paediatricians. If the month goes well, the organisers want to open it to restaurants across the island.

Carrbridge, Scotland · October 4, 2026

Carrbridge crowns its 32nd World Porridge Making Champion.

On October 3, thirty cooks from fourteen countries converged on Carrbridge, a village in Scotland's Cairngorms National Park, for the 32nd World Porridge Making Championship. Dutch cook Gina Saarberg took the Golden Spurtle trophy with a recipe of organic pinhead oats, salt, and water she had carried from the Netherlands, in a contest run entirely by volunteers from the Carrbridge Community Council. The specialty prize went to Rob Reid of Sydney for a porridge cannoli blending his Scottish and Sicilian heritage, proof that a tiny Highland village's contest now draws cooks from every corner of the globe.

Deloraine, Tasmania, Australia · September 30, 2026

A Japanese taste for Tasmanian soba put 300 cherry trees along a Tasmanian river.

Buckwheat trials began in Tasmania in 1986, and its reversed seasons let Japanese millers buy fresh soba flour twice a year. Mill owner Rick Shiratori donated the cherry trees thirty years ago in thanks, and 300 now line the Meander River at Deloraine, blooming for about ten days. Around 200 people came on Sunday to the fifth Hanami Trail festival for aikido, taiko drumming and bamboo flute.

Christchurch, New Zealand · September 29, 2026

Gibbons got Marmite-smeared poles and gift-wrapped locusts for their zoo's fiftieth birthday.

Orana Wildlife Park in Christchurch marked fifty years since it opened on 25 September 1976 with birthday enrichment for its animals. The gibbons had Marmite and peanut butter smeared on poles, plus gift-wrapped boxes of cooked corn and live locusts to unwrap. The party came days after ratepayers gave the park a NZ$1.2 million lifeline.

Bermuda and the Caribbean · September 26, 2026

Oxford's dictionary made room for Bermudian and Caribbean slang.

The Oxford English Dictionary added more than a dozen words long used across Bermuda and the Caribbean, including bimpert, a Bermudian term for a foolish person, and canboulay, Trinidad's 1800s emancipation-carnival tradition. Lexicographers drew on decades of everyday use, from hamma, Bermudian for friend, to vex money, the Caribbean term for a woman's emergency cash reserve. The update gives formal recognition to language islanders have spoken for generations, alongside dishes like peanut punch and sugar cake.

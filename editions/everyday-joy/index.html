Everyday Joy · Deloraine, Tasmania, Australia · September 30, 2026

A Japanese taste for Tasmanian soba put 300 cherry trees along a Tasmanian river.

Buckwheat trials began in Tasmania in 1986, and its reversed seasons let Japanese millers buy fresh soba flour twice a year. Mill owner Rick Shiratori donated the cherry trees thirty years ago in thanks, and 300 now line the Meander River at Deloraine, blooming for about ten days. Around 200 people came on Sunday to the fifth Hanami Trail festival for aikido, taiko drumming and bamboo flute.

Christchurch, New Zealand · September 29, 2026

Gibbons got Marmite-smeared poles and gift-wrapped locusts for their zoo's fiftieth birthday.

Orana Wildlife Park in Christchurch marked fifty years since it opened on 25 September 1976 with birthday enrichment for its animals. The gibbons had Marmite and peanut butter smeared on poles, plus gift-wrapped boxes of cooked corn and live locusts to unwrap. The party came days after ratepayers gave the park a NZ$1.2 million lifeline.

Bermuda and the Caribbean · September 26, 2026

Oxford's dictionary made room for Bermudian and Caribbean slang.

The Oxford English Dictionary added more than a dozen words long used across Bermuda and the Caribbean, including bimpert, a Bermudian term for a foolish person, and canboulay, Trinidad's 1800s emancipation-carnival tradition. Lexicographers drew on decades of everyday use, from hamma, Bermudian for friend, to vex money, the Caribbean term for a woman's emergency cash reserve. The update gives formal recognition to language islanders have spoken for generations, alongside dishes like peanut punch and sugar cake.

Cardross, Scotland · September 25, 2026

Volunteers turned a windswept Scottish train platform into an award-winning garden.

The Royal Horticultural Society gave its first-ever Railway Stations in Bloom prize to Cardross station on Scotland's west coast, chosen from five shortlisted stations across Britain. Since 2022, the Cardross Community Organic Garden group led by Jane Connolly has planted every available bed with fruit trees and vegetables, turning a once-bare stop into somewhere people travel to see. Judges praised how the group pulled in schoolchildren, Brownies, and other volunteers along the way.

George Town, Penang, Malaysia · September 24, 2026

A youth committee brought lantern-making and dough-figurine carving back to Penang's Mid-Autumn Festival.

On the Esplanade in George Town, organizers revived a full slate of Mid-Autumn customs this week, from lantern-riddle games to mooncake-making demonstrations. The Clan-Associations Youth Committee's Goh Kok Liang added dough-figurine carving, paper-cutting, and Sichuan opera face-changing to old standbys like hopscotch and the wind-fire wheel, drawing families ahead of the festival's September 25 date. The point, he said, is giving young people in a modern port city an easy, everyday way to hold onto a shared tradition.

Sumbawa Island, Indonesia · September 23, 2026

A ban on compressor diving revived an ancestral freediving skill in Indonesia.

In the Bajau fishing village of Poto Tano, octopus hunters once dropped to the seafloor trailing air hoses from a compressor; a 2020 ban made the older skill of freediving 15 to 20 meters with a handmade lure called a pocong valuable again. Only about ten people in the community can still use the pocong well, and the roughly 40 fishers of the PELITA brotherhood now close two or three fishing grounds at a time, for months, so the octopus can recover. They find their way by reading stars, wind, and current rather than GPS, a practice a local women's conservation group is now helping document and pass on to the next generation.

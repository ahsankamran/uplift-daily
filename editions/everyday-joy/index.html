Everyday Joy · Gijón, Asturias, Spain · November 13, 2026

A physics student rapped 200 freestyle battles in a single day.

Gazir, a 24-year-old MC from Gijón who studies physics at the University of Oviedo, took on 250 challengers in 200 consecutive freestyle battles on November 7, winning 197 of them in 11 hours, 23 minutes and 14 seconds. He had 20 seconds between battles and a four-minute break after every 40, won his first 115 in a row before losing the 116th to a rapper called Babi, and burned about 3,000 calories by the reckoning of his fitness ring. Guinness World Records has confirmed the attempt, and the next thing Gazir says he wants to try is freestyling in English.

Hervey Bay, Queensland, Australia · November 12, 2026

A house in Hervey Bay holds 5,373 turtles.

Tania Murphy's home in Hervey Bay, Queensland, contains 5,373 turtle and tortoise things — wooden statues, ceramic figurines, plates, glasses, soft toys — which Guinness World Records has confirmed as the largest collection of its kind anywhere. It began when she was five and would not give back her sister's stuffed turtle, and turned deliberate about 20 years ago; two independent witnesses spent some 17 hours counting on video, and she submitted close to 6,000 files and waited three months for the verdict. She has arranged the house like a museum and will show visitors round by appointment, with 10,000 as the next target: There's always room for more turtles.

Kota Bharu, Kelantan, Malaysia · November 11, 2026

A Kelantan net-maker has been knotting mesh by hand since 1987.

Buniamin Abdullah, 73, learned to make fishing nets from his parents in Kota Bharu and has been doing it for 39 years; a small net takes him about 20 days and a large one more than a month, depending on how fine the mesh has to be. He was demonstrating at Nostalgia Anak Kampung 2026 at the National Museum in Kuala Lumpur, where he told Bernama that his eyes no longer let him work the finest meshes. His view of what the craft needs is plain enough: teach it to children, or it goes.

Vatican City · November 10, 2026

A cleaning of the Sistine Chapel shows Michelangelo painted with brushes he made himself.

Restorers took three months to lift a whitish calcium-lactate bloom, left by the breath and sweat of visitors, off The Last Judgment with distilled water and sheets of Japanese tissue, the first cleaning of the altar wall in 30 years. Working that close, the Vatican's chief restorer Paolo Violini noticed that where one brushstroke wavered the strokes beside it wavered in exactly the same way, which can only happen if several tips are moving together: one brush had three, another was nearly six inches wide with ten. The arrangement copies the gradina, the many-toothed chisel Michelangelo used as a sculptor, carried across to a wall 45 feet high and more than 300 figures that took him from 1536 to 1541.

Accra, Ghana · November 8, 2026

A photographer who opened his Accra studio in 1953 is being honoured at 97.

James Barnor, born in 1929, has been given the Outstanding Contribution to Photography award by the World Photography Organisation, and more than 50 of his prints go on show at Somerset House in London next April. He became the Daily Graphic's first staff photographer at 21, opened the Ever Young studio in Jamestown in 1953, photographed Kwame Nkrumah declaring Ghana's independence in 1957, spent the 1960s in London making portraits of Black Londoners for Drum magazine, then went home in 1970 to help set up the country's first colour-processing laboratory. His pictures hang in the Tate, the National Portrait Gallery and the Centre Pompidou; of the work he said, My camera became my companion, and I have never regretted it.

Hull, England · November 7, 2026

A man who started walking in 1998 has reached his mother's front door.

Karl Bushby, 57, arrived in Hull on Monday, 28 years after setting out from Punta Arenas at the southern tip of Chile, having covered about 50,000 kilometres — roughly 31,000 miles — without once using motorised transport. He walked north through the Americas and the Darién Gap, crossed the frozen Bering Strait into Russia, carried on through Mongolia and China, swam the Caspian, and finished by swimming the English Channel in October and walking over the Humber Bridge on Sunday. His mother, Angela, was waiting under a banner reading Welcome Home Son; he said that it took a world to get me here, and gave the credit to the strangers who fed and sheltered him along the way.

Global Progress · Geneva, Switzerland · November 12, 2026

Tuberculosis deaths have fallen by more than a third since 2015.

The World Health Organization's annual tuberculosis report, published on November 12, puts deaths from the disease at about 1.1 million in 2025 — 35% fewer than in 2015, and enough to pass the first mortality milestone of its End TB Strategy. Diagnosis and treatment are reckoned to have saved 86 million lives since 2000, and 8.4 million people were diagnosed last year. The milestone arrives five years later than planned and new cases are down only 14% over the same period, though countries raised their own spending on the disease by 14% last year, to $5.6 billion.

Phnom Penh, Cambodia · November 11, 2026

Cambodia is offering the HPV vaccine to more than 700,000 girls before the end of December.

The health ministry launched a nationwide campaign in Phnom Penh on November 10 that runs in two phases to the end of December, with a free dose for every girl aged 13 and 14 and catch-up doses for 10- to 12-year-olds who missed the routine programme between 2023 and 2025. Teams are working through schools, health centres and villages alongside teachers, local authorities and village health support groups, with the effort aimed at girls who are out of school or live far from a clinic. Gavi is putting in more than three million US dollars, and a single dose, given before exposure, is enough for strong lasting protection.

Source: Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance
Freetown, Sierra Leone · November 10, 2026

Sierra Leone will cook school lunch for 276,000 children this year.

The government has put SLE 158.4 million, about 6.6 million US dollars, through the education ministry into the National School Feeding Programme for 2026/27, after feeding more than 257,000 children in over 1,200 schools the year before. The food is bought at home — the launch of this year's round took in a million kilograms of Sierra Leonean rice — and the programme has already given steady custom to more than 22,000 smallholder farmers. By investing in school meals, Sierra Leone is investing in its children and its future, said Andrew Odero, the World Food Programme's country director.

Source: World Food Programme
Pará and Rondônia, Brazil · November 8, 2026

Amazon cacao grew by a third, nearly all of it on land already cleared.

Embrapa's new mapping puts cacao in Pará at 116,400 hectares in 2024, up from about 87,300 in 2022, with 96 percent of it standing on ground cleared before 2020 — 98 percent in Rondônia, surveyed here for the first time at 5,340 hectares across 51 municipalities. Adriano Venturieri led the work, with field teams from CEPLAC and Emater checking it on the ground; most growers are smallholders, 84.6 percent of them farming five hectares or less, and about 11,444 hectares still read as forest from the air because the trees were planted under a thinned canopy. The figures start to matter on December 30, when the European Union's deforestation rules reach large traders, and Brazil's national plan asks for another 120,000 hectares grown this way by 2030.

Source: Mongabay
Geneva, Switzerland · November 7, 2026

Three-quarters of the world is now online.

The International Telecommunication Union counts 6.3 billion people using the internet in 2026, 76 percent of humanity, up from a revised 6.1 billion the year before. Its Facts and Figures report, out on November 3, also records that landlocked developing countries and small island developing states met the affordability benchmark for mobile broadband — 2 percent of income per head — for the first time. Two billion people are still offline, and the gap remains wide: 93 percent of people in high-income countries use the internet, against 32 percent in low-income ones.

Source: International Telecommunication Union
Mali · November 5, 2026

A Malian enterprise has put 150,000 trees into worn-out ground.

Herou Alliance, the agroforestry enterprise Rokiatu Traoré runs in rural Mali, has planted about 150,000 moringa and baobab trees on degraded land and trained more than 2,000 smallholder farmers to look after them. At the UN Sustainable Development Goals Action Awards in Rome on October 29 she was named a Changemaker; since 2021 the enterprise has also been building commercial moringa supply chains, so the trees pay the farmers as well as holding the soil. Land degradation covered 11.5 million hectares of Mali in 2019 and touched more than 6.5 million people; Traoré's stated aim is 10 million hectares restored by 2040.

Source: Mongabay

Bhutan · November 18, 2026

Bhutan has won an Earthshot Prize for keeping seven tenths of itself under forest.

The country took the Protect and Restore Nature category at the Earthshot awards in Mumbai on 17 November, one of five winners each receiving £1 million, with Princess Chimi Yangzom Wangchuck accepting on its behalf. Bhutan holds close to 70% forest cover and its constitution fixes a floor of 60% for all time, a rule set by the Fourth Druk Gyalpo, Jigme Singye Wangchuck; those forests hold the soil above Himalayan rivers that carry water far downstream across South Asia. The prize money goes to Restore Bhutan, which aims to bring 50,000 hectares of worn-out forest, farmland and urban land back by 2030.

Source: Bhutan Broadcasting Service
Eritrea · November 17, 2026

Eritrea is the 34th country to be rid of trachoma as a public health problem.

The World Health Organization validated the country on 16 November, making it the 11th in WHO's African Region to clear the infection that scars the eyelid and, left alone, turns the lashes inward until they blind. Baseline surveys covered all 58 sub-zobas between 2006 and 2020, after which the health ministry ran the SAFE strategy — surgery for in-turned lashes, azithromycin donated by Pfizer through the International Trachoma Initiative, face washing, and better water and sanitation — using a community-led approach that held costs down and digital mapping to reach places that were hard to find. The number of people worldwide who need trachoma treatment has fallen from about 1.5 billion in 2002 to under 100 million, and Eritrea will keep surveillance running to pick up new cases as they appear.

Source: World Health Organization
Trans-Nzoia County, Kenya · November 15, 2026

Kenya has certified 6,651 villages as free of open defecation since 2021.

That run of certifications has taken the share of villages signed off from 17% to 44%, and in 2025 alone 423 villages crossed the line and 86,341 fewer people were left without basic sanitation, on UNICEF's figures. In Trans-Nzoia County, 24 more villages were declared open defecation-free at the end of July after households in Keiyo and Cherangany wards spent June digging pit latrines and fitting water containers with taps and soap stands; Kwanza sub-county now counts 37. Community health promoters walked it door to door — Vanice Namalwa covering a ward of about 33,000 people — in a country where typhoid still sickens more than 109,000 people a year.

Source: VaccinesWork
Pakistan · November 14, 2026

Pakistan gave 24 million children polio drops in six days.

A door-to-door round that opened on November 2 and closed on November 8 reached about 24 million children under five across 97 districts. More than 200,000 frontline workers carried it, covering 9.1 million children in Punjab, 7 million in Sindh, 4.7 million in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and 2.2 million in Balochistan, with smaller rounds in Islamabad, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. The National Emergency Operations Centre has recorded nine cases in Pakistan this year, one of the last countries where the wild virus still spreads.

Source: Radio Pakistan
Bayelsa State, Nigeria · November 13, 2026

Nigeria has fully vaccinated nearly 250,000 children against malaria.

Since the first malaria vaccines arrived in Nigeria in late 2024, 1.8 million doses have been given and close to 250,000 children have finished the four-dose course, which runs across the first 15 months of life. The rollout covers four of the country's 36 states so far; in Bayelsa, where health workers reach riverside villages by boat and call at houses when a family misses an appointment, PATH records an 18% fall in the share of under-fives carrying malaria at any one time. The vaccine protects only partly and its effect fades, but health officials in the Ogbia local government area say they have since recorded none of the child deaths they were used to.

Source: France 24

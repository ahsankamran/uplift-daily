Global Progress · Dakar, Senegal · October 4, 2026

Senegal starts manufacturing the world's first rapid test for measles.

Gavi approved deployment of the first-ever rapid diagnostic test for measles on September 30, 2026, with production based in Dakar, Senegal. DIATROPIX, a manufacturing subsidiary of the Institut Pasteur de Dakar, makes the test, which cleared a rigorous evaluation by WHO and was validated by the Public Health Agency of Canada's National Microbiology Laboratory. Up to 500,000 tests a year are expected to roll out starting in Senegal, giving frontline health workers a faster way to confirm outbreaks and direct vaccination campaigns where they're needed most.

The Americas · September 30, 2026

Five countries and territories in the Americas were recognised for eliminating diseases, Chile's rabies among them.

The Pan American Health Organization honoured Brazil, Chile, El Salvador, the Bahamas and Turks and Caicos on September 29 for eliminating mother-to-child HIV transmission, leprosy, trachoma and more. Chile became the first country in South America validated for eliminating dog-transmitted rabies this month, and about 60 percent of places worldwide recognised for ending mother-to-child HIV transmission are in the Americas. The region aims to eliminate more than 30 communicable diseases by 2030.

Source: PAHO / WHO
High seas, worldwide · September 29, 2026

The High Seas Treaty passed 100 member countries in a single week.

Germany, Papua New Guinea, Zimbabwe, Canada, Australia and Brunei Darussalam deposited their ratifications during UN General Assembly week, taking the treaty past 100 parties on September 23. It gives the waters beyond national borders, nearly half the planet's surface, their first shared legal framework for conservation, and has been in force since 17 January 2026. Parties meet for the first time in January 2027, when they will begin deciding where the first high-seas protected areas go.

Source: High Seas Alliance
Geneva, Switzerland · September 26, 2026

The WHO widened its safety checklist for medical devices worldwide.

The World Health Organization added condoms, IUDs, and AI-assisted tuberculosis-screening software to its prequalification programme, which vets devices for safety before global procurement. The expansion targets the roughly 70 percent of countries with weak regulatory systems for health products, giving buyers and donors a trusted list to work from. WHO will also take over vetting contraceptive devices from the UN Population Fund, with assessments of the newly added products beginning on a published timeline.

Source: World Health Organization
Côte d’Ivoire · September 25, 2026

Côte d'Ivoire is building a nationwide drone network to keep rural clinics stocked with blood and vaccines.

The Ivorian government approved an eight-year plan to expand Zipline's drone delivery service from one hub in Daloa to ten across the country, eventually reaching more than 2,200 health facilities and 12 million people. The pilot site has already flown 3.55 million medical products, including 20,500 units of blood, to clinics that used to wait days for supply runs over poor roads. Government spokesman Amadou Coulibaly says the expansion is meant to give hard-to-reach communities a "regular and secure supply" of medicine.

Source: DroneXL
Benin · September 24, 2026

Benin doubled access to drinking water in five years by taxing itself first.

Access to drinking water across Benin rose from 40 percent to 80 percent of the population over the past five years, the United Nations reported, funded partly through Africa's first sovereign SDG bond, issued in 2021. The West African country raised its domestic tax revenue by 0.5 percent of GDP every year for a decade, then funneled the proceeds into water systems and a new industrial zone that has created 25,000 jobs. Economic cooperation minister Aristide Medenou said the lesson for other countries chasing the 2030 development goals is simple: raise the money at home first.

Source: UN News

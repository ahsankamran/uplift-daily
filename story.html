Nature · Mendocino County Saturday, April 18, 2026 · 3-min read

A thousand-year-old forest is returning to the Lost Coast.

After three decades of patient restoration, a 3,200-acre stretch of cut-over redwood land north of Fort Bragg has been formally handed back to the Sinkyone Council. The group plans no logging, no roads, and no public campgrounds — only trails, and time.

Morning fog in the Sinkyone wilderness, April 2026. Photograph by Marissa Leshnov for Bay Nature

In the words of the Council's chair, Priscilla Hunter, the land was "never really bought, and so never really sold." The Intertribal Sinkyone Wilderness Council — a consortium of ten Northern California tribes — has held stewardship of the parcel since 1997. This month's transfer made it theirs outright, without covenant, conservation easement, or clock.

The decision ends a thirty-year arrangement with Save the Redwoods League that began in 1996, when the parcel was bought from a timber company for $1.55 million. Biologists surveying three creeks on the property this month counted juvenile coho in all three — small fish, finger-length, in water that had been empty since the 1970s. A pair of marbled murrelets, a seabird that nests only in old-growth canopy, were heard calling in February above a grove the Council had asked be left uncut in 1998. Second-growth trees there are now tall enough to hold them.

There are no plans for campgrounds, no plans for a visitor center, and no plans for paid staff beyond the two-person crew already on the land. "What the forest needs," Hunter told Bay Nature, "is for us to not be in a hurry about it." The Council expects the first redwood seedlings planted in 1999 to reach mast-tree height in roughly four hundred years.

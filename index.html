Everyday Joy · Deloraine, Tasmania, Australia

A Japanese taste for Tasmanian soba put 300 cherry trees along a Tasmanian river.

Buckwheat trials began in Tasmania in 1986, and its reversed seasons let Japanese millers buy fresh soba flour twice a year. Mill owner Rick Shiratori donated the cherry trees thirty years ago in thanks, and 300 now line the Meander River at Deloraine, blooming for about ten days. Around 200 people came on Sunday to the fifth Hanami Trail festival for aikido, taiko drumming and bamboo flute.

Nature · Indus River, Pakistan

Pakistan joined its Indus river dolphin sanctuaries into one 400-kilometre protected corridor.

The Punjnad Indus River Dolphin Sanctuary, created in June 2026, protects nearly 200 kilometres of river in Punjab and connects with existing reserves in Sindh. A 2025 study estimated 1,987 dolphins, up from 617 in 2001, with 99 percent living in a 690-kilometre stretch between the Chashma and Sukkur barrages. Gillnets caused 52 percent of documented deaths from 2008 to 2023, and they remain the main threat the new protections must address.

Small Wonders · Queensland, Australia

Two stick insects new to science turned up in Australia, one hiding in plain sight for decades.

Researchers writing in Austral Entomology on September 21 described Anchiale mabiensis, whose lidded eggs carry concentric rings unlike any recorded in stick insects, and Anchiale robusta, misidentified for decades. They also reinstated Anchiale albopunctata, mistaken for another species for more than a century, and females of mabiensis reach 6.4 inches. Braxton Jones of the University of Sydney said even among Australia's most photographed insects, species are still escaping recognition.

Quiet Kindness · Phoenix, Arizona, USA

A twelve-year-old in Phoenix collected 30,000 diapers for her fifth annual drive.

Sloan began at seven, after volunteering at Nourish Phoenix and finding the diaper shelves empty; staff told her diapers were the most requested item and the first to run out. This year's drive beat its goal of 22,500, filling a U-Haul truck with Huggies, Pampers and Blueys. “If we have the ability to give someone something that they might not have or need, it’s really important to do that,” she told ABC15.

Science & Medicine · Manchester, England

A simple cell test could spare many women with postmenopausal bleeding an invasive check for womb cancer.

The DETECT study tested urine and vaginal fluid from 1,864 women, 115 of whom had cancer, and the combined test found over 80 percent of cancers, including 95.8 percent of high-grade tumours. Its negative predictive value for womb cancer was 98.8 percent, so a clear result is very likely to be right. Professor Emma Davidson, who led the work, said further studies in routine practice are needed before it reaches clinics.

Global Progress · The Americas

Five countries and territories in the Americas were recognised for eliminating diseases, Chile's rabies among them.

The Pan American Health Organization honoured Brazil, Chile, El Salvador, the Bahamas and Turks and Caicos on September 29 for eliminating mother-to-child HIV transmission, leprosy, trachoma and more. Chile became the first country in South America validated for eliminating dog-transmitted rabies this month, and about 60 percent of places worldwide recognised for ending mother-to-child HIV transmission are in the Americas. The region aims to eliminate more than 30 communicable diseases by 2030.

Nature · Ene River, Peru

Asháninka fire brigades brought wildfires in 45 Peruvian communities down to zero.

Across 45 Asháninka communities in Peru's Ene River region, wildfires fell from 25 in 2023 to nine in 2024, and none were recorded in 2025.

Small Wonders · Paris, France

A charred Gallic wall under a Paris hospital may mark the town Caesar besieged.

Archaeologists working in the Hôtel-Dieu courtyard on the Île de la Cité uncovered about 20 metres of a wall carbon-dated to between 200 and 10 BCE.

Global Progress · High seas, worldwide

The High Seas Treaty passed 100 member countries in a single week.

Germany, Papua New Guinea, Zimbabwe, Canada, Australia and Brunei Darussalam deposited their ratifications, taking the treaty past 100 parties on September 23.

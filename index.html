Global Progress · Bhutan

Bhutan has won an Earthshot Prize for keeping seven tenths of itself under forest.

The country took the Protect and Restore Nature category at the Earthshot awards in Mumbai on 17 November, one of five winners each receiving £1 million, with Princess Chimi Yangzom Wangchuck accepting on its behalf. Bhutan holds close to 70% forest cover and its constitution fixes a floor of 60% for all time, a rule set by the Fourth Druk Gyalpo, Jigme Singye Wangchuck; those forests hold the soil above Himalayan rivers that carry water far downstream across South Asia. The prize money goes to Restore Bhutan, which aims to bring 50,000 hectares of worn-out forest, farmland and urban land back by 2030.

Quiet Kindness · Yueqing, Zhejiang, China

Five siblings cut 430 steps into a mountain so their mother could go home.

The old track up to the family house in Yannan village had crumbled and snakes kept appearing along it, so the children hauled stone and mixed cement on the slope themselves between August and October and finished the job before winter. What they built is a 430-step stair that turns eleven times on its way up, with stretches fixed to a sheer rock face, for a mother in her eighties who has lived on that mountain all her life and never took to the city. She goes back each year at the Tomb Sweeping Festival to tend her husband's grave; readers online have taken to calling the stair a filial piety sky ladder.

Nature · Tayside and Forth, Scotland

Scotland now has up to 405 beaver territories, against 39 in 2012.

Surveyors walked 2,397km of watercourses across the Tay and Forth catchments and confirmed 349 beaver territories, with 56 more classed as possible, in a report NatureScot published on 16 November. The fieldwork, run with the Beaver Trust and the University of Exeter, sits on a line of earlier counts — 39 territories in 2012, 114 in 2017-18, 251 in 2020-21 — and records animals newly settled in Fife, along the Firth of Tay and in the South Esk and Clyde catchments. The team gives no single population figure on purpose, because family groups vary in size from one territory to the next.

Small Wonders · Aberdeen, Scotland

Bumblebees turn out to have a preferred amount of salt in their nectar.

Tegan Gaetano and Lesley Lancaster at the University of Aberdeen offered buff-tailed bumblebees artificial nectar dosed with calcium, potassium, magnesium or sodium at a range of strengths, and the bees turned away from the strongest version of all four. What they settled on was a middling level of sodium, the first demonstration that a bumblebee has a preferred amount of salt rather than simply putting up with whatever it finds. The level they chose sits above the sodium most flowers actually supply, which the pair take in Royal Society Open Science as a sign that nectar alone may not give a bee everything it is after.

Everyday Joy · Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA

Five hundred and seventy people dressed as Rocky Balboa and ran the museum steps.

They assembled at the foot of the Philadelphia Museum of Art on Friday 13 November in the required kit — grey hooded sweatshirt, grey sweatpants, black beanie, black Chuck Taylors — and took a Guinness World Records title for the largest gathering of people dressed as the character, more than doubling the 250 the record asked for. Once the adjudicator had counted everyone, the whole field ran up the steps together for a group photograph. The attempt belongs to Rocky Season, the city's run of events marking fifty years since the first film, and drew people from as far as Brazil and Madrid.

Science & Medicine · A multicentre trial

Two arthritis drugs were tested against each other rather than against a placebo.

In a phase 3b trial of 553 adults with active psoriatic arthritis, 55% of those given bimekizumab reached at least a 50% improvement by six months against 44% of those given risankizumab, with the gap at four months running 50% to 38%. Direct comparisons are rare here, because new biologics are normally measured only against a dummy injection, which leaves rheumatologists ranking drugs by inference. Serious side effects stayed under 2% on bimekizumab and near 3% on risankizumab, and candida infections were slightly more common on the first — the sort of detail only a head-to-head can supply.

Nature · Seal Beach and Huntington Beach, California, USA

The first sea turtles known to hatch on the US West Coast have reached the water.

Eggs rescued from two beach nests ahead of a storm hatched in incubators, and staff carried the hatchlings down to their own stretch of sand before sunrise.

Small Wonders · Ramat Gan, Israel

Two gorillas worked out that one of them could be a ladder.

Security footage caught Lisha bracing on all fours three separate times so her half-sister Amelia could climb up and reach a branch 4.5 metres above the ground.

Global Progress · Eritrea

Eritrea is the 34th country to be rid of trachoma as a public health problem.

Two decades of surgery, antibiotics, face washing and better water carried the country past the threshold, and surveillance will carry on.

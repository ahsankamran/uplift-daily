Nature · Kozhikode, Kerala, India

More than 27,000 orchids were lifted off felled trees and tied onto new ones.

Researchers in Kerala gathered 27,324 orchids from trees that had been cut, or were marked for cutting, to make way for road building in the Western Ghats, then fastened them to trees at a beach, an urban wetland park and a botanical garden. After one to two years about 71% were still alive, among them more than half of the 6,126 plants belonging to 12 species found nowhere else, and many of those flowered and set seed capsules despite having come from cooler hill forests. Ten species at Sarovaram Bio Park now carry QR codes that visitors have scanned 126,000 times in two years, though co-author Viswambharan Sarasan of Kew says moving plants should stay a last resort for when the tree itself cannot be kept.

Small Wonders · Caribbean reefs

A tiny reef fish shares almost everything with the sponge it lives in.

Tusked gobies, among the smallest fish on Caribbean reefs, live in groups inside living sponges, and larger sponges hold larger groups arranged in a hierarchy by size. Theresa Rueger's team at Newcastle University, with colleagues in Belize and the United States, sequenced the fish and found no sign that sponge-mates are related — they are neighbours rather than family — and more than 500 kinds of bacteria shared between the gobies' guts and the sponges around them. The two papers, in The American Naturalist and Molecular Ecology, argue that where one of these fish settles goes on to shape nearly everything about it.

Global Progress · Pakistan

Pakistan gave 24 million children polio drops in six days.

A door-to-door round that opened on November 2 and closed on November 8 reached about 24 million children under five across 97 districts. More than 200,000 frontline workers carried it, covering 9.1 million children in Punjab, 7 million in Sindh, 4.7 million in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and 2.2 million in Balochistan, with smaller rounds in Islamabad, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. The National Emergency Operations Centre has recorded nine cases in Pakistan this year, one of the last countries where the wild virus still spreads.

Quiet Kindness · Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, USA

Gettysburg handed out its first awards for being a good neighbour.

Mayor Alison Lintal presented the borough's first Kindness Commendation Awards at the council meeting on November 9, picked from nearly 20 nominations sent in by residents. Olivia McClure, a teenager, was named for walking through woods and over railroad tracks in September to surprise a neighbour with brownies; Michael Casamassina for helping neighbours up after falls, clearing a path to their cars and seeing to their bins; and Dr Smitha Nair for starting free twice-monthly ice-cream gatherings, now attended by more than 30 people, after noticing how many of her patients were lonely. Lintal means to give the awards every year, and has asked her interns to write up the nominations that did not win.

Everyday Joy · Jászberény, Hungary

The Rubik's Cube is being made in Hungary again.

A factory in Jászberény opened this week with an automated line of robotic arms that assemble and test the puzzle, and is expected to turn out up to a million cubes a year. Ernő Rubik, now 82, unveiled it; he built the first cube in 1974 from 27 wooden blocks while teaching design in Budapest, and it went on sale at home in 1977 as the Magic Cube. Around 200 million sold in the boom between 1980 and 1983, and the puzzle is now made again in the country it came from.

Science & Medicine · São Paulo, Brazil

Pushing blood pressure lower after a stroke prevents more of them.

Among 4,368 adults who had already had an ischaemic stroke or a transient attack, treating to a systolic target below 120 mmHg left 10.9% with a further cardiovascular event over a median of 3.9 years, against 13.5% of those held to the usual target below 140. Gisele Sampaio Silva of Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein in São Paulo led the trial across 30 sites, and reports in the New England Journal of Medicine that average pressure settled at 122 mmHg in the intensive group against 135 in the standard one. Kidney function declined more often with the lower target, so the authors ask that anyone treated this way be watched for it.

Small Wonders · University of São Paulo, Brazil

Cats save their meowing for an audience.

Thirty-eight cats in São Paulo meowed about 16 times more often at a box of food they could see but not open when their owner was in the room.

Nature · Al Qurm Wa Lehhfaiiah Protected Area, Sharjah, United Arab Emirates

Young green turtles have settled into a lagoon dug for a waterfront that was never built.

Nine tracked juveniles spent more than 80% of their time inside the Sharjah wetland, favouring a basin excavated in 2003 for a development that was later scaled back.

Science & Medicine · QIMR Berghofer Medical Research Institute, Brisbane, Australia

A genetic score picks out who will lose sight to glaucoma, decades ahead.

Built from more than six million genomes, the score marks a tenth of people who develop open-angle glaucoma roughly 25 years earlier than the lowest-risk tenth.

Everyday Joy · Zócalo, Mexico City, Mexico

Mexico City's Day of the Dead skeleton is the tallest anyone has built.

A Catrina 33.4 metres high — about 110 feet, or eleven storeys — went up in the Zócalo for Día de Muertos, and Guinness World Records certified her as the tallest papier-mâché sculpture ever made. The Xibalbá art workshop and its director, Carlos Camilo Báez, built her, and she stands at the centre of a mega-ofrenda laid with some 70,000 marigolds and 66 axolotl sculptures. She passes the 28.15-metre Catrina that Puerto Vallarta raised in 2023, and she is the fifth Guinness record Mexico City has collected this year.

Nature · Islands off western Britain

Shearwaters flying home through the night drift off course, the way we get jet lag.

Twenty years of GPS tracks from 1,045 breeding Manx shearwaters — 3,688 trips in all, 1,837 of them homeward across the Irish Sea and the northeast Atlantic — show the birds veering clockwise by as much as 15 degrees when they fly in the dark. Patrick Lewin, an ecologist at the University of Oxford, and his colleagues report in Science that the drift looks like an internal clock briefly out of step with the sun, the same mismatch that leaves a long-haul passenger groggy. It costs the birds very little: on a homeward leg of roughly 1,000 kilometres, a seven-degree error adds about five.

Quiet Kindness · Poovathumkadavu, Thrissur, Kerala, India

A Kerala village has turned its bus shelter into a small library.

Residents of Poovathumkadavu, in Vellangallur panchayat in Thrissur district, raised ₹2 lakh and rebuilt their roadside bus stop as a tiled padippura-style shelter stocked with books, the day's newspapers and drinking water. The Sree Bhuvaneswari Club organised the collection, taking contributions from villagers and from Keralites working abroad; the shelter runs on solar lighting, carries an FM radio service and has a camera watching over it. Panchayat president Sulekha Abdullakutty opened it, and anyone waiting for a bus can now read while they wait.

Small Wonders · Eastern Sierra Nevada, California, USA

Ground squirrels with more female relatives nearby come through hibernation better.

Twenty-five years of marking and recapturing Belding's ground squirrels at a field site in California's eastern Sierra show that adult females survived the winter more often when more of their adult female kin were living close by. Jill Mateo and colleagues at the University of Chicago, writing in PLOS One on November 4, found no such effect for males or for young females, and recorded the heaviest losses among juvenile males. Shorter snow seasons brought the squirrels up out of their burrows earlier, which went with more pups surviving and more young per mother.

Science & Medicine · Denmark

Two cholesterol tablets together shrank artery plaque by a fifth.

A Danish trial of 104 adults who had stable chest pain and narrowed coronary arteries, and had never taken a statin, found that 18 months of rosuvastatin plus ezetimibe cut total plaque volume by 21.1 percent, against 13.6 percent on atorvastatin alone. Martin Bødtker Mortensen and colleagues scanned everyone by coronary CT at the start, at nine months and at the end; LDL cholesterol fell 65 percent on the two drugs and 38 percent on the one, and the results are in JAMA Cardiology. The soft, fat-rich plaque most likely to rupture receded in 51 of the 52 people taking the pair, though the trial was far too small to say whether that prevents heart attacks.

Global Progress · Mali

A Malian enterprise has put 150,000 trees into worn-out ground.

Herou Alliance, the agroforestry enterprise Rokiatu Traoré runs in rural Mali, has planted about 150,000 moringa and baobab trees on degraded land and trained more than 2,000 smallholder farmers to look after them. At the UN Sustainable Development Goals Action Awards in Rome on October 29 she was named a Changemaker; since 2021 the enterprise has also been building commercial moringa supply chains, so the trees pay the farmers as well as holding the soil. Land degradation covered 11.5 million hectares of Mali in 2019 and touched more than 6.5 million people; Traoré's stated aim is 10 million hectares restored by 2040.

Small Wonders · University of Bonn, Germany

Jumping spiders have two kinds of sleep, and in one of them their eyes move.

Filmed through the night, Portia fimbriata cycled between a still state and one in which its legs curled, its body twitched and its eyes moved.

Nature · Ile-Balkhash State Nature Reserve, Kazakhstan

A second tiger is loose in the Ili delta, where none has lived for 70 years.

Kazakhstan released a collared male Amur tiger into the Ile-Balkhash reserve on October 31, three months after a female walked out of her enclosure.

Global Progress · Pantanal Matogrossense National Park, Brazil

Brazil's Pantanal national park is about a third larger than it was.

Some 48,000 hectares of wetland and riverside forest were added along the corridors animals already use, taking the park to 183,095 hectares.

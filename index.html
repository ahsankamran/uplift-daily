Everyday Joy · Hull, England

A man who started walking in 1998 has reached his mother's front door.

Karl Bushby, 57, arrived in Hull on Monday, 28 years after setting out from Punta Arenas at the southern tip of Chile, having covered about 50,000 kilometres — roughly 31,000 miles — without once using motorised transport. He walked north through the Americas and the Darién Gap, crossed the frozen Bering Strait into Russia, carried on through Mongolia and China, swam the Caspian, and finished by swimming the English Channel in October and walking over the Humber Bridge on Sunday. His mother, Angela, was waiting under a banner reading Welcome Home Son; he said that it took a world to get me here, and gave the credit to the strangers who fed and sheltered him along the way.

Small Wonders · 22 countries across Europe

Camera traps in 22 countries have made one shared album of Europe's wildlife.

Snapshot Europe has released about 100,000 observations of mammals and birds, gathered at more than 1,000 sites a year by over 160 camera-trap specialists from 118 universities, field stations, parks, museums and conservation groups. Fabiola Iannarilli of the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior and Francesca Cagnacci of Euromammals coordinated the survey, published on November 6 in Ecology; red deer were photographed most often, then roe deer, wild boar, red fox and European rabbit. Rabbits in Belgium turned up as often as ten times a day, the Casentino forests in Italy held the most species at fifteen, and every image is now public.

Quiet Kindness · Castlemilk, Glasgow, Scotland

Castlemilk has a shop selling fresh food again, and it is not there to make money.

Ardenglen Housing Association and Good Food Scotland have opened a not-for-profit community shop in the Glasgow neighbourhood, selling fresh produce and household essentials at up to 30 percent below the nearest supermarket. It replaces a pantry where residents paid a set price for about twelve items from a short list; Ardenglen's Suzanne Casey said that model was not sustainable in the long term, and residents helped shape what took its place. It is the tenth such shop Good Food Scotland runs in the city, between them serving more than 4,000 members, and its funding is secured only to the end of March.

Nature · Tawi-Tawi, Philippines

Horseshoe crabs have been confirmed in a second Philippine province.

Researchers interviewed 142 residents across four municipalities of Tawi-Tawi and logged sightings in Bongao, Simunul, Sitangkai and Panglima Sugala, the first record of the animals anywhere in the Philippines outside Palawan. Ivane Pedrosa-Gerasmio of Mindanao State University–Iligan Institute of Technology first heard of them at a Bongao market in December 2023; residents call them bangkas, after the shape of a boat, and the two kinds described resemble the endangered tri-spine and the mangrove horseshoe crab. More than half of those interviewed believe numbers are falling, and her student Quesie Tolero Omnos argues the new record should guide what local councils choose to protect.

Science & Medicine · Flint, Michigan, USA

Flint halved its newborn deaths after it began paying every pregnant resident.

Rx Kids gives every pregnant woman in the city $1,500 and then $500 a month through a baby's first year; deaths in the first 28 days fell from 10.2 to 5.1 per 1,000 live births, and deaths in the first week from 7.9 to 2.0. Mona Hanna and colleagues at Michigan State University compared state birth and death records from 2021 to 2025 and published the figures in JAMA Pediatrics; the rest of Michigan moved from 4.0 to 3.9 over the same years. Flint's gap with the rest of the state is no longer statistically significant, though the study watched what happened rather than testing it, and cannot show the money is the reason.

Global Progress · Geneva, Switzerland

Three-quarters of the world is now online.

The International Telecommunication Union counts 6.3 billion people using the internet in 2026, 76 percent of humanity, up from a revised 6.1 billion the year before. Its Facts and Figures report, out on November 3, also records that landlocked developing countries and small island developing states met the affordability benchmark for mobile broadband — 2 percent of income per head — for the first time. Two billion people are still offline, and the gap remains wide: 93 percent of people in high-income countries use the internet, against 32 percent in low-income ones.

Everyday Joy · Zócalo, Mexico City, Mexico

Mexico City's Day of the Dead skeleton is the tallest anyone has built.

A Catrina 33.4 metres high went up in the Zócalo for Día de Muertos, and Guinness World Records certified her as the tallest papier-mâché sculpture ever made.

Nature · Islands off western Britain

Shearwaters flying home through the night drift off course, the way we get jet lag.

Twenty years of GPS tracks from 1,045 Manx shearwaters show the birds veering clockwise by as much as 15 degrees when they fly in the dark.

Quiet Kindness · Poovathumkadavu, Thrissur, Kerala, India

A Kerala village has turned its bus shelter into a small library.

Residents raised ₹2 lakh and rebuilt their roadside bus stop as a tiled shelter stocked with books, the day's newspapers and drinking water.

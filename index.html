Small Wonders · Kitimat Fjord System, British Columbia, Canada

A whale's whole genome has been read from its breath.

Researchers taped sterile petri dishes to a small off-the-shelf drone and flew it through the exhaled breath of humpback whales in the Kitimat Fjord System, gathering 58 samples from 26 individual animals. About 84% of the DNA in each plume came from the whale itself, and the genomes matched those read from dart biopsies of the same animals, with no close boat approach needed — more than a third of the samples were collected by flying the drone from shore. The work, led by the University of St Andrews with the University of Copenhagen, the North Coast Cetacean Society and Fisheries and Oceans Canada, will now be used to map kinship and population structure among the whales that feed there.

Nature · Lord Howe Island, Australia

A seaweed no one had seen full-grown since 1935 is standing in forests off Lord Howe Island.

Baited underwater cameras, dropped to survey fish, came up tangled with Sargassum howeanum — known only from small juvenile scraps for 90 years — growing up to 72cm tall between 20 and 38 metres down, 600km off eastern Australia. Matthew Rees of the University of Wollongong and Nathan Knott of UNSW compared four survey years, 2009, 2013, 2017 and 2022, and found the area under Sargassum had grown 22-fold. Water at those depths ran cooler than the surface in the years before 2022, which suggests the deep shelf may be somewhere seaweed can go as the sea warms.

Quiet Kindness · Shaunavon, Saskatchewan, Canada

A Saskatchewan town drives its older neighbours to their appointments.

Age-Friendly Shaunavon, a volunteer committee in a town 286km southwest of Regina, runs a van that has made 30 trips as far as Regina and Medicine Hat since it took to the road in late June. The same committee puts on estate-planning courses, pairs older residents with schoolchildren as reading buddies, and takes the bins out for people who cannot manage them. Twelve Saskatchewan communities have active committees, and the provincial seniors' body is running workshops around the province to bring that to 16.

Everyday Joy · Frankfurt, Germany

Forty years of looking has put the Parthenon's lost pediment back in order.

Vinzenz Brinkmann started trying to work out how the east pediment's 39 figures once stood in 1986, and found his answer in nine Roman sarcophagus lids whose carvers had copied pieces of the scene. Computer comparison matched the surviving marble to the reliefs, and 3D-printed copies of each fragment were shuffled about until the arrangement held, putting Castor and Pollux on the pediment for the first time. Roughly four fifths of the original is still missing; the reconstruction went on show at the Liebieghaus on 18 November and stays up until 25 July.

Science & Medicine · An international trial

Six in ten pancreatic cancer patients responded to a new drug added to standard chemotherapy.

In the phase 1/2 RMC-GI-102 trial, 61 people with untreated metastatic pancreatic cancer took daraxonrasib alongside gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel, and 60.7% had a confirmed response, 8.2% of them with no tumour left to find. Median progression-free survival was 12.8 months and 90.2% had their disease at least held steady, at a median follow-up of 18.3 months. This is an early-phase study in a small group, presented at the EORTC-NCI-AACR symposium in Barcelona on 20 November, and a randomised trial will be needed to settle it.

Global Progress · Sierra Leone

Sierra Leone now gives every newborn a hepatitis B shot on its first day.

The birth dose joined the routine immunisation schedule on 20 November, free at government health facilities in all 16 districts and offered to babies born at home as readily as to those born in hospital. About 13.8% of Sierra Leoneans, roughly 1.1 million people, carry hepatitis B, and up to 90% of babies who catch it in their first year keep it for life; the single injection works best inside 24 hours of birth. The country records around 400,000 births a year and the programme is aiming at 360,000 of them, with Gavi putting up more than $240,000 for vaccine and the government about $60,000.

Nature · Saleh Bay, Sumbawa, Indonesia

More than half of a whale shark bay in Sumbawa is being set aside for the sharks.

West Nusa Tenggara has designated 73,165 hectares of Saleh Bay, covering about 90% of the places the sharks gather.

Quiet Kindness · Luma, Rukum East, Nepal

A 15-year-old paid for her village's new footbridge and helped build it.

Sadiksha Oli put Rs60,000 of her own money into timber and nails, and the four-metre crossing was finished in three days.

Three cheeses tied for best in the world, and the steadiest scores won.

A 24-month Comté from Fort des Rousses finished level on 81 points and took the title on the closeness of its marks.

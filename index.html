Nature · Pacific, Caribbean, Indo-Pacific

Researchers have identified 165,000 square kilometres of coral reef that may withstand climate change through 2050 — a new map of what the ocean still has to offer, and what to protect most urgently.

A study combining more than 45,000 field observations from 1960 to 2025 with AI climate modelling identified reef systems distributed across 71 countries likely to remain functional through mid-century even under warming scenarios; the resilient areas are concentrated most densely in the Bahamas, Cuba, Australia, Indonesia, and the Philippines, and include a significant share of the world's reef fish diversity. Researchers from the Wildlife Conservation Society and Macquarie University presented the findings at the Our Ocean Conference in Mombasa and published them in June 2026, noting that more than half of the resilient reefs lie within the territorial waters of their host nations — meaning local governance is the decisive variable. The study offers conservation planners a ranked list of sites where funding and attention would return the greatest results, shifting the conversation from mourning what has been lost to precision about what can still be saved.

Small Wonders · North Island, New Zealand

A New Zealand cave has yielded the bones of a flying ancestor of the kākāpō, sealed in volcanic ash for a million years and never before seen in the fossil record.

Working with a deposit locked between two ash layers in a North Island cave, researchers from Flinders University identified the remains of a parrot ancestral to the kākāpō that was still capable of flight — revealing that the world's heaviest, most famously earthbound parrot lost its wings relatively recently in evolutionary time. Alongside the proto-kākāpō, the cave held 12 bird species and four frog species not previously documented in New Zealand's fossil record, many of them victims of volcanic upheaval that predates human arrival by a million years. The discovery adds at least a third more lost species to the pre-human picture and suggests the islands were even more extravagantly birdful than their already remarkable modern fauna implies.

Global Progress · South Africa

South Africa has begun rolling out lenacapavir — a twice-yearly HIV prevention injection — across 360 public health facilities in 24 high-burden districts, the world's largest deployment of this technology so far.

The rollout, active across six provinces as of June 2026, targets populations for whom daily pills have been hardest to sustain — including adolescent girls, young women, and people with irregular routines. South Africa carries the world's largest HIV burden, with 8.15 million people living with the virus; lenacapavir's twice-yearly dosing addresses one of the most persistent barriers to prevention. The programme aims to reach one million people by the end of 2027 and three million within three years, in what health officials are calling the most consequential shift in HIV prevention since the introduction of daily pre-exposure prophylaxis.

Everyday Joy · Mexico City, Mexico

One of only six women's blind football clubs in Mexico trains three times a week in Mexico City with blackout covers and a rattling ball — and has set its sights on Copa América in September.

Chilangas FC, founded in 2022 by coach Wendy del Río, gives visually impaired women something rare: a competitive team and a community in a country where many have been told their lives should stay small. Player Pau, 31, commutes two hours across the city to each session and says the sport changed how she sees herself. In September, the club hopes to represent Mexico at Copa América in São Paulo — making Mexico the fourth nation to field a women's blind football national team.

Science & Medicine · Milan, Italy

Older adults who took a placebo pill — and knew it was a placebo — still improved their memory scores by up to 21 per cent in a three-week Milan trial.

In a randomised controlled trial at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, 90 healthy adults over 60 were assigned to no treatment, a standard placebo, or an open-label placebo they were told contained no active ingredient; both placebo groups outperformed the control on cognitive tests (6.9 to 21.5 per cent improvement), physical performance, and self-reported stress. The findings, published in the International Journal of Clinical and Health Psychology, suggest the placebo mechanism operates below conscious belief — opening a path to drug-free, fully ethical cognitive support for older adults that requires no pretence at all.

Quiet Kindness · Eagan, Minnesota, United States

A disability housing home in Eagan, Minnesota launched a refurbished Little Free Library and met neighbours who had lived on the same street for 26 years without ever introducing themselves.

On 16 June, residents of Living Well Disability Services' Highview home hosted the opening of a library stocked with books featuring disabled characters; the afternoon brought yard games and the kind of unhurried conversation that neighbours who have lived within metres of each other for decades rarely find occasion for. One visitor made a spontaneous cash donation on the spot to expand the collection before the day was over.

Nature · Senegal, Madagascar, Chile, Canada

Six ocean sanctuaries across four continents have earned Blue Park status for the highest standard of marine protection — four of the awards going to sites in Africa.

At the Our Ocean conference in Mombasa on 16 June, six protected areas earned 2026 Blue Park status, adding 455,000 square miles of well-managed ocean to the network — all six recognised for governance models that centre Indigenous peoples and local fishing communities.

Science & Medicine · England

In the five years to 2024, no woman in her early twenties has died from cervical cancer in England — the first time this has been true in recorded history.

A Lancet study found zero cervical cancer deaths among women aged 20–24 in the period 2020–2024 — the cohort first offered the HPV vaccine at ages 12–13 in 2008 — and estimates roughly 200 deaths prevented across all vaccinated cohorts.

Global Progress · Italy

For the first time since the Middle Ages, Italy's forests cover more land than its farms — a reversal driven by young people returning to mountain villages.

Woodland now stretches across 60,000 square miles of the peninsula; since 2021, 932 Italian municipalities have recorded positive net migration, suggesting the land recovery and the human recovery are now reinforcing each other.
